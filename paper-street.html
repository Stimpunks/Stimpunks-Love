The street, walked as paper

Paper Street

Every shopfront on the street cut out of card and stood up along a road, the way the wings of a toy theatre stand, and you walking down it.

A paper street is a road that is drawn on a map and was never built on the ground. This is the other way round: a street that is really here, walked as paper. Every door on it is the front page’s own, read when you open this page, so it is the street as it is today, and pressing a door takes you into its room.

The street could not be read just now, so there is nothing to stand up. The street itself is here.

How to walk it

  • Arrow keys, or W, A, S and D, walk you ahead, back and across the road while the street has the keyboard. The buttons under the street do the same, by pressing or by holding.
  • Q and E turn you left and right, R and F look up and down, and X faces ahead again; so do the buttons beside the walking ones. Dragging across the street looks round too; on a touch screen a sideways drag turns and the page still scrolls under your thumb. The street stays where it is as you turn, and standing at a door turns you to face it.
  • Tab goes from door to door down the street, and stands you in front of each one. Enter goes in.
  • Steps or gliding. In steps, every press jumps you one stop, or turns you a twelfth of the way round, with nothing moving in between. Gliding walks you smoothly for as long as you hold a key or a button. Gentle starts in steps and Regular and MAX start gliding; the switch changes it for this visit, and nothing remembers which you chose.
  • Nothing moves by itself. The street stands still until you walk, at every setting of the dial, and nothing on it flashes.

Walking into a room

A door marked You can walk in opens its room here, in its own world, drawn in that room’s own colours, never the street’s. Somewhere inside is the room’s own page, on a screen or a wall, and standing in front of it you read it and press things on it as the page itself; and some of what is on the page stands in the room as itself, the Latibulum’s wireless and television among them, to walk up to and switch on. Nothing in them reaches anywhere until you press; its links that go to another page take the whole window with them. Back out onto the street, or your browser’s Back, takes you out to the door you came in by. Where you are goes after the # in the address, which never reaches a server, so a room you are standing in can be sent to somebody. Every other door takes you to its page, as it does on the street.

Rooms you can walk into are built one at a time, each in its own world, never from one pattern for all of them.

Cel Street

This street is also built a second way, in daylight: Cel Street, in WebGL and drawn like an animation cel, with the same doors as shops on the ground floors of apartment blocks, solar panels on the roofs and the wires tangled overhead. It stands by the front door beside this one, and Paper Street stays as it is.

Who walks with you

If a familiar out of Chimera Obscura is following you round the street, it walks with you here too, down at your feet. Signed on to the CB, Bring my avatar puts the avatar you wear on the CB in front of you, and you walk as it; until you do, or if you have none, you walk with no body at all, because nobody here is handed one.

What it is made of

The light on Paper Street is the street’s own. Nothing lights it from above: every front throws a pool of its own colour on the road in front of it, so the road is lit in patches all the way down, the colours of whatever stands beside it. The backcloth is a printed night, and the stars on it are paint. Under every front is the fold that keeps it standing.

What it keeps

Nothing. Opening the page reads the front page, once. Bring my avatar asks for your avatars, once, when you press it, with the pass the radio already keeps. Your familiar is read from your own browser, where it already is. Where you walked is not written down anywhere, and nothing here is counted: not doors passed, not rooms gone into, not how far you walked.
