The Fridge of Sighs

What we say when we say it together

This is the door of the fridge in the kitchen, and it is covered in word magnets. Anybody signed on to the CB can put up one word, and then somebody else puts up the next, because nobody puts up two in a row. A word ending in a full stop, a question mark, an exclamation mark or an ellipsis finishes the sentence, and it stays on the door with the others. None of the words say whose they were. It is after the sentence builder in our Discord’s Collaborative Nonsense channels.

        How the door works

        • One word at a time, and never two in a row. Once you have put up a word, the next one is somebody else’s. After theirs you can go again. A word is letters and numbers, in any language, with an apostrophe or a hyphen inside it if it needs one, a quotation mark or bracket before it, and a mark or two after it, up to twenty-four characters. No spaces, because that would be two magnets.
        • A full stop, a question mark, an exclamation mark or an ellipsis finishes the sentence, and so does the fortieth word, because the door is only so wide. A finished sentence stays on the door, and the door holds the newest forty; older ones go into the drawer, by the month they were finished. They are kept for good.
        • No word says who put it up. The only thing the fridge keeps about anybody is a scrambled mark saying the last word was theirs, so that they cannot put up the next one, and it is replaced by the very next word. It is never shown, it cannot be turned back into a handle without the community password, and a finished sentence holds none of it. A sentence is everybody’s.
        • Nothing is counted. Not the words, not the sentences, and not how many anybody put up, because nobody knows which were whose.
        • The base station can take a word or a sentence down, and so can we if you ask: what the fridge keeps. Search engines are asked not to keep the door.
        • Nothing on the door moves, at any setting. The magnets sit where they were put, a little uneven, the way a hand leaves them.

        The name, and the magnets

        The name is a joke on the Bridge of Sighs, in Venice, and on the noise a fridge makes when its door seals. Word magnets on a fridge were Dave Kapell’s idea: he invented them in 1993, while he was a songwriter with writer’s block, and the kits are sold as Magnetic Poetry. None of his kits’ words are here; every word on this door came from somebody in our community. From Wikipedia’s article on Magnetic Poetry, read on 30 September 2026.

        The game is our community’s own, from the Collaborative Nonsense channels in our Discord, where a sentence grows one message at a time. The room is set in Noticia Text, by JM Solé, and credited in the liner notes.
