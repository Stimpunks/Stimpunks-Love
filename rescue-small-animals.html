Rescuing or adopting takes a CB handle and the community password: sign on at the Community Center, then come back. Looking takes nothing.

Under the floorboards of the shop it is dark, and the only light is the day coming down through the cracks between the boards, lying on the dust in long pale strips. Every so often somebody small turns up down there who needs bringing in: a rabbit, a rat, a mouse, a hamster, a gerbil, a guinea pig, a chinchilla, a ferret or a hedgehog, under the stairs, in a boot by the back door, in the sock drawer. You will see them where they stand in a strip of light. Signed on to the CB, the first to press rescues them and can give them a name, and anybody can adopt one from the hutches, who then lives in Profile on their CB radio. There is a cat shelter and a dog shelter too.

        How rescuing and adopting work

        • Small animals turn up at random, one at a time, and each waits however long it takes somebody to come for them. Not even the hutches know who is next.
        • The first person signed on to the CB to press brings them in. Anybody a moment later is told they are already safe. Looking takes nothing.
        • They stay in the hutches until somebody adopts them. When the hutches are full, the next one waits outside until somebody adopts.
        • Adopting is first to press too, and each is adopted once. They go into Profile on your CB radio, which follows you to any device you sign on with, and onto the list of everybody adopted, for good. That list never says who adopted anybody.
        • Once they are yours, you can rename them, from Profile, as often as you like. The list of everybody adopted shows the new name and the one they came in under, so whoever rescued them keeps their part of it.
        • You can show them off. Sticker on the radio sends a drawing of one of your pets to the channel. With a claimed username, Show my pets on the CB in Profile lets anybody on the CB press your handle and see them. It is off until you switch it on.
        • Your pets are kept under your handle, scrambled so the store does not list handles. Until you claim your username from Profile on your radio, a handle is not an account, and anybody who signs on with the same handle sees the same pets; once it is claimed, only your password reaches them. Forget me in Profile deletes them; they stay on the list of everybody adopted, with nothing connecting them to you. What the shelters keep.
        • No animal is rarer than another. Every species is as likely as every other, each comes in three coats it really comes in, and every marking and every place is as likely as any other, so nobody is a prize and nobody a disappointment. Some have three legs or one eye, and they are rabbits, rats and hedgehogs all the same.
        • Nothing says who rescued whom, anywhere on the street, and nobody is counted: no totals, no board of rescuers, no streaks. The ones you rescued are kept in your own browser only.
        • The base station can take a name off an animal, wherever they are, if the name should not be on show.
        • Nothing moves, at any setting. Everybody is drawn standing still in their strip of light.

        The game

        This is Rescue A Cat’s game, which is our community’s own from the Collaborative Nonsense channels in our Discord, with small animals in it; adopting is Ryan’s, for every shelter. The floor, the cracks and every animal are drawn for this room. It is set in Carter One, by Vernon Adams, and Balsamiq Sans, by Michael Angeles, and credited in the liner notes.
