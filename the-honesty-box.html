A farm stand on the street

The Honesty Box

What the land past the campgrounds grows, on a stand at the kerb: prices on the board, money in the box, and nobody watching.

The stand is a counter with a little roof, at the kerb, and it lights itself: a panel on the roof charges a battery under the counter all day, and at night a white lamp under the eave throws a cone of light over the shelves, so everything in it keeps its own colour while the street around it is dark. It runs the fridge and the freezer too. Everything on it comes from Nature’s Contribution, most of it from All Together Now Farms, and the farm trusts you with it.

At the kerb

In words: a dark street with a pale kerb. A small wooden stand with a pitched roof and a solar panel on the roof, and under its eave a white lamp throwing a cone of light down over two shelves: corn, squash, red and green peppers, cherry tomatoes, bunches of herbs and orange marigolds on the top shelf, and a box of eggs, skeins of wool, bags of nuts, a coil of rope, folded cloth and seed packets on the lower one. Under the counter, a fridge, a freezer and a battery. At the end of the counter, a wooden box with a slot and a padlock. A painted board leans against the post beside it, with lines of red writing on it.

How the box works

  • Take what you want, and put the money in the slot. Change is in the tin beside it.
  • Short tonight? Take it anyway, write it in the book on the counter, and settle up another time.
  • Nobody is watching. No camera, no attendant, and nobody counting what went.

What is not in the box

  • Cannabis. The cannabis farm’s flower, rosin and resin go to licensed dispensaries and nowhere else. You have to be 21 to buy it in Colorado, and a box nobody watches cannot ask anybody’s age. The hemp cordage and cloth are fibre, from the stalks, and they are here.
  • No raw milk. Colorado does not allow it to be sold at retail, and the farm would not sell it if it did: the farm’s page says why.
  • Anything from the sanctuary. Its residents are not stock, and nothing leaves it but its manure.
