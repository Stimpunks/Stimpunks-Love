HOW LOUD DO YOU WANT IT?

Starts wherever your device says. Nothing moves, flashes, or plays until you say so.

The street has a name, and it is not ours. Danny the Street is a character in DC Comics’ Doom Patrol, created in 1990 by Grant Morrison and Richard Case: a living street who turns up wherever somebody needs shelter and rearranges what is on them to suit whoever has arrived. Rooms that refuse to match, a field for anybody who would rather not be on a street at all, a shopfront with the keys in the door — that is all Danny’s, and we would like it on the record.       WHAT THIS STREET IS FOR

      Every other site we run is careful on purpose — careful palettes, careful sourcing, careful attribution, careful hedging. Those sites are load-bearing and they stay. This one is the other half of the same lung.

      It has no single design system, deliberately. The street is the system; the rooms refuse to share one. And it has edges — the garden through the gate in the wall, where the sites we publish grow, and one at each end of the road: the campgrounds past the treeline, for anybody who would rather not be on a street at all, with Nature’s Contribution past its wood, where the street’s food and power are grown and the wildlife is left room, and The Outskirts past the last streetlight the other way, where the after-dark keeps premises. At the far end, where the buildings stop, there is open ground: lots side by side behind a fence, the Run, the Truck Stop, the Green and the Pile. And under all of it, by lift or stair, Arlesglad Caverns, for everything that needs things to stay the same. Two things stay careful even here: attribution, because that is a licence and not a house style, and contrast, because clashing is not the same as illegible. Everything else went out the window.
