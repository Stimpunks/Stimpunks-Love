Privacy

What this site knows about you is very little, and this page is all of it. Last updated 2026-10-18. Every change to it is dated in the changelog.

THE SHORT VERSION

  • No cookies. This site sets none, on any page.
  • No advertising, no trackers, nothing sold, and no email. There is nothing to sign up for and nobody to sell anything to. The CB asks for a handle and a shared password. An account is optional: signed on, you can claim your username with a password of your own, and that account is the username, its password and nothing else. More below.
  • A few things are kept in your own browser, each because you asked for it. Only one of them is ever sent anywhere, and only to us, and only by the CB: the radio, the chalkboard, the pebble bowls, the suggestion box, Pando Calrissian’s tree and its fence, the Fridge of Sighs, Count Me In’s stair, the cat and dog shelters and Profile, the forums, and the Slake.
  • The CB is the one thing on this site that sends anything, and only while you have it open. What is said on it is kept for the day, ten messages at most, and then deleted, unless somebody copies it into a forum topic.
  • A room’s call is run by 8x8, and only people signed on to the CB can join one, except the three public calls behind Cavendish Coworking’s meeting doors, which a guest joins under a name they type. While somebody is in a call we keep their name in it, from 8x8, and it goes when they leave. There is also a World call, which anybody signed on can join from any room. The radio’s Who’s online names the World call and every room whose call has somebody in it, and never who or how many. More below.
  • You can choose to be seen. Signed on, Be seen here puts your handle and the room you are in on the radio’s Who’s online list, for anybody signed on to the CB, and shows you and anybody else seen in the same room to each other, with who is in its call. Nobody who has not chosen it is shown, and nothing is counted. In a room that keeps to itself, being seen shows you only to people seen there, and never on Who’s online. More below.
  • Hosting a film is the CB too. If you press Host this film, everybody signed on sees which film you are watching, in which room, and where it has got to, until you stop. More below.
  • The chalkboard on Plural Mural is the CB’s too, and it is public. Only people signed on to the CB can write on it, anybody at all can read it, and every note is rubbed out after seven days.
  • The forums are the CB’s too, and only claimed usernames can read them. Topics in categories, each kept for a week, three months, six months or for good after its last post, with your username on what you write. Nothing about who reads is kept, and a link on a line of its own is previewed by our server reading that page once, when the post is made. Messages copied from the CB last as long as their topic, and whoever sent one can take it out again. More below.
  • The suggestion box in The Sandbox is the CB’s too, and it is public. Signed on, you can drop in a suggestion, a feature request or a bug report, with your handle on it. Anybody can read it, a moderator answers it with their own username on the reply, and it stays until you or a moderator take it down. More below.
  • Pando Calrissian’s tree is the CB’s too. Signed on, you can water it. It keeps how many times it has been watered, for good, and never who watered it. The fence beside it holds notes on the chalkboard’s rules. More below.
  • The Fridge of Sighs is the CB’s too. Signed on, you can put up one word of a sentence. Finished sentences are kept for good and no word says who put it up. More below.
  • Count Me In’s stair is the CB’s too. Signed on, you can take the next step, by number or by the lift. It keeps the step it is on and the highest it has reached, for good, and never who took any step. More below.
  • Rescue A Cat, Rescue A Dog and Rescue Small Animals are the CB’s too. Signed on, you can rescue an animal and name it, and adopt one into Profile on your radio. The shelters and the list of everybody adopted never say who rescued or adopted anybody. Your pets, your avatars, your battle jacket’s patches and, if you ask it to, the jobs you hand in at the Guild are the things the street keeps under a person, filed under a scrambled form of your handle, and Forget me in Profile deletes them. More below.
  • Avatars are the CB’s too. Signed on, you can keep a bodymind you drew at the Bodymind Booth, or an outfit from the Dress-Up Den, as an avatar in Profile, and with a claimed username wear one beside your name on Who’s online. An avatar is a list of choices, never a picture, and Forget me in Profile deletes them. More below.
  • The Slake, out the back of the MUD Room, is the CB too. Signed on, you can choose to be seen out there, on each visit, and only then does it send anything: where you are and what you say. You meet people in the place you are in; nobody gets a list of who is out there, and nothing is counted.
  • Nothing from anybody else loads until you press it. Every video and player on the street waits for a press.
  • Our host keeps ordinary server logs, the way every web server does.

What our host sees

This site is served by Netlify. Like any web server, it receives what every browser sends with every request — your IP address, your browser’s user-agent string, the address you asked for and the time — and it keeps logs of that under Netlify’s own privacy policy, which also says how long. We need that to run and secure a website, which is the legal basis for it (legitimate interests). We have not switched on Netlify’s analytics, we do not add anything to those logs, and we do not use them to find out who anybody is.

Every typeface, script, stylesheet and image on this site is served from this site, so opening a page makes no request to anybody but Netlify.

Searching the street

The search box on the street and on the Map does its searching in your browser. The first time you type in it, it fetches two files from this site, the list of rooms and the words on every page, and nothing else. What you type is never sent anywhere, to us or to anybody, and it is not kept: not in your browser and not in the address. Following a result is an ordinary link on this site.

When you follow a link away

A lot of this site is links to other places. When you follow one, the site you arrive at learns that you came from stimpunks.world — the address of the site, never the page you were on — and from then on you are covered by that site’s policy.

People on these pages

Some pages carry photographs of people, recordings of people’s voices, and the names of the people we credit. Every photograph of somebody, and every recording of somebody’s voice, is published with that person’s consent, recorded in our files with a date — the readings in Hear Queer Here included — and it comes down when the person in it says so: one message, no reason needed. A young person who is doing the work and has said yes themselves is published and credited like anybody else; a parent’s yes does not stand in for theirs, and a child too young to give that consent is not published at all. The whole of that is on the Polaroid Wall’s page. The photographs are excluded from this site’s CC BY-SA licence precisely so that taking one down is something we can actually do.

The CB

The CB is a chat channel, patterned after citizens band radio. You sign on at the Community Center with a handle you make up and a password we share with the community and change from time to time. Until you sign on, the radio is not on any page and nothing is sent.

  • Before you sign on. A folded bar in the corner of each page, with a teleporter and a way to find out how to get on the CB. It is not the radio: it sends nothing to the channel, knows nothing about who is on it, and shows nothing that is said. Opening the teleporter fetches this site’s list of rooms, from this site, and nothing else is fetched. If you leave its panel open, your browser remembers that under love-cb-guest, and folding it away deletes it. You can sign on from it too, if you know the password: that sends exactly what the front desk sends, only when you press Sign on, and then the radio takes its place.
  • What it sends. While the radio is open on your screen and the tab is in front of you, it asks this site for the latest messages every few seconds, and the first time it does, or the first time you open its teleporter, it also fetches the list of rooms on the street, so that a room named with a # can be shown as a link and the teleporter knows where it can take you; that request carries nothing about you. Teleporting to a room is an ordinary link on this site: it sends nothing to the channel and tells nobody where you went. Typing an @ into it asks us who is online, once, and narrows that list in your browser, so what you type after the @ is not sent. More below. Closed, folded down, or in a tab you are not looking at, it sends nothing. When you transmit, your handle and your words come to us.
  • World, or this room. The radio hears one channel at a time. World is the one everybody shares, and listening to it tells us nothing about where you are on the street. This room is the room’s own channel, and to hear it the radio has to tell us which room it is, each time it asks. We do not keep that: nothing records who listened to which room. The radio starts on World, and it remembers which you chose, in your own browser.
  • A place in a film. Somebody can put a place like @34:12 in a message, and on every radio it is a button that moves the film on your page to that place. It never presses play, and nobody else can move your film: only your own press does. Add my spot reads where the film on your page has got to, and the film’s title and the room you are in, and writes them into your message box. The room’s name in a spot links to that room with the spot on the end of the address, after a #, which your browser never sends to any server, ours included; arriving that way gets that film’s play button ready to start at the spot, and nothing plays until you press it. The radio and the film talk to each other inside your browser. That sends nothing, to us or to YouTube, until you transmit it like anything else you type.
  • What we keep. The latest ten messages on each channel, World and every room’s, each with its handle, its words, the time it was sent, whether it came from the base station or from a claimed username, the picture sent with it, if there was one, and its description, and the sticker, if it was one: a copy of what one of your pets looks like and is called, or of one of your avatars, its name and what you wrote about it, or which patch, or which patches were on your battle jacket, taken when you sent it. Reactions are kept on the message they were pressed on: which reaction and the handle that pressed it, shown to everybody on that channel by handle and never as a number, and deleted with the message. An eleventh pushes the oldest off, picture and all. Everything on every channel is deleted every day at midnight, Colorado time. Whoever sent a message can delete it sooner with Take off, picture, reactions and all, and a moderator can delete any message. Taking one off sends your pass, which message and which channel. There is no archive and no backup, and the only copy is one somebody makes into a forum topic.
  • A picture. A screenshot or a photo sent on the channel is redrawn in your own browser before it leaves it, which takes off everything a camera or a phone writes into the file, where it was taken first of all; this site refuses a file that still carries any of it. It is kept only with its message and deleted when the message goes, and one that was never sent is deleted within the hour. It has no public address: only a radio signed on to that channel can fetch it, and a picture sent in one of the Town Hall’s private rooms only by the people that room is for. Download saves the copy your radio already holds onto your own device, which is yours; it asks us for nothing more.
  • A link to another site. An address on another site in a message is a link that opens in a new tab and says which site it goes to. Following it sends that site nothing from here: no referrer, so it is not told which page of ours you came from, and nothing about the channel. What it does once you are there is up to that site.
  • Copy. Copy on a message puts its words on your own clipboard, in your browser. It sends nothing, to us or anybody.
  • Take to the forums. With a claimed username, Take to the forums on a message lets you tick messages on the channel and start a forum topic with them. Our server copies them from the channel itself, words, handles, marks and times exactly as they were sent, and they then last as long as the topic does. Pictures and stickers stay behind. Whoever sent a copied message can take it out of the topic again, and a moderator can. Ask everybody in the conversation first, as you would before a screenshot.
  • What we do not keep. Your IP address is not stored with a message or anywhere else by us, and we do not write handles or messages into any log. We keep no list of who has signed on.
  • Where it is kept. Netlify, our host, stores the channel for us and runs the small program that answers the radio, under Netlify’s privacy policy. Netlify’s ordinary logs record that the radio made a request, the same as for any page. Netlify also limits how often one address can try the password; it does that itself, without handing the address to us.
  • Who can read it. Anybody signed on, which means anybody who has the current password. That makes it a room full of people rather than a private line, so say on it what you would say in a room. An unclaimed handle is not checked, so a handle is not proof of who somebody is, except a moderator’s: our moderators’ handles, and which of them hold the board, director or administrator role, are on a short list kept in the site’s settings on Netlify and not published. A handle on it signs on only with the moderators’ password, and is refused with the community one. The list is read each time a pass is checked and never written to by the radio, and the only thing a radio is told about it is its own roles, so that it can keep out of the Town Hall’s rooms it cannot use.
  • What it does not do. No notifications, no chimes, no badges, no unread count. If the radio is closed, nothing reaches you and nothing is saved up for later.
  • Speaking aloud. If you switch on Speak new messages aloud, the radio reads each new message out, handle first, using a voice built into your own device. It only uses voices your device says are on the device itself, so the words are not sent to a speech service anywhere else; some browsers also offer voices that are, and the radio will not use those. If your device has no voice of its own, nothing is spoken. Each message comes with a burst of radio static before it and the tail of static after, made in the page and not fetched from anywhere; at the Gentle setting the words come without it, and there is no static without a message being spoken. It is off until you switch it on, it only reads what arrives while the radio is open, and folding the radio away stops it mid-word.

We keep a message because you sent it for other people on the channel to read, and keeping it for the day is what that takes. Transmitting is your consent to it, and you can take that back by asking us to delete what you said.

Hosting a film

Anybody signed on to the CB can host the film they are watching, with Host this film on the radio, so that everybody else on the channel can see where it has got to and catch up. It is off until you press it, it is never remembered, and a new page starts with nobody hosting.

  • What it sends, and when. Your pass, your handle, the room you are in, the film’s title, its YouTube id and how long it runs, the place in it and whether it is playing. For a live video it sends that it is live, in place of a length and a place. The id is what lets everybody else’s radio find that film’s own play button on the page, when several films have nearly the same name, and how long it runs is what a play button says when a radio has to put one up (below). It goes when you start hosting, when the film plays, pauses, jumps or changes, and every half minute besides, and only while the radio is open and the tab is in front of you. Put the tab behind another and it stops sending until you come back; fold the radio away or leave the page and you stop hosting.
  • What we keep. One record for each room somebody is hosting, holding exactly that and the time it was last heard. Every update replaces it rather than adding to it, so there is no history of what you watched or where you were in it. It is deleted the moment you stop hosting. If you go quiet instead, by folding the radio or leaving, it is shown to nobody seventy-five seconds after it was last heard, and it is gone for good at the next update from any host or at the hourly sweep, whichever comes first. There is no archive, no backup and no copy.
  • Who can see it. Anybody signed on, on every page: their radio shows who is hosting which film in which room, and where it has got to. Everybody’s radio is sent every host on the street with the channel, so nobody’s radio has to tell us which room they are in to find the host for theirs. A room that keeps to itself is the exception: its host is sent only to radios tuned to that room’s own channel, and to nobody else.
  • What it does not do. It moves nobody’s film. Catch up is a button on each person’s own radio, and only their press moves their film, the same as a place in a message. Follow, beside it, keeps your film in step with the host’s, playing, pausing and moving with it, but only once you have pressed it yourself; it sends nothing, it is never remembered, and your own pause ends it. One person hosts a room at a time, and only the base station can take a room over.
  • A moderator’s own video. A moderator can paste the address of one YouTube video into Screen a video on the radio, and it goes up on the first screen of the page they are on. It plays on their page and nowhere else, and nothing is sent to us until they host it. If they do, pressing Catch up or Follow beside them puts that video’s play button on the first screen of your page, in place of what it had, saying how long it runs and who put it there. Nothing reaches YouTube until you press it, and the screen’s own controls put back what was there. On a set you tune, like the Solarpunk Hermitage’s television, Looming Rocks’ stage or the Rebellion Rave Room’s big screen, it is tuned on the set, which stays off until you press play. Only a moderator’s hosting can do this, and our server, not their radio, says whose hosting is a moderator’s.
  • What we do not keep. The same as the radio: no IP address, nothing in any log, and no list of who watched.

We keep that you are hosting because you pressed Host this film so that other people could see where you are, and keeping it while you are hosting is what that takes. Pressing it again takes that back.

Being seen in a room

Signed on to the CB, the radio has a switch, Be seen here, in every room on the street. It is off until you press it, and once pressed your browser remembers it, so you are seen in each room you open with the radio until you switch it off. Since 2026-10-07, being seen puts you on the radio’s Who’s online list, for anybody signed on to the CB, with the room you are in. Before that it showed you only to people seen in the same room. If you switched it on before then, it now does more than it did when you pressed it, and switching it off takes you off the list at once.

  • What it sends, and when. Only while you are seen, the radio is open, and the tab is in front of you: every few seconds, your pass, which room this page is, and a random number the page makes up for this visit, which nobody else is shown. Switch it off, fold the radio away, put the tab behind another or leave the page, and it tells us you have gone and sends nothing more.
  • Who can see you. Anybody signed on to the CB, seen or not, on Who’s online: your handle, whether it is a claimed username or the base station, and the room you are in. Anybody else seen in the same room sees you there too, and only they are shown who is in that room’s call. Names come in alphabetical order and nobody is shown a number. A room the base keeps is named only to the people it is for, and somebody seen only there is on nobody else’s list. A room that keeps to itself is named to nobody: being seen there shows you only to people seen there. An unclaimed handle is not checked, so a name without CLAIMED beside it is not proof of who somebody is.
  • What we keep. While you are seen, one record: your handle, whether you are the base station, whether your handle is a claimed username, which room, the time of your last check and the visit’s number. Opening another room replaces it rather than adding to it, so which rooms you have been in is not kept. It is shown to nobody thirty seconds after your last check, and deleted when you stop being seen, or within two minutes by anybody else’s check, or by the hourly sweep at the latest.

We keep that you are in a room because you chose to be seen there, and keeping it for as long as you are is what that takes. Pressing Be seen here is your consent to it, and pressing it again takes that back.

Who’s online

Who’s online, beside World and This room on the radio, lists everybody seen in a room and which room, with a link to go there, and, beside a claimed username, the avatar its owner wears on the CB, if they wear one (below), except a room that keeps to itself, which is never on it. While its panel is open, the radio is open and the tab is in front of you, it asks us every few seconds, sending your pass and nothing else. Typing an @ in the teleporter asks the same once and narrows the list in your browser, so the letters you type are not sent. Nothing records who looked, or when.

A room’s call

People signed on to the CB can join a voice and video call for the room they are in, with Join this room’s call on the radio, and in the rooms behind Cavendish Coworking’s doors from the room itself. Three of those calls are open to anybody; see below. The rooms off the Town Hall for our directors, our board and our moderators are the other way round: anybody can walk in, and their channel, their call and any film hosted in them are handed to the base and to nobody else, so nobody else’s radio hears anything about them. The call is run by 8x8’s Jitsi as a Service, not by us, and it opens in a window of its own at the foot of the page.

  • What we send, and to whom. Pressing the button asks this site for a pass into that room’s call, which carries your handle and, if you are the base station, that you are. We sign it and send it back; your browser hands it to 8x8 when the call opens. 8x8 sees your handle, as your name in the call, an id we make from your handle so that 8x8 counts one person once, and which room it is.
  • What 8x8 gets. Whatever any video call gets: your IP address and browser details, and, once you join and switch them on, your camera, your microphone, what you type in the call’s own chat and any screen you share. What 8x8 does with that is covered by 8x8’s privacy notice, not by this page.
  • Camera and microphone. Both start off. Opening the window may make your browser ask about them, so the call’s first screen can show you a preview; nothing goes to the call until you join on that screen. The call is the only thing on this site allowed to ask for a camera, a microphone or a shared screen, and our pages never ask for any of the three themselves.
  • Sharing a YouTube video in a call. Anybody in a call can share a YouTube video for everybody in it to watch together, from the call’s own menu. That is Jitsi’s feature, and when somebody uses it, Jitsi loads the video from YouTube itself (youtube.com, not the no-cookie address our own players use) in the call window of everybody in the call, so YouTube gets what it gets from any of its players, and may set its cookies, under Google’s privacy policy. Jitsi’s analytics, its avatar lookups and its animated-picture search stay switched off.
  • The public calls, and guests. The calls in the rooms behind Cavendish Coworking’s Events, Operations and Editorial doors are open to anybody, not only people signed on to the CB. Somebody who is not signed on types the name they want to be called, and that name comes to us with the room, is signed into their pass and sent back, and goes to 8x8 as their name in the call; we do not keep it. 8x8 also gets an id we make from that name, so that 8x8 counts one person once. The Adventurer’s Guild stamps the jobs you hand in, in your own browser, under love-quests, and works out your badges from them there; a badge is a set of things done that go together, mostly jobs, and it is kept nowhere. The jobs you hand in are the third thing the street can keep under a person, after pets and avatars, and only once you switch on Keep my jobs in my Profile on the Guild’s board, which starts off. That was Ryan’s call, 2026-10-14.

  • What the board sends. Signed on to the CB, opening the Guild’s board sends your pass to us once, to ask whether your Profile keeps your jobs, and, as yes or no, whether it has a pet, for the Pet Parent badge; signed off, it sends nothing. Switching keeping on sends your pass and the jobs this browser has stamped. After that, handing a job in sends your pass and which job. The pass is checked and not kept.
  • What we keep. Which jobs you have handed in, as a sorted list, and nothing about when or in what order: a list of times would be a record of how you spend your days. Only a job that is on the board can be in it. Your badges are not kept: the board and the radio work them out from the jobs, from whether your Profile has a pet, and from the animals your own browser rescued.
  • Which badges other people see. In your profile, only what your jobs and your pets already show: the badges your jobs make if you show your jobs, and Pet Parent if you show your pets. Rescue Ranger is never shown to anybody but you, because nothing on the street knows who rescued which animal.
  • Where they are kept. Under a scrambled form of your handle, the same as your pets and your avatars, at Netlify, beside the channel. An unclaimed handle is not an account: anybody who signs on with the same handle sees the same jobs, and can stop keeping them, until you claim your username.
  • Showing them, if you choose to. With a claimed username, Show my jobs on the CB in Profile lets anybody signed on to the CB who opens your profile see your jobs and the badges they make. It is off until you switch it on. We keep only that it is on, and when you switched it on. Opening somebody’s profile sends your pass and their handle, and we keep neither; the answer never says whether somebody keeps jobs they have not shown.
  • Deleting them. Switching Keep my jobs in my Profile off, which asks once more, deletes them and whether they are shown. So do Forget me and Delete my account in Profile. The stamps in your browser are yours to forget with the board’s own button.
  • What we do not keep. The same as the radio: no IP address, nothing in any log, and no list of who keeps jobs, who has which badge or who looked. Every answer about your jobs asks search engines not to index or archive it.

We keep your jobs because you switched on keeping them and asked the street to remember, and keeping them is what that takes. Switching it on is your consent to it, and switching it off takes it back.

Your battle jacket and its patches

Patches lie about the street, and some are on a counter in a shop, free to take. Each one is drawn in its own room and goes on your battle jacket, in Profile on the radio. The patches are the fourth thing the street keeps under a person, after pets, avatars and jobs, and only when you press a patch’s own button. That was Ryan’s call, 2026-10-14.

  • Picking one up. Only signed on to the CB: a patch’s Pick it up or Take one sends your pass and which patch, and nothing else. Signed off, the button is not there and nothing is sent. The page itself sends nothing until you press.
  • What we keep. Which patches are on your jacket, as a sorted list, and nothing about when you picked any of them up or in what order; the jacket sews them on in the order the street lists them. Only a patch that is on the street can be in it. Nothing counts who else has a patch: there is no list of a patch’s takers, only each person’s own jacket.
  • Where it is kept. Under a scrambled form of your handle, the same as your pets, at Netlify, beside the channel. An unclaimed handle is not an account: anybody who signs on with the same handle has the same jacket until you claim your username.
  • Showing it, if you choose to. With a claimed username, Show my battle jacket on the CB in Profile lets anybody signed on to the CB who opens your profile see your jacket and what is sewn on it. It is off until you switch it on. We keep only that it is on, and when you switched it on. Opening somebody’s profile sends your pass and their handle, and we keep neither.
  • As a sticker. Sticker on the radio can send one of your patches, or your whole battle jacket, to the channel you are tuned to. It is a message like any other, kept and deleted with the channel’s messages, and it carries which patch, or which patches were on your jacket when you sent it, and nothing else. A jacket sent as a sticker does not change when you pick up another patch.
  • Downloading a picture. Download in Profile saves a picture of one of your avatars, pets, badges or patches, or of your battle jacket, onto your own device. It is made in your browser from the drawing already on your screen, with the typefaces its words are set in fetched from this site; nothing is sent to us or anybody, and nothing is kept.
  • Deleting it. Forget me in Profile takes every patch off, with your pets, your avatars and your jobs; Delete my account does the same with your username.
  • What we do not keep. The same as the radio: no IP address, nothing in any log, and no list of who has which patch or who looked. Every answer about your jacket asks search engines not to index or archive it.

We keep your patches because you picked them up and asked the street to remember, and keeping them is what that takes. Pressing a patch’s button is your consent to it, and Forget me takes it back.

Your profile card

Pressing somebody’s handle on the channel, or their name on Who’s online, opens their profile: their card, and beside it their avatars, pets, badges and jobs and battle jacket, each as they have chosen to show it. The card is the fifth thing the street keeps under a person, after pets, avatars, jobs and patches, and only a claimed username has one, because an unclaimed handle is anybody’s who signs on with it. That was Ryan’s call, 2026-10-17, after requests for profiles more like Discord’s.

  • What is on it. Only what you fill in, at Edit my profile card in Profile: a theme, a banner, a decoration round the avatar you wear, a frame round your profile, an effect over it, a face for your name and a nameplate, each chosen from the street’s own lists, which our server checks against those lists and refuses anything else; and, in your own words, your pronouns, a few hundred characters about yourself, up to five links to your other places, and your favourite room on the street with a line on why. A part left empty shows nothing.
  • Who sees it. Whatever you fill in is shown to anybody signed on to the CB who opens your profile, the way the avatar you wear is: there is no switch, because you wrote it to be shown. Your nameplate is shown under your name on Who’s online, while you are seen in a room; to do that, which nameplate you chose is kept where Who’s online can read it, under a scrambled form of your handle, and it is shown only beside a claimed username, a mark we take from your pass. Opening somebody’s profile sends your pass and their handle, and we keep neither.
  • What it never says. When you claimed your username or joined, and nothing on it is counted. A room that keeps to itself cannot be named as a favourite, because naming it would list it.
  • Links. Each must start with https:// or http://, with no name or password in it. It is shown as you typed it, with the site it goes to written after it, the way a link on the channel is, so nobody is sent somewhere it does not say. Following one opens that site, which has its own privacy policy, in a new tab with no referrer.
  • Saving and changing it. Save my card sends your pass and everything on the card, and replaces what was there. Nothing is sent while you choose, and closing the window without saving sends nothing.
  • Deleting it. Clear my card, which asks once more, deletes it and takes your nameplate off Who’s online first. So do Forget me and Delete my account in Profile.
  • A moderator clearing it. If a card breaks our covenant, a moderator can clear it from their desk, which deletes it and takes your nameplate off Who’s online exactly as Clear my card does, and leaves your username, its password, your pets, avatars, jobs and patches as they were, so you can fill in a new card. We keep nothing about it: not who cleared it, not when, and not what the card said. That was Ryan’s call, 2026-10-18; until then a card could only go with the whole username.
  • Where it is kept. Under a scrambled form of your handle, the same as your pets, at Netlify, beside the channel. Every answer about a profile asks search engines not to index or archive it.
  • What we do not keep. The same as the radio: no IP address, nothing in any log, no time the card was saved, and no list of who has a card or who looked at one.

We keep your card because you filled it in and saved it to be shown, and keeping it is what that takes. Saving it is your consent to it, and clearing it takes it back.

The Slake

The MUD Room has a world out its back door called the Slake, a multi-user dungeon with nobody to fight. Playing it on your own happens inside the page and sends nothing. Meeting other people on it is part of the CB and runs on the radio’s rules, with one switch of its own, Be seen out here, which is off every time you arrive and is never remembered.

  • What it sends, and when. Only once you are signed on to the CB and have pressed Be seen out here, and only while the tab is in front of you. Then, every few seconds and whenever you move, it sends your pass, which place you are in, and a random number the page makes up for this visit, which nobody else is ever shown and which is how your visit moves and leaves without a list of visits anywhere. When you say something, your words come too. Switch it off, put the tab behind another or leave the page, and it tells us you have gone and sends nothing more.
  • Who can see you. Anybody else who is signed on, has chosen to be seen, and is in the same place as you, by your handle. Being seen is also the only way to see: nobody can watch the Slake without being seen on it. Nobody is given a list of who is out there, or a number of people, and the map shows only you. An unclaimed handle is not checked, so a name out there is not proof of who somebody is.
  • What we keep. While you are seen, one record: your handle, whether you signed on as the base station, which place you are in, the time of your last check, and the visit’s random number. Moving replaces it rather than adding to it, so where you have been is not kept. It is shown to nobody thirty seconds after your last check, and deleted when you stop being seen or leave, or within two minutes by anybody else’s check, or by the hourly sweep at the latest. What is said in a place is kept like the radio’s channel: the latest ten things said there, each with its handle, its words and its time, all of it deleted at midnight, Colorado time, and the base station can take one off sooner. There is no archive, no backup and no copy.
  • What we do not keep. The same as the radio: no IP address, nothing in any log, no list of who was out there, and no record of where anybody went. Your tide, what you are carrying, the places you have found and the map stay in your page and are gone when you close it.
  • Where it is kept. Beside the channel, at Netlify, under Netlify’s privacy policy.

We keep that you are in a place because you chose to be seen there, and keeping it for as long as you are is what that takes. Pressing Be seen out here is your consent to it, and pressing it again takes that back. We keep what you say in a place for the day because you said it to whoever is there, the same as on the radio, and you can ask us to delete it sooner.

Your rights

Because we hold so little, most of what the law gives you a right to ask about is not here to ask about. What there is:

  • Your photograph, your recording or your name in a credit: ask us to see it, correct it or take it down, and we will.
  • What you said on the CB: ask us and we will delete it now rather than at midnight. After midnight there is nothing left to ask about.
  • What you said on the Slake: ask us and we will delete it now rather than at midnight. Where you were is already gone.
  • What you chalked on the board: ask us and we will rub it out now rather than at the end of its week.
  • What you pinned on Pando Calrissian’s fence: ask us and we will take it down now rather than at the end of its week. Your waterings are not there to ask about: the tree never knew they were yours.
  • What you dropped in the suggestion box: take it down yourself, from the box, or ask any moderator and they will.
  • Your pets: Forget me in Profile on your radio deletes them, with your avatars, at once, or ask us and we will.
  • Your avatars: delete any one from Profile, or Forget me there deletes them all at once, and the one you wore comes off Who’s online first.
  • The jobs kept in your Profile: switch off Keep my jobs in my Profile on the Guild’s board, or Forget me in Profile deletes them with everything else.
  • Your battle jacket: Forget me in Profile takes every patch off it, or ask us and we will.
  • Your username: Delete my account in Profile deletes it, its password, your pets, your avatars, your jobs and your patches at once. Ask a moderator if you cannot get in, and they can delete it for you.
  • An animal’s name in a shelter or on the list of everybody adopted: tell us which animal and we will take the name off. Nothing says which ones were yours, so only you can tell us.
  • A word on the Fridge of Sighs: nothing says which words were yours, so we cannot find them for you; but if a word or a sentence should not be there, tell us what it says and we will take it down.
  • Our host’s logs: Netlify holds those. Ask us and we will ask on your behalf, or contact Netlify directly.
  • What your browser keeps: it is on your device and yours to clear. We never keep a copy, the CB pass included.

If you are in the EU or the UK, you also have the right to access, correct, erase, restrict, object to and move your personal data, and to complain to your data protection authority. We do not sell or share personal information for advertising, so a Global Privacy Control signal from your browser has nothing here to opt you out of — the answer is already no.

Reporting a security problem

If you have found a hole in this site, report it privately on GitHub; the policy is in SECURITY.md and security.txt. A report there is handled by GitHub under GitHub’s privacy statement.
