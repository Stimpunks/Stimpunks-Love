Privacy

What this site knows about you is very little, and this page is all of it. Last updated 2026-10-24. Every change to it is dated in the changelog.

THE SHORT VERSION

  • No cookies. This site sets none, on any page.
  • No advertising, no trackers, nothing sold, and no email unless you give us an address. There is nobody to sell anything to. The CB takes an invite, from a moderator or from our community’s join form, and joining with one claims a username of your own with a password of your own. That username is your account: the username, its password, and an email address only if you give one, which is used for nothing but sending you a reset code you ask for. Moderators can see a list of every username, how many there are and who is seen in a room now, and nothing else about anybody. More below.
  • A few things are kept in your own browser, each because you asked for it. Only one of them is ever sent anywhere, and only to us, and only by the CB: the radio, the chalkboard, the pebble bowls, the suggestion box, Pando Calrissian’s tree and its fence, the Fridge of Sighs, Count Me In’s stair, the cat and dog shelters and Profile, the forums, and the Slake.
  • The CB is the one thing on this site that sends anything, and only while you have it open. What is said on it is kept for 48 hours, fifty messages a channel at most, and then deleted, unless somebody copies it into a forum topic.
  • A room’s call is run by 8x8, and only people signed on to the CB can join one, except the three public calls behind Cavendish Coworking’s meeting doors, which a guest joins under a name they type. While somebody is in a call we keep their name in it, from 8x8, and it goes when they leave. There is also a World call, which anybody signed on can join from any room. The radio’s Who’s online names the World call and every room whose call has somebody in it, and never who or how many. More below.
  • You can choose to be seen. Signed on, Be seen here puts your handle and the room you are in on the radio’s Who’s online list, for anybody signed on to the CB, and shows you and anybody else seen in the same room to each other, with who is in its call. Nobody who has not chosen it is shown, and nothing is counted. In a room that keeps to itself, being seen shows you only to people seen there, and never on Who’s online. More below.
  • Hosting a film is the CB too. If you press Host this film, everybody signed on sees which film you are watching, in which room, and where it has got to, until you stop. More below.
  • The chalkboard on Plural Mural is the CB’s too, and it is public. Only people signed on to the CB can write on it, anybody at all can read it, and every note is rubbed out after seven days.
  • The World room is a list of every room on the street, and anybody can read it. Signed on to the CB, it also shows which rooms are active now: who has chosen to be seen in each, whether its call has somebody in it, and when its channel last had a message today. Its Most active order is the one place on the street that counts people, to put the busiest rooms first; it never shows the numbers. More below.
  • The forums are the CB’s too, and only claimed usernames can read them. Topics in categories, each kept for a week, three months, six months or for good after its last post, with your username on what you write. Nothing about who reads is kept, and a link on a line of its own is previewed by our server reading that page once, when the post is made. Messages copied from the CB last as long as their topic, and whoever sent one can take it out again. More below.
  • Tasks, the team’s to-do list, are the CB’s too, and only our directors, board members and administrators can read them, with the usernames of who each task is assigned to. Each director’s saved filters are kept under their username, for them alone. More below.
  • The office holds the people and organisations we are in touch with, our grant requests, and the connection requests and volunteer applications that come in, moved here from EspoCRM, and only our directors, board members and administrators can open it. Most of them never signed on to the street, so everything in it is encrypted where it is kept, nothing about it is ever logged, and nothing records who looks. More below.
  • The suggestion box in The Sandbox is the CB’s too, and it is public. Signed on, you can drop in a suggestion, a feature request or a bug report, with your handle on it. Anybody can read it, a moderator answers it with their own username on the reply, and it stays until you or a moderator take it down. More below.
  • Pando Calrissian’s tree is the CB’s too. Signed on, you can water it. It keeps how many times it has been watered, for good, and never who watered it. The fence beside it holds notes on the chalkboard’s rules. More below.
  • The Fridge of Sighs is the CB’s too. Signed on, you can put up one word of a sentence. Finished sentences are kept for good and no word says who put it up. More below.
  • Count Me In’s stair is the CB’s too. Signed on, you can take the next step, by number or by the lift. It keeps the step it is on and the highest it has reached, for good, and never who took any step. More below.
  • Rescue A Cat, Rescue A Dog and Rescue Small Animals are the CB’s too. Signed on, you can rescue an animal and name it, and adopt one into Profile on your radio. The shelters and the list of everybody adopted never say who rescued or adopted anybody. Your pets, your avatars, your chimeras, your battle jacket’s patches and, if you ask it to, the jobs you hand in at the Guild are the things the street keeps under a person, filed under a scrambled form of your handle, and Forget me under Account deletes them. More below.
  • Avatars are the CB’s too. Signed on, you can keep a bodymind you drew at the Bodymind Booth, or an outfit from the Dress-Up Den, as an avatar in Profile, and with a claimed username wear one beside your name on Who’s online. An avatar is a list of choices, never a picture, and Forget me under Account deletes them. More below.
  • Chimeras are the CB’s too. Signed on, you can keep a chimera you made in Chimera Obscura in Profile, name it, and send it as a sticker. A chimera is a list of choices, never a picture, and Forget me under Account deletes them. More below.
  • Props are the CB’s too. With a claimed username, you can open a Props tab on your profile, where anybody else with a claimed username can give you props: a sentence or a short paragraph thanking you. Each waits until you confirm it, and only then can anybody else read it. Forget me under Account deletes them. More below.
  • The Slake, out the back of the MUD Room, is the CB too. Signed on, you can choose to be seen out there, on each visit, and only then does it send anything: where you are and what you say. You meet people in the place you are in; nobody gets a list of who is out there, and nothing is counted.
  • Nothing from anybody else loads until you press it. Every video and player on the street waits for a press.
  • Our host keeps ordinary server logs, the way every web server does.

What our host sees

This site is served by Netlify. Like any web server, it receives what every browser sends with every request — your IP address, your browser’s user-agent string, the address you asked for and the time — and it keeps logs of that under Netlify’s own privacy policy, which also says how long. We need that to run and secure a website, which is the legal basis for it (legitimate interests). We have not switched on Netlify’s analytics, we do not add anything to those logs, and we do not use them to find out who anybody is.

Every typeface, script, stylesheet and image on this site is served from this site, so opening a page makes no request to anybody but Netlify.

Searching the street

When you follow a link away

A lot of this site is links to other places. When you follow one, the site you arrive at learns that you came from stimpunks.world — the address of the site, never the page you were on — and from then on you are covered by that site’s policy.

People on these pages

Some pages carry photographs of people, recordings of people’s voices, and the names of the people we credit. Every photograph of somebody, and every recording of somebody’s voice, is published with that person’s consent, recorded in our files with a date — the readings in Hear Queer Here included — and it comes down when the person in it says so: one message, no reason needed. A young person who is doing the work and has said yes themselves is published and credited like anybody else; a parent’s yes does not stand in for theirs, and a child too young to give that consent is not published at all. The whole of that is on the Polaroid Wall’s page. The photographs are excluded from this site’s CC BY-SA licence precisely so that taking one down is something we can actually do.

The CB

The CB is a chat channel, patterned after citizens band radio. You join with an invite, on the joining page, which claims a username of your own with a password of your own, and after that you sign on at the Community Center or on the bar in the corner of any page. Until you sign on, the radio is not on any page and nothing is sent. Until 21 October 2026 the CB took a starter password the community shared, and a username was claimed only if you chose to; that password signs nobody on now, and a radio still on with it can only claim the username it is on.

  • Before you sign on. A folded bar in the corner of each page, with a teleporter and a way to find out how to get on the CB. It is not the radio: it sends nothing to the channel, knows nothing about who is on it, and shows nothing that is said. Opening the teleporter fetches this site’s list of rooms, from this site, and nothing else is fetched. If you leave its panel open, your browser remembers that under love-cb-guest, and folding it away deletes it. You can sign on from it too, with your username and your password: that sends exactly what the front desk sends, only when you press Sign on, and then the radio takes its place.
  • What it sends. While the radio is open on your screen and the tab is in front of you, it asks this site for the latest messages every few seconds, and the first time it does, or the first time you open its teleporter, it also fetches the list of rooms on the street, so that a room named with a # can be shown as a link and the teleporter knows where it can take you; that request carries nothing about you. With a claimed username it also asks us, once, which categories in the forums you can read, because each of them is a room on the CB that the public list does not name; that request carries your pass, the answer names only categories you can read, and nothing records who asked. Teleporting to a room is an ordinary link on this site: it sends nothing to the channel and tells nobody where you went. Typing an @ into it asks us who is online, once, and narrows that list in your browser, so what you type after the @ is not sent. More below. Closed, folded down, or in a tab you are not looking at, it sends nothing. When you transmit, your handle and your words come to us.
  • World, or this room. The radio hears one channel at a time. World is the one everybody shares, and listening to it tells us nothing about where you are on the street. This room is the room’s own channel, and to hear it the radio has to tell us which room it is, each time it asks. We do not keep that: nothing records who listened to which room. The radio starts on World, and it remembers which you chose, in your own browser. In the forums, this room is the category you are reading (Ryan’s call, 2026-10-21): a category’s page, a topic in it, or a new topic you start in it, and a category inside another is the room of the one it is in. Each category’s room is a room in every way, with its own channel, Be seen here, call and Follow this room, and only the claimed usernames that can read the category can hear it, be seen in it, call in it or be told about it, so a staff category’s room is its staff’s, as its topics are. The forums’ home, Latest, Mentions and Search are the forums’ own room. Going from one category to another tells us the new one the next time the radio asks, as walking into another room does. On the chat page (since 2026-10-22) the radio is docked into the page, and the channel you pick there is the one it hears, with that room’s Be seen here and call: picking one tells us which, the next time the radio asks, as walking into the room would, and nothing records it. The page keeps your pick only in its own address, after a #, which your browser never sends. It marks what is new in the other channels from what Unreads already asks, and asks nothing more. If you sort the channels under tags by Recently active or Most active (Ryan’s ask, 2026-10-24), the page asks us once when it opens, when you choose the order and when you press Sort again, never as messages come in: we look at the channels you can hear and answer with two orders, newest message first and most messages in the last 48 hours first, never a time or a number, leaving out any room that keeps to itself and any room you cannot go into, and counting none of the street’s bot’s messages. Nothing is kept for it and nothing records who asked. Which order you chose is kept in love-cb, in your own browser. A to Z, where everybody starts, asks nothing.
  • A place in a film. Somebody can put a place like @34:12 in a message, and on every radio it is a button that moves the film on your page to that place. It never presses play, and nobody else can move your film: only your own press does. Add my spot reads where the film on your page has got to, and the film’s title and the room you are in, and writes them into your message box. The room’s name in a spot links to that room with the spot on the end of the address, after a #, which your browser never sends to any server, ours included; arriving that way gets that film’s play button ready to start at the spot, and nothing plays until you press it. The radio and the film talk to each other inside your browser. That sends nothing, to us or to YouTube, until you transmit it like anything else you type.
  • What we keep. The latest fifty messages on each channel, World and every room’s, each with its handle, its words, the handles it @mentions, which message it replies to, if it is a reply, the time it was sent, the time its sender last edited it, if they did, whether it came from the base station or from a claimed username (since 2026-10-21 every username is claimed, so the radio no longer shows that one), the picture sent with it, if there was one, and its description, and the sticker, if it was one: a copy of what one of your pets looks like and is called, or of one of your avatars, its name and what you wrote about it, or of one of your chimeras, which animal gave each part and its name, or which patch, or which patches were on your battle jacket, taken when you sent it. Reactions are kept on the message they were pressed on: which reaction and the handle that pressed it, shown to everybody on that channel by handle and never as a number, and deleted with the message. A fifty-first pushes the oldest off, picture and all. Every message is deleted 48 hours after it was sent. That was Ryan’s call, 2026-10-18, because people on the street live in timezones all over the world and were missing what was said while they slept; until then everything on every channel was deleted every day at midnight, Colorado time, and everything sent before that midnight still was. Whoever sent a message can delete it sooner with Take off, picture, reactions and all, and a moderator can delete any message. Taking one off sends your pass, which message and which channel. There is no archive and no backup, and the only copy is one somebody makes into a forum topic.
  • A picture. A screenshot or a photo sent on the channel is redrawn in your own browser before it leaves it, which takes off everything a camera or a phone writes into the file, where it was taken first of all; this site refuses a file that still carries any of it. It is kept only with its message and deleted when the message goes, and one that was never sent is deleted within the hour. It has no public address: only a radio signed on to that channel can fetch it, and a picture sent in one of the Town Hall’s private rooms only by the people that room is for. Download saves the copy your radio already holds onto your own device, which is yours; it asks us for nothing more.
  • An @mention. Typing an @ into the message box offers the people who have spoken on the channel and the people on Who’s online, and the first one asks us who is online, once. A message keeps the handles it mentions, at most ten, so every radio draws them as names: your radio says who it means, and we keep only the ones the words really name. Who is told about a mention is unchanged, and only a device that asked is.
  • A link to another site. An address on another site in a message is a link that opens in a new tab and says which site it goes to, and so is one with words of its own, [words](an address), whose site is written after the words. Following it sends that site nothing from here: no referrer, so it is not told which page of ours you came from, and nothing about the channel. What it does once you are there is up to that site.
  • Copy. Copy on a message puts its words on your own clipboard, in your browser. It sends nothing, to us or anybody.
  • Replies (since 2026-10-21). A reply keeps which message it answers, and nothing else of it: not its words and not who sent it. Every radio draws the line above a reply from what is on the channel at that moment, so when the message answered is edited the line changes, and when it is taken off or its 48 hours are up, the line says it answered a message no longer on the channel. Replying sends your pass, your message, which channel, and which message it answers, which must still be on that channel. It tells whoever sent that message only if they switched on When somebody replies to me.
  • Edit. Whoever sent a message can change its words with Edit; a moderator can edit only their own, like everybody else. The words it had before are not kept, by us or anywhere: the message is rewritten where it is, says edited and when, keeps its picture, sticker and reactions, and still goes 48 hours after it was first sent. Editing sends your pass, which message, which channel and the new words. An edit tells nobody about it unless it @mentions somebody it did not name before, and then it tells only them, if they asked to be told about mentions.
  • Take to the forums. With a claimed username, Take to the forums on a message lets you tick messages on the channel and start a forum topic with them. Our server copies them from the channel itself, words, handles, marks and times exactly as they are on the channel, with edited on any their sender has edited, and they then last as long as the topic does. Pictures and stickers stay behind. Whoever sent a copied message can take it out of the topic again, and a moderator can. Ask everybody in the conversation first, as you would before a screenshot.
  • How many messages a day. For our directors’ dashboard in the office, every message sent on World or in a room adds one to a count for its day: three numbers a day, for World, for the rooms, and for the rooms only some people can hear: the moderators’ rooms, and since 2026-10-21 the rooms of the forum categories only staff can read. No handle, no room, no words and no time go with it, so it says how busy the channels were and nothing about who was on them or where. Direct messages are not counted. Each day’s three numbers are kept for 400 days.
  • What we do not keep. Your IP address is not stored with a message or anywhere else by us, and we do not write handles or messages into any log. We keep no list of who has signed on.
  • Where it is kept. Netlify, our host, stores the channel for us and runs the small program that answers the radio, under Netlify’s privacy policy. Netlify’s ordinary logs record that the radio made a request, the same as for any page. Netlify also limits how often one address can try the password; it does that itself, without handing the address to us.
  • Who can read it. Anybody signed on, which means everybody who has joined. That makes it a room full of people rather than a private line, so say on it what you would say in a room. A username signs on only with its owner’s password, so a name says whoever holds that password sent it, and nothing more about who they are. A moderator’s is marked BASE: our moderators’ usernames, and which of them hold the board, director or administrator role, are on a list that is not published. A username on it is claimed only with the moderators’ password as well as its owner’s own. The list is read each time a pass is checked and never written to by the radio, and the only thing a radio is told about it is its own roles, so that it can keep out of the Town Hall’s rooms it cannot use.
  • The radio in a window of its own. Pop out moves the radio into a browser window of its own, which stays open while you go from room to room in the window you came from. It is the same radio with the same pass, and it sends what the radio on that page would have: it reads which page you are on, its address and title and what it says about itself, straight from that window, inside your own browser, to know which room you are in. Your other pages learn that it is out, and ask it to come back, over a channel that stays inside your own browser, and love-cb notes that it is out until the window closes. Nothing more about it is sent to us or kept.

  • Replies, once a conversation is accepted. As on the channel: a reply keeps which message in the conversation it answers and nothing else of it, and says so once that message has gone.
  • Pictures, once a conversation is accepted. Ryan’s call, 2026-10-19, after somebody in our community could not send him a screenshot privately: a first message is words only, so nobody’s first contact with you can be a picture, and once a conversation is accepted either of you can add a picture to a message. It is the channel’s picture in every way that matters: redrawn in your own browser before it is sent, which takes off everything a camera or a phone wrote into it, where it was taken first of all, and refused by our server if any of that is still in it; a description is offered and never required; and it has no public address. It is kept beside the conversation, with which of you sent it and when, is fetched with your pass by the two of you and nobody else, and goes the moment its message goes: taken back, at its 48 hours, pushed out by the fiftieth after it, or with the whole conversation when either of you blocks the other, rejects it or deletes their username. One you added and never sent is deleted within the hour. Ask anybody recognisable in a picture before you send it.
  • Blocking. Block, under a conversation, stops that person writing to you and you to them, and deletes your conversation for both of you. They are not told: if they try to write, they are told you are not taking messages from them, the same words as if your switch were off. A block is kept in your own record, by a scrambled form of the name you blocked, so it holds if that name is deleted and claimed again, and nobody but you is shown your block list. Unblock under All messages takes it off.
  • They are not end-to-end encrypted. Your words travel to our server encrypted, like everything on this site, and are kept at Netlify beside the channel, where our server can read them, as it reads the channel to send it to you. Nothing on the street shows a conversation to anybody but the two people in it. No moderator can read one: there is no way for them to, on the desk or anywhere else. End-to-end encryption, where only your devices hold the keys, would be a much bigger thing to build and to keep working on every device, and it is not this.
  • Reporting a message. Report, on a message the other person sent you, is the one way a message reaches the moderators. It copies that message’s words, its picture if it has one, who sent it and when, your username and a note if you write one onto the moderators’ desk. A reported picture is shown on the desk only when a moderator presses to see it. The person you report is not told. A report stays until a moderator closes it, which deletes it, or for thirty days at most, even if either of you deletes your username, because it is how the moderators look after the street. Nothing else about the conversation goes with it.
  • Being told. If you switch on When somebody messages me under Notifications, a device switched on as your claimed username is told when somebody writes to you. The sender is never told anything about it.
  • What is sent. While Messages is open on your radio, and only then, it asks for your conversations, or the one on your screen, every few seconds: your pass, and the username you are talking with, in the body of the request rather than the address. Folded, in a tab behind another, or with Messages shut, it asks nothing. On the chat page your conversations are listed beside the channels, so while that page is in front of you it also asks for that list, at most every half minute and sooner when somebody new has written; which conversation you pick there is kept nowhere, not even in the page’s address.
  • What is kept. Whether you take messages, and who you blocked. For each conversation: the two usernames, which of you asked, whether it was accepted, and its messages, each its words, its picture and description if it has one, the sticker if it is one (a copy of one of your avatars, chimeras, pets or patches, or your battle jacket, taken when you sent it), who reacted to it with what, who sent it, BASE if a moderator did, and when it was sent and last edited. An empty marker for each of you, with when it was written, so your list can be found without anybody keeping a list of who talks to whom. Nothing about who read what, for anybody else: no read receipts, nothing telling the other person a message was seen, nothing saying somebody is typing, and nothing is counted: no number of unread messages anywhere. For yourself, when you last read each conversation is kept so the radio can mark what is new to you; see what is new to you.
  • Deleting them. Forget me and Delete my account under Account delete your switch, your block list and every conversation you are in, for both of you. Reports stay on the desk for their thirty days.
  • What we do not keep. The same as the radio: no IP address, nothing in any log, no copy anywhere, and no list of who has written to whom. Every answer about messages asks search engines not to index or archive it.

We keep messages because the two of you wrote them to each other, and keeping them for 48 hours is what a conversation takes. Accepting a conversation is your consent to it, and Block, Forget me and deleting your username take it back.

Your chimeras

A chimera is one creature you made out of two animals in Chimera Obscura. Making one, changing it and naming it happen in your browser and send nothing: a link to it carries it after the # in the address, which never reaches any server. Chimeras are the sixth thing the street can keep under a person, after pets, avatars, jobs, patches and profile cards, and only when you press Keep it in my Profile. That was Ryan’s call, 2026-10-18.

  • A chimera is a list of choices, never a picture. What is kept is which animal gave each part, every one a word out of the room’s own shelf, which our server checks against that shelf and refuses anything else; and its name, if you give it one. Nothing about when you made it is kept. The drawing is made fresh from the choices wherever it is shown.
  • Keeping one. Signed on to the CB, Keep it in my Profile in the room sends your pass, the choices and the name. Renaming or deleting one from Profile sends your pass, which chimera and what changed. We keep up to 24 at once.
  • Where they are kept. Under a scrambled form of your handle, the same as your pets and your avatars, so the store does not list handles, at Netlify, beside the channel. Only your password reaches them: nobody else can sign on as your username.
  • Showing them, if you choose to. With a claimed username, Show my chimeras on the CB in Profile shows them in your profile, with their names, to anybody signed on to the CB who opens it. It is off until you switch it on. We keep only that it is on, and when you switched it on; switching it off, Forget me and deleting your username delete that. Opening somebody’s profile sends your pass and their handle, and we keep neither; the answer never says whether somebody has chimeras they have not shown.
  • As a sticker. Sticker on the radio can send one of your chimeras to the channel you are tuned to, with its name and which animal gave which part. It is a message like any other, kept and deleted with the channel’s messages.
  • Deleting them. Delete any one from Profile, which asks once more; Forget me under Account deletes them all with your pets, and whether they are shown; and Delete my account deletes them with your username.
  • What we do not keep. The same as the radio: no IP address, nothing in any log, and no list of who has a chimera or who looked at one. Every answer about your chimeras asks search engines not to index or archive it.

We keep your chimeras because you pressed Keep and asked the street to remember them, and keeping them is what that takes. Pressing Keep is your consent to it, and deleting them takes it back.

The Slake

The MUD Room has a world out its back door called the Slake, a multi-user dungeon with nobody to fight. Playing it on your own happens inside the page and sends nothing. Meeting other people on it is part of the CB and runs on the radio’s rules, with one switch of its own, Be seen out here, which is off every time you arrive and is never remembered.

  • What it sends, and when. Only once you are signed on to the CB and have pressed Be seen out here, and only while the tab is in front of you. Then, every few seconds and whenever you move, it sends your pass, which place you are in, and a random number the page makes up for this visit, which nobody else is ever shown and which is how your visit moves and leaves without a list of visits anywhere. When you say something, your words come too. Switch it off, put the tab behind another or leave the page, and it tells us you have gone and sends nothing more.
  • Who can see you. Anybody else who is signed on, has chosen to be seen, and is in the same place as you, by your handle. Being seen is also the only way to see: nobody can watch the Slake without being seen on it. Nobody is given a list of who is out there, or a number of people, and the map shows only you. A username says whoever holds its password is there, and nothing more about who they are.
  • What we keep. While you are seen, one record: your handle, whether you signed on as the base station, which place you are in, the time of your last check, and the visit’s random number. Moving replaces it rather than adding to it, so where you have been is not kept. It is shown to nobody thirty seconds after your last check, and deleted when you stop being seen or leave, or within two minutes by anybody else’s check, or by the hourly sweep at the latest. What is said in a place is kept like the radio’s channel: the latest fifty things said there, each with its handle, its words and its time, each deleted 48 hours after it was said, and the base station can take one off sooner. There is no archive, no backup and no copy.
  • What we do not keep. The same as the radio: no IP address, nothing in any log, no list of who was out there, and no record of where anybody went. Your tide, what you are carrying, the places you have found and the map stay in your page and are gone when you close it.
  • Where it is kept. Beside the channel, at Netlify, under Netlify’s privacy policy.

We keep that you are in a place because you chose to be seen there, and keeping it for as long as you are is what that takes. Pressing Be seen out here is your consent to it, and pressing it again takes that back. We keep what you say in a place for 48 hours because you said it to whoever is there, the same as on the radio, and you can ask us to delete it sooner.

Your rights

Because we hold so little, most of what the law gives you a right to ask about is not here to ask about. What there is:

  • Your photograph, your recording or your name in a credit: ask us to see it, correct it or take it down, and we will.
  • What you said on the CB: ask us and we will delete it now rather than at its 48 hours. After that there is nothing left to ask about.
  • What you said on the Slake: ask us and we will delete it now rather than at its 48 hours. Where you were is already gone.
  • What you chalked on the board: ask us and we will rub it out now rather than at the end of its week.
  • What you pinned on Pando Calrissian’s fence: ask us and we will take it down now rather than at the end of its week. Your waterings are not there to ask about: the tree never knew they were yours.
  • What you dropped in the suggestion box: take it down yourself, from the box, or ask any moderator and they will.
  • Your pets: Forget me under Account on your radio deletes them, with your avatars, at once, or ask us and we will.
  • Your avatars: delete any one from Profile, or Forget me there deletes them all at once, and the one you wore comes off Who’s online first.
  • Your chimeras: delete any one from Profile, or Forget me there deletes them all at once.
  • The jobs kept in your Profile: switch off Keep my jobs in my Profile on the Guild’s board, or Forget me under Account deletes them with everything else.
  • Your battle jacket: Forget me under Account takes every patch off it, or ask us and we will.
  • Your props: take any one off your Props tab, or Forget me under Account deletes them all with everything waiting for you. A prop you wrote for somebody else: take it back from their tab, or ask a moderator and they will take it down.
  • Your username: Delete my account under Account deletes it, its password, your pets, your avatars, your chimeras, your jobs, your patches, your profile card, your props and your chat status at once. Ask a moderator if you cannot get in, and they can delete it for you.
  • An animal’s name in a shelter or on the list of everybody adopted: tell us which animal and we will take the name off. Nothing says which ones were yours, so only you can tell us.
  • A word on the Fridge of Sighs: nothing says which words were yours, so we cannot find them for you; but if a word or a sentence should not be there, tell us what it says and we will take it down.
  • Our host’s logs: Netlify holds those. Ask us and we will ask on your behalf, or contact Netlify directly.
  • What your browser keeps: it is on your device and yours to clear. We never keep a copy, the CB pass included.

If you are in the EU or the UK, you also have the right to access, correct, erase, restrict, object to and move your personal data, and to complain to your data protection authority. We do not sell or share personal information for advertising, so a Global Privacy Control signal from your browser has nothing here to opt you out of — the answer is already no.
