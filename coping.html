Coping

Getting through hard moments in a hard world: what to reach for, why it works, and why cope is not an insult.

This room is the eye of the storm. The storm is the hard world: paperwork, punishment, noise, people, and systems built for somebody else. It is still there, all the way round, in plain sight, and nothing in here pretends otherwise, because that is the difference between coping and denial. In the middle of it there is a patch of still air, clear sky straight up, and room to breathe.

This is a coping toolkit for getting through hard moments in a hard world.

Right now

Pick one. Do it imperfectly. Tiny is valid.

Stimpunks, Seeds of Cope

  • Lower sensory load

    Dim the lights, or switch the overheads off. Headphones or earplugs. A different position: sitting, lying down, leaning, on the floor. Fewer tabs, fewer voices, fewer tasks.

  • Breathe and downshift

    In for four seconds and out for six, six times over, the breath out longer than the breath in. A hum, a sigh or a yawn. Cold water on your face or your wrists.

  • One body move

    Hands pressed together hard for ten seconds. A blanket wrapped round you, or deep pressure. Rock, pace, flap, stim, which our page grants permission for in as many words. A stretch of the jaw, the shoulders, the hands.

If you only do one thing: reduce inputs. Lower sensory load first. Everything is harder when your nervous system is on fire.

The cope map

Not everything is psychological. A lot of it is sensory, social, and systemic.

Stimpunks, Seeds of Cope

Seeds to plant ahead, or grab in a storm

These are small, repeatable interventions. You can plant them ahead of time, or grab them in a storm.

Stimpunks, Seeds of Cope

  1. Sensory safety

    Change the room: another chair, another corner, another light. Fewer sounds, fewer things to look at, fewer obligations. Somewhere to go without having to explain, and a small kit to carry, with earplugs, sunglasses, gum, something to fidget with and water.

    Stim is regulation. Let your body do its job.

    Stimpunks, Seeds of Cope

  2. Boundaries, exits and social load

    Writing instead of talking, which our page says is often the great social equalizer. Less performing: drop the eye contact, the small talk, the masking. Being together without talking, which our page calls parallel presence. And an exit line written ahead of time, from the words further down.

    Permission to leave. You don’t need to justify your nervous system.

    Stimpunks, Seeds of Cope

  3. Monotropic spiral breaks

    One small change in the body: stand up, drink water, open a window. Write the thought down once and park it. Walk to a beat, tap, rock, count steps. Give the thought seven minutes on a timer, and then stop. Or move over to a safe special interest, a softer focus that can bring you back. Our page reads the loop through monotropism, and our glossary has a word for it, the monotropic spiral.

    Sometimes you’re not “overthinking.” You’re stuck in a loop because your attention won’t release and your body won’t downshift.

    Stimpunks, Seeds of Cope

  4. Body-based grounding

    Temperature: cold water, a warm drink, a heating pad. Orientation: five things you can see, four you can feel, three you can hear. Breath: a long breath out, humming, sighing. Hands: something to squeeze, palms pressed together, lotion rubbed in slowly.

    Pressure: weighted blanket, tight hoodie, firm hug (if wanted).

    Stimpunks, Seeds of Cope

  5. When it is the system

    Our page’s reframe is that this is a mismatch, not a moral failure. Ask who benefits from the rule as it is written. Broken systems, not broken people. Ask for support, which you deserve without humiliation rituals, and remember that refusing can be a way of protecting yourself.

    If your life is being squeezed by paperwork, punishment, sorting, surveillance, and “standards,” you may be reacting normally to abnormal conditions.

    Stimpunks, Seeds of Cope

  6. Aftercare, after a crash

    Fewer demands: cancel what you can, put off what you can. Water, food and rest, which are not optional. Quiet and predictability, because our page says routine is medicine. Repair the shame, in our page’s words: my nervous system did what it had to do.

    After a meltdown/shutdown/panic spike, your job is gentle recovery — not “getting back to normal.”

    Stimpunks, Seeds of Cope

Words you can borrow

Use these as-is. Edit them. Make them yours. The point is to reduce effort in the moment.

Stimpunks, Seeds of Cope

  • Exit, or a pause

    I need a moment to regulate. I’ll return when I’m able.

    I’m hitting sensory overload. I’m going to step out.

  • Asking for support

    I’m not okay right now. Can you stay nearby and be quiet with me?

    I need fewer questions. Please give me time and space.

  • At work or school

    I work/learn better with breaks. I’m taking one so I can come back regulated.

    Written is easier for me than verbal. Can we switch to text/email?

The science of cope

Coping is not imaginary. It is neurobiological.

Stimpunks, The Science of Cope

  1. The body notices threat before thinking does

    Our page starts from Stephen Porges’ Polyvagal Theory and his word for it, neuroception: the body moves into survival before reasoning has caught up. Fight, flight, freeze, fawn. Withdrawing, stimming, pacing, shutting down, seeking comfort and seeking connection are all, on our page, ways of steadying the autonomic nervous system.

    These are not personality flaws. They are adaptive responses.

    Stimpunks, The Science of Cope

    That is physiology, not weakness.

    Stimpunks, The Science of Cope

  2. Trauma is the body adapting

    After Bessel van der Kolk and Judith Herman, our page reads trauma as a body-level answer to overwhelming stress. Hypervigilance is adaptive under threat, dissociation protective under overwhelm, and avoidance saves energy under danger.

    Trauma symptoms are not random. They are adaptations that once increased survival probability.

    Stimpunks, The Science of Cope

  3. The load adds up

    Bruce McEwen’s allostatic load is the wear chronic stress leaves on a body. Sleep changes, sensory tolerance narrows, emotional bandwidth shrinks, executive function declines. Reducing light, seeking sameness, limiting social demand and controlling the environment are, our page says, attempts to rebalance.

    Coping behaviors often emerge to lower allostatic load.

    Stimpunks, The Science of Cope

  4. Neurodivergent coping gets misread

    Repetitive movement, eating the same food, avoiding eye contact, needing parallel presence. Our page says autistic burnout research increasingly frames burnout as a stress injury, built out of masking, overextension and sensory overload, and coping as protection against it.

    They are not “malfunctioning.” They are regulating.

    Stimpunks, The Science of Cope

  5. The environment matters

    The social model of disability says difficulty does not come from impairment alone. When an environment overloads a body, the body adapts, and coping is often an answer to inaccessible design.

    If removing fluorescent lights reduces shutdown, the problem was lighting — not character.

    Stimpunks, The Science of Cope

    If written communication reduces panic, the problem was phone culture — not fragility.

    Stimpunks, The Science of Cope

    Coping behaviors frequently expose structural flaws.

    Stimpunks, The Science of Cope

  6. Regulation comes first

    Internet culture uses copium to mean fooling yourself. Our page answers that regulation is the first step towards higher-order thinking: without it, learning declines, reasoning narrows and social capacity collapses. Regulation is what makes agency possible.

    Coping is not delusion. It is prerequisite to change.

    Stimpunks, The Science of Cope

Coping is not the enemy of change. It is what keeps people alive long enough to build it.

A reclamation

They turned cope into a sneer.

Stimpunks, Cope Is Not an Insult

“Cope” and “copium” get thrown at people as a sneer, as if surviving were something to be embarrassed about and adapting were a delusion. Our manifesto rejects that frame.

Cope is not denial. Cope is not fantasy. Cope is not failure.

Stimpunks, Cope Is Not an Insult

Cope is what living nervous systems do under pressure.

Cope is intelligence

When the ground shifts, organisms adapt, and when systems turn hostile, people improvise. Our manifesto calls that evolutionary brilliance, and names it everywhere it turns up.

Stimming? Cope. Parallel play? Cope. Mutual aid? Cope. Lowering the lights? Cope. Leaving the room? Cope. Naming the harm? Cope.

Coping is applied knowledge of reality.

Not “just cope”

When somebody says “just cope”, they usually mean stop complaining, accept the harm, adjust to it quietly. Real coping is not passive acceptance; our page calls it strategic survival.

That is not coping. That is submission.

Stimpunks, Cope Is Not an Insult

We cope to reduce harm. We cope to stay alive. We cope so we can build something better.

Stimpunks, Cope Is Not an Insult

Then yes. We inhale that.

Cope is resistance

Removing a demand. Refusing an unreasonable expectation. Stimming without apologising. Asking for written communication. Making a space sensory-safe. Resting instead of collapsing.

You are not escaping reality. You are refusing to let reality erase you.

Stimpunks, Cope Is Not an Insult

Coping is resistance scaled to the nervous system.

We cope because systems harm

Burnout is not a personality trait, overwhelm is not a moral defect, and shutdown is not laziness. When systems overload bodies, bodies respond.

Coping is not evidence of weakness. It is evidence of strain. And strain tells the truth about the structure.

What is not in the eye

  • No grit. Nobody here is told to toughen up, push through or bounce back. Our manifesto takes cope back from all of that, and this room keeps it.
  • Nothing clinical. This room is not clinical advice. Nothing in it tells you what you have or what will fix it, and nobody here is your doctor.
  • No tally. No mood tracker, no streak, no check-in that remembers you, no count of good days or bad ones. Nothing on this page keeps anything about you.
  • No calm down. Nobody here tells you to calm down, cheer up or look on the bright side. A storm is a storm.
  • Nothing moves by itself. Nothing in the eye moves by itself at any setting, MAX GLITTER included. The fractal window at the foot of the page moves only when you switch it on, in a window of its own.

Who reads the channel, and for how long

  • Everybody signed on to the CB can read this room’s channel, so say only what you would say in a room with all of them in it. It keeps the house norms, like every channel.
  • Every message on it goes 48 hours after it was sent, like every channel on the CB. For something that needs longer, Coping in the forums keeps a topic 1 week after its last post.
  • Nobody on it is a professional, and nobody has to fix anything or answer. If you want people who can help with more than talk, Community Care is the room for asking.
