Cavendish Coworking

every door propped open, and nothing to sign up for

Stimpunks is run in the open. Our team meetings are open to the world, because we default to open, and until now the only way into them was our Discord — which a great many people will not use, and that is their call to make rather than a thing to be talked out of. So here they are on the street, with the room our events meet in beside them, each behind a door that opens straight onto the call. You do not need the Discord, an account, or anybody’s say-so to walk through.

Every door says when its room is in use before you open it. The times are Central, as our events page gives them; where your browser will say, the room works out the same hour on your own clock underneath. Clocks do not all change for summer on the same day, so the week they change, trust the Central.

On the other side of a door

Each door opens Proton Meet’s guest page for one room, in your browser. We opened every one of them on 2026-09-25 as far as that page and no further. Here is what was there:

  • No account, and nothing to install. It asks for a name, which can be whatever you would like to be called, and it names the room you are about to walk into, so you can check it is the right one. It offers a desktop app; you do not need it.
  • Your microphone and camera can stay off. If your browser has not been given them, it says so and offers to carry on without, and you can go in that way.
  • Proton’s own terms apply over there. Its page says that joining means agreeing to Proton’s terms and conditions and privacy policy. That is between you and Proton, and it is written here so that nobody finds it out after the press.
  • The part of each link after the # is the room’s password, and it is there on purpose. These rooms are open, so it is not a secret; it is what takes you straight to the name box instead of a locked door. A browser never sends that part of an address to any server, and this site tells Proton nothing on the way out but that you came from stimpunks.world.

They are doors and not windows, and that was measured rather than assumed. Proton Meet will only let Proton’s own apps frame it, so a call inside this page would load as a blank box; and this street gives no page on it your camera or your microphone. A link dressed as a window would promise the one thing it cannot do. Nothing on this page reaches Proton until you press a door.

Why Cavendish

Cavendish Space is our name for psychologically and sensory safe space: caves for quiet reflection, campfires for learning together, watering holes for peer connection. We named it after Henry Cavendish, the eighteenth-century natural philosopher who discovered hydrogen, as Steve Silberman tells his life in NeuroTribes. Cavendish needed his cave. He also needed, now and then, a small set of his peers — the Royal Society’s Monday Club was his campfire — and he came to it on his own terms.

It is not true, however, that he wanted to remove himself entirely from the company of his peers; he just wanted to stand off to the side, soaking everything in.

Steve Silberman, NeuroTribes, p. 25, as our Cavendish Space entry quotes it

The chair against the wall is for anybody who would rather come in the way he did. It is out of the light and off to the side, facing the open doors, and Proton will let you go in with your microphone and camera both off. Everything else in the room is glass, because the rest of how we work is too.

Next door in spirit: body doubling, which our Watering Hole Hangs welcome, and whose nature cams run up the street in the Jungle Room. And the calendar with everything on it is on our events page.

What is not in here

  • Nothing to sign up for, and no Discord between you and the door.
  • No count of who went through. This page keeps nothing and sends nothing. Working out your own hour happens in your browser, from its own clock and time zone, and goes nowhere.
  • No call inside the page. See above: a door, shaped like one.
  • No blinds. A meeting room that drew them would put this whole floor in the dark, which is the argument for leaving them off.

Give me the answer
