The Den

He did not go to the studio. The studio came into the den.

Green shag on the floor. Carved wood, fake fur, a waterfall of cut fieldstone on the north wall, and a jade lamp on either side of it. In 1976 somebody rolled recording equipment into this room, and the last two Elvis Presley albums came out of it.

The room, and then the records

Graceland has a den that Elvis Presley filled with Polynesian furniture after Blue Hawaii — carved wood, fur over the seats, plants, coloured glass, and an indoor waterfall of cut field stone on the north wall. People have been rude about it for fifty years. It is one of the great rooms.

And then in 1976 it was turned into a recording studio, and the bulk of his final two albums were made in it: From Elvis Presley Boulevard, Memphis, Tennessee, out that May, and Moody Blue, out in July 1977. Felton Jarvis produced. The band was the touring band — James Burton, Ron Tutt, Jerry Scheff, Glen D. Hardin, John Wilkinson, Charlie Hodge, David Briggs — with J.D. Sumner & The Stamps, Kathy Westmoreland and Myrna Smith singing. They played it live in a room with the furniture still in it.

That is the whole reason this is a room and not a link. A shag carpet is a joke; a discography made on top of one is a listening bench. The sixteen cuts below are in the order they were recorded, with the night each one was cut, and none of them is an outtake — the takes are out there on the same official channels and are not what came out on the records.

29–31 October 1976

October 1976

Three nights, eight months later, in the same room. Everything from them went on Moody Blue — the album he did not live to see released.

  • 13

    Written by Layng Martine Jr. · cut 29 October 1976 · Moody Blue

    Released as a single on 6 June 1977 and the last one out in his lifetime. J.D. Sumner goes lower on this than anybody needed him to, which is the point of it.

    Runs 2:38 · on YouTube, via ElvisPresleyVEVO. Nothing of it is hosted here.

  • 14

    Pledging My Love

    Written by Don Robey and Ferdinand Washington · cut 29 October 1976 · Moody Blue

  • 15

    It's Easy for You

    Written by Andrew Lloyd Webber and Tim Rice · cut 29 October 1976 · Moody Blue

    He'll Have to Go

    Written by Joe Allison and Audrey Allison · cut 31 October 1976 · Moody Blue

    Jim Reeves' song, taken very slow indeed. The last thing on this bench, and the last studio session of his life.

House rules, in here

  • Nothing plays until you press play. Every one of those is a button, not a video. Until you press one this page has asked YouTube for nothing on your behalf — no iframe, no cookie, no request. Press one and exactly one becomes a song.
  • Every button says how long it runs. Which is the rule the room you came through cannot follow, because a live camera never ends. These are songs with a length, so withholding it would be keeping back the one fact that lets you decide. The two generators enforce opposite checks and are making the same promise.
  • The vibe, not the lyrics. Same rule as the dancefloor and the chapel. We name the artist, we link the song, and we let them keep their own words. And we name the people who wrote them, because Elvis Presley wrote none of these and a room that credited only the voice would be doing the exact thing this site keeps attribution for.
  • Nothing is hosted here. Not one second of audio. Every button is a facade that points at Presley’s own official channels — the estate’s art-track channel and his VEVO — and the recordings stay where their owners put them.
  • This room is not the room upstairs. Both are called the Jungle Room and both are green, and that is where it stops. Up there the green is the darkness and the light falls from above; down here the room is brown, the carpet is the brightest thing in it, and the list is set dark on the green. Same colour, opposite job.
