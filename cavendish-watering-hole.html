The Watering Hole

The Watering Hole is for open, come-and-go, group Watercooler chat where random and off-topic are on-topic. This is a casual, drop-in space where people come together without any particular agenda.

How it goes here

Watering Hole Norms

  • Video optional.
  • Mute when you’re not actively talking.
  • Quietly lurking with camera and mic off is perfectly fine and accepted.
  • Come-and-go as you please. If you need to drop out suddenly, no need to worry with goodbyes. We understand.

The screen by the water

Somebody usually leaves something on. These are the Jungle Room’s cams, the waterholes in the Namib and at Etosha, the otters and the jellies, read out of that room’s own list, and they are live, so each one runs until you close it and the picture may be darkness, an empty pan or a cam between streams. Play one here, or put it on the screen; each is yours, at your own pace. In the call, anybody can put a YouTube video on for everybody from the call’s own menu, under Share video, and then whoever shares it plays and pauses it for everybody.

The cams: the waterholes, the otters and the jellies

  • Live stream in the Namib Desert

    NamibiaCam, on YouTube

    Desert, a pan of water, and long stretches of nothing at all. Oryx, springbok, jackals, and at night whatever the infrared finds.

  • Okaukuejo waterhole, Etosha National Park

    NamibiaCam, on YouTube

    The floodlit waterhole at Okaukuejo. Elephant and rhino come in after dark and the light stays on all night, which makes this the cam to have up at three in the morning.

  • Sea Otter Cam

    Vancouver Aquarium, on YouTube

    Sea otters in the water, being extremely busy about nothing in particular.

  • Sea Otter Cam 2

    Vancouver Aquarium, on YouTube

    The second angle on the same otters, which is a different room to them and therefore a different cam to us.

  • Live Sea Otter Cam

    Monterey Bay Aquarium, on YouTube

    Monterey's otters. Grooming, mostly. An otter spends an enormous amount of its day on its own coat and is not embarrassed about it.

  • Live Sea Otter Cam (second view)

    Monterey Bay Aquarium, on YouTube

    The alternate Monterey feed. This one is often between streams; when it is, you get the last one instead, which is still otters.

  • Live sea otter and fur seal cam

    Seattle Aquarium, on YouTube

    Otters and fur seals sharing a frame. The fur seals are louder about it.

  • Live Jelly Cam

    Monterey Bay Aquarium, on YouTube

    Moon jellies, lit from behind, drifting in a circular tank. The slowest thing in this room and the one most likely to still be on an hour after you meant to close it.

Talking

The call in here is for people signed on to the CB. Anybody can come in and look around; to talk, and to be in the call, you need the CB, and the Community Center says how to get on it.

You are not signed on to the CB in this browser. Sign on at the Community Center, then come back and the way in is here.

A Note on Communication Access. We support multiple modes of participation including text chat, audio, and video — whatever works for your communication needs on a given day. You do not need to speak to belong here.

The call is run by 8x8’s Jitsi as a Service. Your camera and microphone start off, the name you are called by goes to 8x8 inside your pass into the call, and while somebody is in the call we keep their name in it, until they leave. What goes where.

Why a pan at noon

A watering hole is a pool in a dry place, and whoever turns up. Nobody arranged to meet there; everybody needed water. At noon there is no shade anywhere but what you stand in, so nothing hides and nothing is in a hurry. Come in, drink, stand about, and go when you like. Nothing in this room moves, at any setting.
