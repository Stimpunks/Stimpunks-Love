A cat café

Pekoe and Purrs

Coffee, tea, tea sandwiches, and cats from the shelter, waiting to be adopted.

A café with a window at every height. Somebody cut them all to the size of a cat, from one down at the floor for watching feet go by to a round one up under the eaves, so every cat in here has a window of its own, and every window lays a small square of day on the wall beside it. The light in the room is all of those squares at once. The cats were found out on the street in the dark; in here every one of them can be seen whole, in daylight.

Who is in today

    The cats

    Every cat in here came from Rescue A Cat, the shelter on the street, and every one of them is waiting for a home. The café keeps no list of its own: each time it opens it reads the shelter’s, so a cat adopted this morning has already gone home, and a cat brought in an hour ago is already here, finding somewhere to sit.

    Press a cat to meet them: their name, what they are like, where they were found and when they came in, all in the shelter’s own words. Adopting happens at the shelter. Every cat’s card has a link to that cat’s own card there, where anybody signed on to the CB can adopt them. The café is not told who adopted whom, and does not ask.

    Where a cat goes is up to the cat. The shelter wrote down each cat’s mood when they came in, and that is all the café goes by: a cat who watches everything from the top of the cupboard stays up high, a cat who is only interested in food stays down on the floor and the chairs, where the crumbs are, a cat who went straight for a lap stays near the tables, and a cat who fell asleep before the door shut finds somewhere soft. Three legs, one eye, a kink in the tail: none of it decides where a cat may go. A cat with three legs gets to the top of the tree like anybody else.

    You can pick a cat up and put them on your lap, unless they want to be left alone, which is allowed. The shelter wrote that down too, and the café keeps to it: those cats have no button for picking them up. Sit near them instead. One cat to a lap: picking up another means the first hops down and goes somewhere they like. Look down and you see your lap under the café’s blanket, which it lends to anybody with a cat on them, because claws, and your table with whatever you asked for from the menu.

    At MAX GLITTER the cats wander about by themselves, now and then, one at a time, the way cats do. At Gentle and Regular they stay where they are until something moves them: wave the feather wand, roll the crinkle ball or put down a box, and whoever is interested goes after it. A cat who is asleep, or wants to be left alone, is not interested, which is allowed as well. The words say who went where at every setting; only the moving changes.

    The telly

    A telly hangs by the hatch, and the rack under it holds films from other cat cafés, most of them in Japan, picked by Ryan, in his order. Each one plays where it hangs, or goes up on the telly with the button under it. Nothing loads from YouTube until you press play, and every button says how long the film runs before you press it. The titles are the channels’ own, as they wrote them; none of the channels is ours or affiliated with Stimpunks.

    The menu

    Every item on the board says what is in it, whether it is vegan or vegetarian or which animal it came from, and which of the nine major food allergens the FDA names it has, and gluten. Every drink says how much caffeine it has, in words: decaf is never quite none, chocolate carries some, and a tisane brewed from flowers or leaves that are not tea has none. The house milk is oat, so every milk drink is vegan as it comes, and soy or dairy is there if you ask.

    Everything is made in one kitchen, behind the hatch, so if an allergy is serious, say so there, and somebody will tell you what has been near what. Nothing here is certified halal or kosher; the two sandwiches with meat in them say which animal. And every cup, mug and pot in here is the same teal mug, because the regulars knock things off tables, and the good china would not last an afternoon.

    House rules

    • Let the cats come to you. A cat who walks away has said no, and that is the end of it.
    • A cat who would rather not be picked up is not picked up. The shelter wrote it down, and the café has no button for it.
    • No cat sits on the hatch, and none goes into the kitchen. The food is made behind the wall, because cats and kitchens do not mix.
    • Nothing in here makes a sound. The purring is the cats’ business.
    • Nobody is counted: not the cats, not the cups, and not you.

    What the café keeps

    • Nothing about you. Your lap and your table live in this page and nowhere else, and are gone when you leave it. The café’s own script stores nothing in your browser and sends nothing anywhere: it reads the shelter’s list when it opens, and that is the only thing it asks for. A film on the telly loads from YouTube only when you press it.
    • No list of cats of its own. Who is in the café is who is in the shelter, read fresh every time.
    • Nothing about who adopted whom. That is between the shelter and the person, and the shelter keeps it under a scrambled name.
    • Every item says what is in it before you ask for it, and every drink how much caffeine.
    • Nothing moves by itself except at MAX GLITTER, and nothing flashes at any setting. Nothing plays until you press play, and every film says how long it runs first.
    • Every mug is the same mug.

    Whose it is

    The brief is Ryan Boren’s, of 2026-10-04, and so is the name. The cats are Rescue A Cat’s, and they are drawn by the same file that draws them in the shelter and in the Profile of whoever adopts them, so a cat here looks exactly as it does there. What orange pekoe is was read on Wikipedia’s article on tea leaf grading, and what carries caffeine on the FDA’s page about it and Wikipedia’s on herbal tea, each on 2026-10-04; the allergen line is worked out by the same engine as Hey, Good Cookin’. The menu and every other word in the room were written for it. Set in Cherry Bomb One, by Satsuyako, and Gabarito, by Naipe Foundry (Leandro Assis, Álvaro Franca and Felipe Casaprima), both under the SIL Open Font License. Every source is in the liner notes.
