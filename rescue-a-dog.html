        How rescuing and adopting work

        • Dogs turn up at random, one at a time, and a dog waits however long it takes somebody to come for them. Not even the kennels know when the next one will be.
        • The first person signed on to the CB to press brings them in. Anybody a moment later is told they are already safe. Looking takes nothing.
        • A dog stays in the kennels until somebody adopts them. When the kennels are full, the next dog waits outside until somebody adopts one.
        • Adopting is first to press too, and a dog is adopted once. They go into Profile on your CB radio, which follows you to any device you sign on with, and onto the list of everybody adopted, for good. That list never says who adopted anybody.
        • Your pets are kept under your handle, scrambled so the store does not list handles. Until you claim your username from Profile on your radio, a handle is not an account, and anybody who signs on with the same handle sees the same pets; once it is claimed, only your password reaches them. Forget Me in Profile deletes them; the dogs stay on the list of everybody adopted, with nothing connecting them to you. What the shelters keep.
        • No dog is rarer than another. Every coat, every marking and every place is as likely as any other, so no dog is a prize and none is a disappointment. Some have three legs or one eye, and they are dogs.
        • Nothing says who rescued which dog, anywhere on the street, and nobody is counted: no totals, no board of rescuers, no streaks. The ones you rescued are kept in your own browser only.
        • The base station can take a name off a dog, wherever the dog is, if the name should not be on show.
        • Nothing moves, at any setting. Every dog is drawn sitting still on the snow, tail up.
