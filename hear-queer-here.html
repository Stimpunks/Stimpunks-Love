THE READINGS

Selected passages on monotropism, written for this room and read by Ryan Boren. Not the glossary entry itself — most of that belongs to other people, and it stays with their names on it. 2 of 5 recorded.

What monotropism is

Monotropism is a theory of Autistic attention, made by Autistic people — Dinah Murray, Wenn Lawson and Mike Lesser. The short version: attention is a resource, and some of us spend it deeply on a few things rather than thinly across many. That is the whole claim. Most of what gets said about us follows from it.

The A side: what the instrument reports

This is the A side. What follows is not our description. It is the one the clinic writes down. Restricted interests. Preoccupation. Poor attention switching. Rigidity. Resistance to transition. Failure to respond to name. Every one of those is a real observation. Every one is also a measurement, reported as though it were a property of the person rather than a property of the instrument.

The B side: the same wall

This is the B side. Same person, same afternoon, different instrument. Attention pulled hard toward a few things, with not much left over for the rest. Interruption is expensive, because leaving a tunnel costs more than staying in one. The name went unanswered because it arrived from outside the tunnel — not because it did not matter. Nothing in that list is a deficit. It is a description of where the attention went.

WHY THIS IS NOT A METAPHOR

Quantum queering is not one field borrowing a little glamour from another. Barad makes the strong version of the claim: the queerness is not ours, projected onto matter. It is matter’s own. If queerness is in the nature of spacetimemattering, then it is not a deviation from the natural order — it is the natural order, and “acts against nature” becomes an incoherent charge.

Barad also names the reaction: panicked attempts to contain, tame, or normalize what will not stay contained. We know that reflex. It is the same one that built the behaviorist clinic and the compliance classroom, one scale down.
