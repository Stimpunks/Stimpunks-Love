The Fishbowl

A room for community Fishbowl Meetings for anyone with CB access.

How a fishbowl goes

A fishbowl is a conversation for a lot of people that only a few hold at a time. A small circle in the middle talks. Everybody else sits in a wider circle round the outside and listens. One seat in the middle is always left empty, and anybody from the outside can come in and take it; when they do, somebody in the middle gets up and goes back out, so there is always a seat free. Nobody is called on, nobody is on a panel, and the circle is never closed.

It suits a big group with one thing to talk through, because it keeps the talking small and the listening wide. James Carrier’s introduction at PeopleShift is where this room started, and it also says that some people find a format this loose and this level a little daunting. That is a fair thing to feel, and the outer circle is there for it.

How it goes here

  • The middle is whoever has their microphone on. Everybody else is the outer circle, and listening there is a whole way to take part.
  • There is always an empty seat. To come in, raise your hand in the call or say so in the chat, then unmute when there is room.
  • When somebody comes in, somebody goes out. Stepping back is a thing you choose, and nobody needs a reason for it.
  • Typing is talking. Something written in the call’s chat can be read into the middle, if you ask; you do not have to speak to be in the circle.
  • Nobody is put on the spot. Nobody has to come into the middle, and nobody is asked to.

The circle

The call in here is for people signed on to the CB. Anybody can come in and look around; to talk, and to be in the circle, you need the CB, and the Community Center says how to get on it.

Why a bowl on a sill

Because it is a fishbowl: a small round world you can see all the way into from outside, with a ring of stones on the gravel that always has one gap in it and a wider ring of shells round the foot of the glass. The sun comes through the water and lands on the gravel and the sill in bright tangled lines, and that is all the light the room needs. Nothing in here moves, at any setting.
