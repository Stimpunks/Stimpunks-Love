The Adventurer’s Guild

Job board Hints at every job No score kept Rooms: all of them

Somebody has come onto this street and filed everything. Ruled boxes, square corners, a class in the margin, a stamp for a job that is done. It is the only tidy room here and it is tidy the way a form is tidy, which on a street whose whole architecture is that nothing matches is the strangest thing anybody could have built.

Every job below sends you to a marker standing in one of the rooms — a small drawing of something lying about in there, drawn in that room’s own colours, because no two rooms on this street look alike and neither do their markers. Open the marker and it gives you a quest code. Bring the code back here.

Hand in a quest code

Spaces and capitals do not matter. If you followed a link from a marker the code is already in the box — press the button when you are ready, because pressing it is the only thing that makes a noise.

Your browser is not letting this page store anything, so stamps will not survive you leaving. Everything still works; it just will not be here when you come back. Nothing about this is kept anywhere else unless you switch on keeping your jobs in your Profile on the CB.

This deletes the jobs kept in your Profile on the CB, and whether they are shown there. The stamps in this browser stay where they are.

House rules

  • Nothing here is scored. No total, no percentage, no streak, no rank you climb, and nothing that expires. The board stamps a job when you hand its code in, a badge is worked out from the stamps, and that is the entire bookkeeping. One job is a perfectly good number of jobs to do.
  • A badge is a set of things done that go together, and nothing else. Most are jobs; two are the shelters’, for rescuing an animal and for adopting one. Each is worked out from what you have already done, every time, and kept nowhere of its own. No badge is rarer or worth more than another, none is secret, none ever asks for more than it did when it was posted, so none can be taken away, and nothing counts how many you have. It certifies nothing: every code is printed in its room and every question gives its answer away, so a badge says where you have walked, and that is all it says.
  • Your stamps stay in your browser unless you ask. Signed on to the CB, you can switch on Keep my jobs in my Profile, by the box above. Then the jobs you hand in are kept there as well, which jobs and nothing about when, they follow you to any device you sign on with, and you can choose to show them, and your badges, to people on the CB. Switching it off deletes them there. Privacy has the rest.
  • The difficulty class is how far you walk. I is on the street, II is through a door off another room, III is off the street altogether — the field past the treeline at one end and the land past its wood, the road past the last streetlight at the other, or the caverns underneath. It says nothing whatever about the person doing it, which is why it is measured in doors.
  • Every job has hints, and some have a question. A question is answered by a sentence already published on the page you are standing on — that is the point of asking. The hints are at the job, the question’s own hints are at the marker, and every question has a “give me the answer” way out. Taking it is a real way through this and not a lesser one.
  • The fanfare does what the dial says, and the words never change. At Gentle handing a code in says its piece and does nothing else. At Regular it chimes. At MAX GLITTER it chimes properly and throws sparks up the page. You get the same sentences at all three.
  • Nothing in here flashes or strobes, at any setting. That is Club Chronic’s rule, and it holds everywhere on this street except in one room: the Rebellion Rave Room has a strobe, and it is off until you switch it on and say yes.
  • The markers are not hidden. They are quiet, which is a different thing: every one is a full-size control in the tab order with a real name on it, and every one opens without JavaScript. A secret only a sighted mouse user can find is not a secret.

Where badges come from, and what we left behind
  • Scouting America’s merit badges are a patch for a list of requirements in one subject. We kept the list, printed in full where you can read it before you start. We left anybody signing it off, because nobody here checks anything.
  • Open Badges is a format for digital badges that its own site calls verifiable and portable: a badge you can take elsewhere and have checked. We left all of that. These do not leave the street, and there is nothing in them to check.
  • Video game achievements are goals laid over a game, shown on a player’s profile; Wikipedia traces them to the iron-on patches Activision posted in 1982 to players who photographed a high score. We kept the fun of hunting them and the profile, if you choose to show them. We left the score they add up to, the rarity and the secret ones.
