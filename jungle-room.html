The Jungle Room

Drop in. Space out. Belong here.

A room with the cameras on and nobody presenting. Every screen in here is somebody else’s camera pointed at somewhere real, and every one of them is off until you open it.

Why a room is just a lot of screens

Every weekday our Watering Hole Hangs puts a nature cam up in the background and leaves it there. No slides, no presenter, no outcomes — people turn up, do their own thing near each other, and let the otters carry the social weight. That is body doubling, and it is one of the most underrated things neurodivergent people do for one another.

A watering hole is the third of our three gathering modes: not the cave you go into alone, not the campfire gathered around a story, but the in-between place where community actually forms because nobody scheduled it to. Co-regulation happens there, quietly, and a bodymind settles in the presence of other bodyminds even at this distance. The cam is doing some of that work too. Something about water, movement, and animals going about their quiet lives — it earns its place in the background.

This room is the cams, and only the cams. The groups below, their order, and the order inside each of them are lifted from that events page exactly as they stand there — it is a list somebody keeps, not one for this street to re-sort. The train cams on it are not here: a cab view down a Norwegian line is a fine thing to hang about in and it is not a watering hole. Everything else is. The galleries are built from data/jungle.json by tools/make-jungle.py.

What the room is

  • Nothing plays until you press play. Every screen above is a button. Until you press one, this page has asked YouTube for nothing on your behalf — no iframe, no cookie, no request, no pixel. Press one and exactly one becomes a video; the rest stay buttons.
  • Nothing here says how long it runs, because none of it ends. Every other press-to-play control on this street carries a runtime, and that is the promise that lets you decide before you press. A live camera has no runtime to carry. Putting the length of whichever stream happens to be up today on the page would be a number that is wrong tomorrow, so the label says the true thing instead: it runs until you close it.
  • The picture is whatever is happening there right now. Which is sometimes darkness, sometimes rain, sometimes an empty waterhole, and sometimes a cam between streams showing you the last one. None of that is a fault. A camera pointed at somewhere real is a camera that is often pointed at nothing, and sitting with nothing happening is most of what this room is for.
  • Nothing is hosted here. Not one frame. Every camera belongs to the people who put it up — aquariums, sanctuaries, research institutes, reserves, and a few people with a feeder and a good angle — and every screen says whose it is on its own face rather than filing it away in the credits.
  • Nothing is counted. No minutes watched, no cams visited, no streak, nothing that notices you were here. This is a room for being near something alive, and a tally beside that would turn it into an activity.
  • Gentle takes away the wobble, never the words. There is no content behind an intensity level anywhere on this street. At MAX the light shifts through the leaves and the dot beside the word LIVE breathes; that is the whole of it. Turn the dial down and you lose two flickers and not one camera.

Every cam our events page lists is playing. Three of them open on YouTube rather than in a screen here, because their owners have embedding switched off — those are doors rather than windows, and they say so on the way out.

Whose cameras these are

Every one of them is somebody else’s, and none of them made anything for this site — they are credited here as the people who put a camera somewhere and left it running, which is what a facade is for. The names and the channels were resolved against YouTube itself rather than typed off our own link text, because the one careful habit this street kept is attribution.
