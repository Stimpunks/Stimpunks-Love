What changed on the street, and when. Every Stimpunks site publishes one of these.

Subscribe to this page — the feed is generated from it, so the two cannot disagree.

2026-09-20 — the third photograph, and the promise it broke on arrival

  • Helen sent her portrait, for both places at once. It is on Enid’s wall and it is the picture beside the welcome in her own room — the empty slot the yurt shipped with two commits ago, because the file was not ours to invent. Description, caption and naming are hers, and she took it herself. No EXIF, not resized, not cropped.
  • And not filtered, which is the part worth writing down. Her mockup put a sepia wash over this photograph — filter: sepia(.18) saturate(1.05) brightness(.96) — and the terms promise, in these words, that we will not “Crop you. Filter you.” A promise the site makes to everybody is not waived by the one person it happens to be about this time, because the next photograph would arrive under a rule that had already been bent once. The wooden frame is matting and stays: a border, a shadow or a mat goes around a picture; a filter is done to it.
  • So the tool checks it rather than trusting anybody to remember. make-polaroids.py now refuses a filter in an <img>’s own style attribute, and refuses any rule in love.css whose selector reaches a photograph and carries one. Every photograph on the site wears class="photo" so there is something exact to look for.
  • The first version of that check did not work, and the way it failed is the point. It scanned only the classes on the <img> itself, so it sailed straight past .portrait-frame img { filter: … } — the filter was on a descendant selector, which is the shape this actually arrives in. It was written, it passed, and it was wrong; it only got caught because putting the sepia back and watching the tool say nothing is cheaper than believing it. A checker that passes a case it was built for is worse than no checker, and this one nearly shipped as exactly that. All three shapes refuse now — descendant selector, the plate’s own class, and an inline style — each one tested by breaking it on purpose.

2026-09-20 — the street grows an edge, and Helen pitches a yurt on it

  • There is a signpost at the end of the shopfronts now. The Campgrounds — a field past the treeline with numbered pitches on it, for the people who do not want a storefront. A row of lit shops is a wonderful place to be and an exhausting place to live, and a site that only had one of those was telling its own quiet people that the loud room is the only room. The signpost is deliberately not a seventh door: a door here is a shop you walk into, and this is where the street stops.
  • Nothing in the field moves at any setting — not at Gentle, not at Regular, not at MAX GLITTER. Every other page has an ambient layer waiting for the dial to be turned up; this one has none, and says so on the page. An area that exists for people who want quiet cannot have a loud setting and mean it. The dial still governs each pitch, which are their own worlds and set their own noise.
  • Pitch 01 is the Faery Yurt, and it is Helen Edgar’s room rather than ours. The palette, the three typefaces, the drawings, the twelve book spines, the shutters on the glimmer window and every line of copy came from a mockup she built and sent. We added the plumbing and changed one colour. It is credited to her in the liner notes, in a section that did not exist until there was a room by somebody else to put in it.
  • The yurt shares nothing with the field it is pitched on, and that was the hardest version of this rule yet. The Chappell at least had a parent room to differ from; this is the first thing in a brand new area, where “an area should look like an area” is the most reasonable-sounding sentence the tidying instinct has produced here since “it is only a subroom”. The field is cold blue spruce, bone signs routed like park lettering, and bare ground. The yurt is candlelit brown, old serifs and soft cards. You walk in out of the night air; the contrast is the door. They got a share card each, ninth and tenth, for the same reason.
  • The contrast checker caught two things in Helen’s mockup before it shipped. Her smallest grey, #8a7462, carries the eyebrow, the shelf note, the window hint, the animal cards and the footer — and it measures 4.30 on the flat tent ground, 3.85 on a card and 3.43 under the ember glow, failing everywhere it is used. It is #A4907B now. Her fourth book spine ran to #9a6c2f, where the cream lettering measured 3.74; it stops at #8A5F26. Thirty-nine new pairs, including one against #31231A — a colour nobody wrote, which is what the yurt’s canvas weave and ember crown actually composite to. 126 pairs, none failing. This is the only thing about her room this site changed, and it is written down rather than quietly applied.
  • The consent record now guards every page, not just the wall. The yurt hangs one of Enid’s photographs in Helen’s own room, which put the consent record and the publication in two different places for the first time — exactly the gap where a withdrawal gets half-honoured. make-polaroids.py walks all the HTML now: a photo published anywhere without an entry is a refusal, a photo hung outside the wall without that entry saying so is a refusal, and a file sitting in photos/ with no record at all is a refusal. Delete a withdrawn entry and the tool refuses to run until the yurt lets go of it too, instead of leaving a broken image on a page nobody thought to check.
  • Five typefaces arrived at once, which is what it costs to open an area whose two pages share nothing with the street or with each other. Alfa Slab One and Bitter are the campground’s routed sign and its field notice; Cormorant Garamond, Crimson Pro and Caveat are Helen’s, named in her own mockup. All five are self-hosted, so the yurt still makes no third-party request — verified in the network panel, along with the field: every byte on both pages comes from this origin.
  • A keyboard fix the mockup could not have caught. The glimmer window’s shutters are opened by a visually hidden checkbox, so the site’s focus ring was landing on something invisible and a keyboard visitor had no way to tell they were on it. The ring goes on the window frame now.

2026-09-20 — the second photograph

  • Enid’s wall has somebody else on it. Helen Edgar, on a grand staircase — the first photograph up there from somebody other than the person who built the room, which is the difference between a process and a person waving his own picture through. The description and the caption are the words it arrived with, because the terms do not allow any other kind.
  • The photographer is credited without being named. A friend took it and would rather stay anonymous, and the permission is on the record anyway — the tool refuses a credited photographer with no permission logged, and anonymous is a way of being named rather than a way of skipping the question. Being in a picture is not the same as owning it.
  • Checked before publishing, not promised: no EXIF, so no location data left on the file; the photograph keeps its own shape, because the plate stopped cropping when the first one went up; and it carries no onward licence, so it comes down the day she says so and nobody else gets to reuse it in the meantime.

2026-09-20 — a door at the back of the dancefloor

  • Pink Pony Club has a subroom. The Chappell — thirteen Chappell Roan videos under the heading Unabashed Pleasure and Righteous Inclusion, which is the name of a playlist we already published. The room argues that the two halves of that title are usually kept apart, that Disabled people get handed the righteous one and told the other is a reward for good behaviour, and that unabashed pleasure is not the opposite of inclusion but what inclusion is for.
  • Rhinestone Vatican, and it shares nothing with its parent. The dancefloor is flat hot pink, cream Shrikhand and ten small white cards inside one big cream tray. This is votive indigo, gold Monoton, and thirteen free-standing arches with stained glass over each one, standing on the bare ground. A subroom is the place where inheriting the parent’s look would have seemed most reasonable, which is exactly why it does not — and it got its own share card for the same reason, the eighth.
  • Monoton was the last unclaimed display face on the street. It held one 27px line on the index, “Living out loud”, and no room had taken it as a headline. A neon marquee face set in gold leaf is the whole joke of this room in one typeface.
  • Every button says how long before you press it. There is a twenty-three minute Tiny Desk concert in there sitting beside three-minute videos, and “press play” is only consent if you know what you are agreeing to — the same reasoning that put the passage count on the audio room’s sequence control. The generator refuses a track with no runtime rather than rendering a blank one, because a missing runtime would not look like a bug, it would look like a choice.
  • Nothing was guessed. Ids came out of the playlist page’s own data and were cross-checked against the playlist panel on the watch page; titles, channels and runtimes were resolved against YouTube; every id was then put through the same playability check that caught the dead track on the dancefloor, and all thirteen answered OK. Guessing which song goes with which video id is the exact fabrication the zine table spends a page arguing against.
  • The playlist says fifteen and thirteen render. YouTube counts private and deleted entries in that total and shows nothing at all for them to anybody but the owner, so the missing two cannot be read from outside. Thirteen is what plays and thirteen is what is published — written down in data/chappell.json rather than quietly rounded off.
  • The rot checker now watches both lists. It only knew about the dancefloor’s ten, and would have left thirteen facades unwatched while printing “all tracks checked” in a tone of complete confidence. That is worse than not running it, because it reads like coverage.
  • The contrast checker learned this room’s ambient layer before it shipped, not after. Thirteen new pairs, and two of them are against #4E2E60 — a colour nobody wrote, which is what the rose window actually makes over the nave. The gold headline measures 11.21 flat and 6.73 at the brightest point of the glow. The pony’s cream is 3.01 and 2.64 in the same comparison, which is why its card leaves the rays off and this one keeps them. Eighty-seven pairs now, none failing.

2026-09-19 — seven share cards, because one would have been a design system

  • Every page has an image now when somebody pastes its link, and there are seven card designs for eleven pages — one per room, allowed to contradict each other exactly as the rooms do. The street’s collides six typefaces; the zine’s is a ransom note on photocopier grain; Hear Queer Here is a terminal face over interference fringes; the Playhouse keeps its bunting and its checkerboard floor. A single template would have been the tidying instinct arriving in the one asset nobody reviews, because nobody opens a PNG in a diff.
  • The cards are built with the site’s own stylesheet rather than a copy of it. Each one is a scrap of markup on the page’s own body class with love.css attached, and the h1 is lifted from the page verbatim — which is how the zine’s card keeps its per-word ransom spans and the street’s keeps its two-part wordmark. Change a room and its card changes with it.
  • No card ships without alt text. The tool has no way to write an og:image without an og:image:alt, because words baked into a picture are words nobody can hear, on a site that exists to say so.
  • “It fits” is measured, not eyeballed. Chrome reports the layout back out of the same run that takes the screenshot, and a card whose content runs past 1200×630 fails the build. A slightly longer title is enough to push a word off the edge, and the crop lands in somebody else’s timeline looking like our bug.
  • It found contrast nobody had measured. The checker held 66 named pairs and every one of them assumed a flat background; the rooms have ambient glows over those backgrounds. Enid’s aside grey is 4.55:1 on the black wall and 3.22 in the corner where the green half of the glow peaks — the card’s footer uses a lighter grey, and the room has the same line in its other corner at 3.39.
  • And one we have not fixed, on purpose. The Pink Pony Club’s cream headline measures 3.01:1 on flat hot pink — clearing the 3:1 large-text threshold by four thousandths — and 2.64 over the room’s own mirrorball rays, where it fails. How that room looks is not a checker’s call to make, so it is written down at the foot of check-contrast.py rather than quietly patched, and the pony card leaves the rays off until it is decided.

2026-09-19 — the yell button yells, and takes yours

  • It was blooping. It played the same oscillator as the stim box, an octave and a half down, and a pure tone is a bloop however low you put it. It builds the noise now — breath through a filter that sweeps as a shout runs out of air. Measured: zero-crossings went from 158 a second to 1,747, which is the difference between a tone and a shout.
  • It can be your voice. Send us a yell and it joins the others, one chosen at random on each press, with the room saying whose it was out loud. The synth stays as the fallback, so the toy works whether or not anybody has sent one.
  • The house rule rewrites itself. The page said the noises are synthesised and not fetched from anywhere, which stopped being true the moment a recording landed. That sentence is generated from the same data as the button now, because a claim the site makes about itself should not depend on somebody remembering to edit it.
  • A correction we owed the readings. Enid’s wall refuses a photograph carrying EXIF; audio was never held to the same rule. The first yell arrived with the recording device, its OS build and the exact second in it — and so did all five readings already published. All six are stripped, re-muxed rather than re-encoded so the sound is untouched, and both audio tools now refuse a file that still carries any.

2026-09-19 — Chairy learns twenty-three more things to say

  • Five sayings became twenty-eight, drawn from our philosophy, framing and creed pages. They live in data/chairy.json now rather than an HTML attribute, because an attribute cannot record where a line came from.
  • Two of them are not ours, and Chairy says so out loud. “Nothing About Us Without Us” is a motto of the self-advocacy movement — credited on our own page to Michael Masutha and William Rowland of Disabled People South Africa, 1993 — and “Design for Real Life” is a book by Eric A. Meyer and Sara Wachter-Boettcher. A talking chair passing a movement slogan off as a house line would be the exact thing this site argues against, so the credit is spoken rather than filed.
  • Every source URL was read from the mirror’s own frontmatter, not constructed from a slug — the same rule the jukebox ids follow.

2026-09-19 — the first photograph

  • Enid’s wall has a picture on it. Ryan, a tankard, and a description and caption in his own words — which is the process working rather than being waved through by the person who wrote it.
  • It exposed a promise the code was breaking. The polaroid plate was square with object-fit: cover, and cover crops — while the terms promise in as many words that we will not crop anybody. Nobody caught it while the wall was grey, because there was nothing in the frame to cut. A photograph keeps its own shape now.
  • Checked before publishing, not promised: no EXIF, so no location data; resized for the web but not cropped and not filtered; the alt text is the subject’s own sentence, not our description of him.

2026-09-19 — a second term, and a second voice

  • Hear Queer Here holds two terms now. Developmental Pace joins Monotropism, five passages each. The entry itself makes the case for the pairing: monotropic time is what happens to time inside a flow state, and developmental pace is the longer arc — when a capacity arrives at all.
  • The panel now measures a term as well as a pattern. With one term, “collapse to A” was unambiguous; with two it is not. There is a picker above the collapse row, which keeps the argument rather than working around it — the instrument and the object both matter, and you choose both.
  • These passages are cut from our own entry rather than written fresh. Monotropism’s entry is mostly other people’s work and exactly one line in it was ours, so its passages had to be written. Developmental Pace is substantially our own prose, so it could be read as the room originally promised. The rule is the same either way: we read our own words and nobody else’s.
  • A borrowed voice is not ours to licence. LICENSE said nothing about recordings. Ours are our work; a reading by anybody else carries no onward licence, because CC BY-SA cannot be revoked and somebody who lends us their voice has to be able to take it back. Fixed before the recording exists rather than after — the first time on this site that has gone in that order.
  • Five slots are waiting on a voice, credited in the future tense until they are filled.

2026-09-19 — the panel plays the sequence

  • All five passages are recorded. What monotropism is, the A side, the B side, both are measurements, and a love language — read by Ryan Boren, one minute thirteen seconds end to end. The room stops promising audio and starts having some, and the Not built yet list below is one item shorter.
  • Collapsing to a reading now lets you hear it. The panel only highlighted passages before, which set an expectation it did not keep. A plays what monotropism is and then how the clinic writes it down; B plays it and then what it looks like from inside; leaving it open plays the whole argument, ending on the line that resolves it.
  • One press, several passages — and the label says so first. “Play the A sequence — 2 passages, 39 seconds”, not a bare triangle. Starting a sequence you asked for is still press-to-play; getting four minutes you did not expect would not be.
  • The collapse buttons still make no sound. Choosing a measurement and listening to one are separate acts, which also means you can collapse to A and read it somewhere you cannot play audio.
  • Missing recordings are skipped and counted, so the control works at two of five exactly as it will at five — and the passage playing is marked on the page and announced for screen readers, with a stop always available.

2026-09-19 — the first passage is recorded

  • “What monotropism is” has a voice. Eighteen seconds, read by Ryan Boren. The room that has been promising audio since launch now contains some. Four passages to go, and the words for all five are on the page either way.
  • The generator would have ignored it. It looked for .mp3 and the recording is .m4a — what a Mac produces by default — so the first real recording of this project would have been silently skipped. It now accepts whatever a recorder gives you: .m4a, .mp3, .opus, .ogg, .wav, .aac, .flac. A tool that quietly ignores the file it was built for is worse than one that refuses.

2026-09-19 — the polaroid wall gets a consent process

  • The licence would have swept up your face. This site is CC BY-SA 4.0 and LICENSE excluded the typefaces, the songs and the borrowed names — but said nothing about photographs. A community photo published under that licence would have let strangers reuse a Disabled person’s picture commercially, and CC licences cannot be revoked. Photographs now carry no onward licence at all, which is the only thing that makes “it comes down when you say so” true.
  • The terms are written down — what we ask for, what we promise, and how a photo comes back off the wall. You write the caption and the description; your name is however you write it, including not at all.
  • The promises are enforced rather than intended. tools/make-polaroids.py refuses a photo with no alt text, no named subject or no consent date — and refuses any file still carrying EXIF, which is where a phone records where and when a picture was taken. Sending us a photo should not tell the internet where you live.
  • We do not publish photographs of children. A parent can give the consent; the child cannot take it back in ten years when it matters to them.
  • The wall is no longer grey. Ryan sent the first photograph, of himself, with the description and the caption in his own words. One photo is a wall that has started.

2026-09-19 — the audio room, built and waiting on a voice

  • Hear Queer Here stops promising something it does not do. It said “the glossary read aloud, in our own voices” and delivered nothing. It now holds five written passages on monotropism, each shown as text, each with a visibly empty slot where its player will be. A passage goes live the moment its audio file exists.
  • The prose is new, and that was forced by attribution. The glossary entry is mostly curation — the explanation in it belongs to Dinah Murray, Wenn Lawson and Mike Lesser, to the Monotropism Questionnaire’s authors, and to Helen Edgar, whose opening is hers. Exactly one line in it was ours. So the passages were written for this room rather than lifted, and nothing recorded here is anyone else’s to clear.
  • The superposition panel finally has a subject. It was a mechanism with no content: A and B were not readings of anything. Three of the passages now answer to it — the clinic’s description, the monotropic one, and both at once. Its old caption said “neither one wrong”, which asserted a symmetry we do not hold; it now says that only one of the two knows it is a measurement.
  • The words are the point, not the transcript. Every passage is printed whether or not the audio exists. An argument you can only follow by hearing it is an audio-only requirement, and this is a Disabled people’s organisation.

2026-09-19 — the changelog has a feed

  • There is a feed now, and it is generated. The front door briefly linked /feed.xml before the file existed and the link was removed rather than the file invented. tools/make-feed.py builds it from this page, the way the sitemap is built from the pages’ own heads, so a changelog entry and its feed item cannot drift apart.
  • Every entry here now has a stable anchor, because that anchor is the feed item’s permalink. The generator refuses to run if one is missing — a feed whose guids move republishes every old entry into somebody’s reader as though it were new.

2026-09-19 — the zine prints in black and white, for real this time

  • The second half of the room’s own sentence was false. “Prints to one sheet on a bad office printer, in black and white” — the ransom headline printed in full yellow, cyan and red, and the print rules reset backgrounds only on a fixed list of components. Anything not on that list kept its dark ground while the text on it was forced to black: 1.14:1, invisible. What actually vanished on paper was the most prominent quotation on each sheet and the entire credits box, on the page that argues attribution is the cheapest thing you can do.
  • Colour and text are now reset together, universally. Fixing either alone breaks the other — pale citation text on a newly-white ground would have been just as unreadable the other way round.
  • check-print.py now checks both halves, reading the inks out of the rendered PDF against an allowlist rather than a threshold, so a new colour on the page has to be looked at by a person instead of quietly tolerated.

2026-09-19 — a dead track on the dancefloor

  • Bad Cop / Bad Cop, “Warriors”, has a new id. The id carried over from our Double Rainbow page had started returning “Video unavailable” inside the embed. Replaced with one that plays, resolved against oEmbed like every other entry, with the old id and the reason kept in data/jukebox.json rather than quietly overwritten.
  • Two channel names now read the way a person would write them. The credits said “via Merge Records on YouTube”, which is genuinely what that channel is called now, and “via Bad Cop Bad Cop - Topic”, which is an auto-generated name missing the slashes the band uses. Both print properly now. YouTube’s exact wording is kept beside them in data/jukebox.json so the checker can still tell a real rename from a choice we made.
  • The other nine were checked too, and all nine play. Worth saying plainly how, because two obvious tests are useless here: YouTube’s oEmbed endpoint answers 200 for a video that will not play, and loading an embed URL directly fails identically for every video, working or not. What distinguishes them is the watch page’s own playabilityStatus, which read UNPLAYABLE for the dead one and OK for the rest.

2026-09-19 — the zine table takes issues

The Zine Table stops being one sheet and becomes a table with sheets on it.

  • Issue #2, One Root, Two Rainbows — seven lessons from our own Neurodiversity and Gender course cut down to seven lines, using the course’s own one-idea-per-line device. Seven glossary terms linked, each one checked before it was written.
  • Submissions have a route now. The room asked for pages by paper, photo, voice note and AAC, and the link under the ask pointed at Your Room — which is a whole storefront, a different and much larger offer. The zine table now carries the spec and the contact route itself, and names Your Room as the separate thing it is.
  • Correction: the one-sheet promise was not true. The room says every page in it prints to one sheet and that the print stylesheet is not a joke. Rendered to PDF, issue #1 came out at three sheets — the two columns wrapped into one long one and every link expanded to its full URL after it. Fixed with print rules scoped to this room and a declared page margin, so it no longer depends on the print dialog. Both issues now land inside one sheet, and tools/check-print.py renders them through headless Chrome on every run so the claim cannot quietly come apart again.
  • The rule on quoting songs changed. This site launched saying it would reproduce no lyric at all. That was never argued for on its own terms, and queering.earth had already settled it properly: a line or two, never the chorus alone, the songwriter named separately from the performer. The deciding argument is an access one — “go and listen to the recording” is an audio-only requirement. We follow that policy by reference now rather than restating it.

2026-09-19 — the street opened

Six rooms, one front door, and a dial. Live the same day at stimpunks.love, with the source public at github.com/Stimpunks/Stimpunks-Love.

  • The Stoop — the front door, six shopfronts, and the tagline set in six typefaces because that is the thesis rather than the decoration.
  • Pink Pony Club — ten tracks lifted from our own Double Rainbow page, each one a press-to-play facade that contacts YouTube only after you press it.
  • The Zine Table — xerox and ransom note, and the page that says which two habits survived the move: attribution and contrast.
  • Hear Queer Here — quantum queering, with Barad and Helen Edgar quoted and cited, and a superposition panel where “leave it open” is a real answer.
  • Enid’s Room — colorful goth, a sticker wall wired to seven glossary entries, and somewhere to sit when the glee runs out.
  • The Playhouse — four toys, all real buttons, all synthesised rather than fetched, all reachable by keyboard.
  • Your Room — the sixth storefront, left empty on purpose, with the terms written down.
  • The intensity dial — three settings on every page, defaulting to whatever your device asks for and letting you turn it up past that. Gentle takes away the wobble, never the words.
  • Twelve typefaces self-hosted, so reading a page here makes no third-party request.

Not built yet

Enid’s polaroid wall has two photographs and room for more. Issue #2 of the zine has a quotation slot shaped for the last line of the course and nothing in it, because the credit that line needs is not something we will guess. All of them are listed here rather than hidden, because a room that says what it is missing is more use than one that pretends.
