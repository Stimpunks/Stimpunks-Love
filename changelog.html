2026-09-22 — The decks on the dancefloor, and a playlist that turned out to be broken at the top

Diagnosed with a control rather than guessed. The plain embed failed twice; the same playlist started at its first visible video played; and the Neuroqueer playlist, framed identically, played. Its page said thirteen unavailable videos were hidden — the page hides them and the embed does not, and one was sitting at position 1. The first five visible videos all answered status OK and playableInEmbed true, so the order was the fault and not the videos.
  • It was not worked around, and that is why it got fixed. Storing a known-good starting id would have worked that afternoon, and it would have been an id that drifts plus a playlist still opening on a dead video for anybody reaching it on YouTube. It was held instead — nothing rendered — and reported with the diagnosis. Ryan removed the dead entry from the playlist the same day, and it was re-checked exactly the way it was diagnosed: header 83 → 82, hidden 13 → 12, plain embed plays, control still plays. Both decks are live.
  • The episode stays in the data file rather than being quietly dropped, because the lesson has no other home: a playlist can be perfectly healthy and still fail to embed because of what sits at the top of it, and nothing in this repo can see that. love-embed.js only asks whether a URL is on an allowed origin, and check-jukebox.py asks watch pages about single videos — it says in its own source that a playlist is not one video.
  • held survives as a state, and it earned its keep on its first day. It is the Jungle Room’s dark cam arriving on a dancefloor: the entry keeps its place in the data with the date and the diagnosis on it, and nothing renders, because the room does not carry a control that cannot work. make-jukebox.py refuses a hold with no reason written down — a hold nobody can explain is deleted by the next person to read the file — and refuses a tray with everything held, which would be a heading over an empty box.
  • That tool had no refusals at all before today. It now also refuses a runtime on a playlist — the pair of rules make-club.py and make-chappell.py already hold — a frame this site will not build, and a frame with no list= in it, which is not a playlist at all.   • And this room was carrying a sentence about the room next door that yesterday’s work made false. It said every button in there says how long the thing runs before you press it — true until The Chappell got a chancel, whose whole point is that it cannot say. Nothing caught it, because it is a claim about one room living in another room’s copy, and no generator owns that. Corrected here, and worth remembering: when a room’s promise changes, the rooms that describe it are not in its tool.
  • The decks are not a second jukebox. Same cream tray, same white card, same dark red rule, same small plum credit line — every colour already a pair check-contrast.py holds, so they introduced nothing to measure. What separates them is proportion: the jukebox packs narrow cards into columns and the decks never have more than one across, because a record you put on is not a thing you scan. If they ever grow a second column they have become the jukebox with longer titles in it.

Verified as rendered: zero iframes on load, and one press produced one iframe at the right playlist at 516×288 with the other deck still a button and the ten jukebox buttons untouched.

  • And make-club.py stopped keeping its own copy of that list. It held a two-item tuple with a comment saying three places were deliberate; it was a hand-kept copy of something with a single source of truth, and it would have refused a deck the browser was perfectly willing to frame. Apple’s player shows a grey placeholder for about ten seconds before it initialises — that is its loading state and not a fault, which is worth knowing before somebody “fixes” it. 2026-09-22 — Club Chronic drops the needle, and the word it will not use

  • The stage’s YouTube deck now starts somewhere random in the playlist and plays on in order from there. Ryan asked whether the embed could be set to shuffle. It cannot, and the room says so in its own copy rather than borrowing the word: that is not shuffle: the player has no such button.
  • Three levers were measured before anything was built, because two of them look like they work. Framed from a served page so the referrer was real: shuffle=1 is inert; index= is inert on both the videoseries and the listType=playlist form; only /embed/<id>?list= moves the starting point. The first pass of that measurement read the posters and would have reported index= as working — the poster is the playlist’s first entry whatever the player then does, so all four were re-checked with muted autoplay. The Playhouse’s lesson in a different room: the DOM was right and the screen was wrong.
  • The only lever that exists takes an id, so the file holds ids rather than a count — and that cost was stated before it was paid. A random start therefore needs the playlist mirrored here, and the mirror drifts by design: a song added to the playlist cannot be picked until somebody pulls again, and a song removed becomes a start point the list no longer has. Neither is visible on the page. pull-club.py --check is the only thing that sees either.
  • The obvious continuation token is the wrong one, and taking it mirrors a hundred rows of nearly four hundred while reporting success. A playlist page carries two: the section list’s sits at the tidy continuationEndpoint.continuationCommand.token, and the video list’s — the only one that pages the songs — is buried inside a commandExecutorCommand. The tool now takes any token it finds anywhere inside the node. A short list is not an error anywhere, which is why it also refuses rather than carrying on when it cannot read the page at all.
  • The playlist’s own header claims a number YouTube will not serve — The Chappell’s discrepancy, met again and written down rather than rounded off. The hidden rows are not marked unplayable, they are simply absent, so the guard against private entries dropped none of them.
  • Nine songs are in that playlist more than once and each is one start point. Checked rather than assumed: every repeat has its own setVideoId, so the list genuinely holds those songs twice and one of them three times. A start point is a video rather than a position — /embed/<id>?list= opens at the first occurrence whichever one you meant — so a second copy cannot be reached and would only double that song’s odds. make-club.py refuses a repeat, and the refusal says which of those two things it means.
  • love-embed.js is still the only thing on this site that builds a frame. club.js builds nothing: it rewrites the button’s data-embed-src before the press reaches the document, and the shared builder then does exactly what it always did with exactly the attributes it always read. The ordering is the whole trick — this listener is on the button and that one is on the document, so this runs at the target and that runs on the way up. It fails back to the top of the list, never to nothing.
  • “Nothing plays until you press play” was re-verified rather than reasoned about. Zero requests to youtube on load, with a control to prove the recorder was awake; one press produced one iframe at /embed/<id>?list=, rendered 561×313 rather than 0×0, with its referrerpolicy intact; three presses gave three different tracks; and the Spotify deck was untouched, still a button, still carrying no start points it could not use.
  • New: club.js, tools/pull-club.py and its --check. Changed: data/club.json, club-chronic.html, tools/make-club.py and this README. Not changed: the Content-Security-Policy, which already covers a script of our own, and love-embed.js.

2026-09-22 — One Space Grotesk, and a bold the bench had been hiding

  • Space Grotesk was stored three times over, byte for byte. Same sha256, same 22320 bytes, and fonts/_sources.json recorded the same gstatic URL for all three, because the family ships as a variable font and Google serves one file for every weight. On the shared base that every page on the street loads, that cost about 45KB. It is one file now, declared three times against the weight axis, the way §1 already handles Cabin, Karla and the rest. The Feed was preloading the copy that got deleted, which would have 404’d on a published page; it preloads the one that survives.
  • And then the interesting half: identical bytes are not an absent bold. The obvious reading of three identical files is that a weight-700 declaration renders the same drawings as the 400 — and The Foundry had been built on exactly that reading since it opened, grouping each family’s variants by the sha of the file they point at and keeping one. It is the wrong question. A variable font carries a weight axis inside the file, and the browser instances it at every @font-face declaration: same bytes, genuinely different outlines. The rule was throwing away bolds that work.
  • Measured rather than argued about, because the premise sounded right. Rendered out of one file at 64px: Cinzel’s 700 lays down 68% more ink than its 400, Work Sans’ 600 45%, Space Grotesk’s 700 41%, Nunito’s 39%. Not one pair on the shelves came out the same. Twelve families had a weight the street holds and the bench was keeping in a drawer — Cabin, Cinzel, EB Garamond, Faustina, Jost, Karla, Mulish, Nunito, Quicksand, Rubik, Space Grotesk and Vollkorn — and every one of them is offered now.
  • tools/check-weights.py is the check the sha was standing in for. It renders the published picker — never the generator, which is check-quests.py’s rule — and refuses two offerings of one family that come out the same drawings, a weight love.css does not declare, and a declared weight the room does not offer. Advance width alone cannot decide it: Courier Prime and Space Mono are monospaced, so their 400 and 700 share an advance to the hundredth of a pixel while the bold lays down about 40% more ink. It measures ink and advance and calls two the same only when both match.
  • It found a bug in itself on the first run, which is why it was broken on purpose first. @font-face is lazy, so nothing is downloaded until a page sets it — and a canvas asked to draw a font it has not got silently draws the fallback instead. The first draft measured before loading anything, so every face on the street would have measured identically and been reported as the same drawings. It loads, waits, then measures, and still asks whether each face actually arrived. Fed a static face declared at two weights, it correctly calls the second one a control that does nothing; that is the protection the sha was giving, and it is still there.
  • Nunito’s bold was not in the record at all. love.css declares that one file at 400 and 700; fonts/_sources.json listed only the 400, and nothing compared the two. The new checker reads the stylesheet the browser actually obeys, so that class of drift now refuses rather than sitting there.
  • Two things found and deliberately not changed. Jost is still stored twice — jost-400.woff2 and jost-500.woff2 are byte-identical, the same duplication Space Grotesk had — and it is logged here rather than swept into a commit about something else. And the campground sets its emphasis in Alfa Slab One because Bitter’s bold “was byte-identical to its regular”: that reasoning is the one corrected above, so the decision rests on a premise that turns out to be false. It is a room’s own design decision and not a bug, so it stays until somebody who owns that room says otherwise.

2026-09-22 — The Foundry, and the first room whose content is the typefaces

  • A type foundry on the street: The Foundry. Ryan’s brief — a font play storefront, your own text or text out of The Playhouse, The Mopery and The Zibaldone, every setting the type has, and a way to print it. A grey iron workshop under a dirty roof with a bench in it. Every other room on this street uses a typeface; this is the one where they are the thing in the window.
  • It is the first mid-grey ground here, and that is the whole separation. Every other world on the street is either a night ground with light applied to it or a sheet of paper with the room cropped away. This is the middle — the colour of the metal — and the only pale object in it is one proof lying on a bench. The four paper rooms are the obvious collapse and each one is held off by something structural: The Mopery is a cold building you stand up in whose type is impressed into the page, the guild is manila lit like a photocopy from nowhere, The Doomscroll is one unbroken roll of warm foxed newsprint, and The Zibaldone is bound leaves with a gutter and a fold. There is no fold in here and nothing is bound.
  • It is the third daylit room, and north light is what a workshop was built for — the glazing faces away from the sun so the work has no glare on it, which means flat, cool, directionless daylight and no lit face on anything. The Hermitage has a low morning sun with long sideways shadows; the Garden’s light has come through leaves and tints every pale surface green. A furnace would have been the obvious build and would have made this the seventh warm room on a street that already has six. The only warm value in here is brass, and brass is a metal rather than a light.
  • Nothing about any typeface was typed. tools/pull-foundry.py reads every family’s own record out of github.com/google/fonts — family name, designer, licence, copyright, repository, classification — and cross-checks the licence against which directory of that repository the family lives in. It refuses a family it cannot resolve rather than publishing a face with nobody’s name under it. It is kept out of the pre-deploy sequence with check-jukebox.py because it needs the network, and make-foundry.py needs nothing but the file it writes. Do not merge them.
  • The first thing it found was that four of these families are not OFL, and three published pages said they were. Permanent Marker, Rock Salt, Special Elite and Ultra are under the Apache License 2.0. love.css’s §1 comment said “every family here is OFL-licensed”, the liner notes said “all are licensed under the SIL Open Font License” and again “the typefaces keep their own OFL licence”, and the Mopery’s generated credit line said it a fourth time. All four are corrected and every face now carries its own licence on the shelves. A licence nobody has checked since the file was added is a claim rather than a fact, which is the whole argument for a tool that goes and reads.
  • The Mopery’s picker had quietly lost eighteen families, and tools/check-faces.py is why it cannot again. That room says in its own copy that the picker holds every typeface on this street and llms.txt says the poem can be set in any face on it; both had been false for a long time, because adding a file to fonts/ and adding an <option> in a room two doors away are two edits in two places and only one of them is obviously required. The picker is generated now, from the same record The Foundry’s shelves are built from — when something has to agree in several places, make the others read it rather than write it out again, which is love-embed.js’s ORIGINS lesson. The new checker reads both rooms’ published markup and never the generators, in both directions, and it was broken on purpose before being believed.
  • An italic is not a slant, and the bench is built to show you rather than tell you. A real italic is a second alphabet, cut separately, with different letters in it. A machine slant is the upright pushed over by something that has no idea what it is looking at. Both are offered and both are labelled, and the italic is disabled where this site holds no italic file — because the browser answers a request for one it has not got by inventing it, nothing looks broken, and the room has told you a lie about somebody’s typeface. Measured rather than assumed: on Rye, a requested italic renders at the roman’s own advance widths; on Cardo, which has a real italic, oblique 12deg comes out at 399.65px against the italic’s 377.42px, so the slant really is the roman pushed over.
