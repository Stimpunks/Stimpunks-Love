A cave in the woods

Luis’s Burrow

Luis’s: a cave dug in under the roots of an old tree, heaped with cushions and blankets, with fluffy animals curled up in it, a basket of fidgets, a coffee machine, a bookshelf and a telly in the corner.

The burrow is dug in under the roots of an old tree, so the roots come through the ceiling, and the only light is the wood: the round mouth looks out on it, bright green in the afternoon, and the daylight comes in and lies across the floor. Everything in here is soft. There is no chair and no table, only cushions and blankets, orange and apricot and moss green, heaped as deep as you like.

Whose this is

This is Luis’s. A burrow of his own on the campgrounds, a surprise from Helen Edgar, pitched in the wood at the edge of the field with the street still in reach down the path. Come in, take your shoes off, and sink into something.

The nest

  • Cushions, big and small, orange, apricot and moss green, stitched round the edge with a button in the middle, heaped along the walls and across the floor.
  • Blankets, one over another all the way to the walls: a thick orange one, an apricot fleece and a green one with a fold in it for burrowing into.
  • Moss on the walls, soft enough to lean on.
  • Pumpkins, a heap of them on the blanket by the basket, two orange and one green, for leaning on and for the season.
  • The roots of the tree through the ceiling, which is the tree holding the burrow up.

Who curls up here

The wood’s fluffiest come in out of the weather and nobody minds. A fox is curled asleep on the orange blanket, nose under tail. Two rabbits sit together on the long cushion. An owl dozes on a root under the ceiling, and a dormouse is asleep in a fold of the green blanket. Nobody has to do anything, and they come and go as they please.

The coffee machine

HOW LOUD DO YOU WANT IT?

Starts wherever your device says. Nothing moves, flashes, or plays until you say so — and in this burrow, the fox breathes in its sleep only at MAX GLITTER, and a fidget squashes only when you press it, and never at Gentle.

On the ledge by the mouth, a coffee machine with a grinder on top, a shelf of beans in jars, and lots of cups hanging on hooks under it, no two alike. Help yourself. Pick a bean, grind it fresh, pour a cup, and take it back to the cushions: nothing is for sale in here and nobody is keeping a tally of cups. The beans come from all over, and each jar says where it grew, how it is roasted and how strong it is, one to ten, which is the burrow’s own scale and nobody’s standard. The tasting words are ours, the way a bag of beans describes itself.

The basket of fidgets

A basket on the blanket, full of things to squeeze, pop, pull and push, and nothing to do with any of them but that. Press one and it says what it did, where you pressed. Nobody is counting, nothing is kept, and nothing makes a sound.

The telly

The telly sits on a cushion in the corner with its aerials up. On it: five Frasier clips, and then the wood, animals running and playing in forests and a waterfall in one, to leave on. Each plays where it hangs on the rack, or goes up on the telly with the button under it. Every button says how long it runs before you press it, and nothing loads from YouTube until you do.
