The Campgrounds

Not everybody wants a shopfront. The street is a row of them shoulder to shoulder under a marquee that shouts at you, and some of us would rather be out at the edge of town with a stream going past. So the street stops here and the field starts, and the field has pitches on it.

WHAT THE FIELD IS

  • Nothing out here moves. Not at Gentle, not at Regular, not at MAX GLITTER. The dial still works and it still governs every pitch, because each pitch is its own world and sets its own noise — but this field has nothing for it to turn up. An area that exists for people who want quiet cannot have a loud setting and mean it.
  • You can arrive and leave without telling anybody. There is nothing to sign, nothing to join, and nothing that notices you came.
  • Read the sign before you go in. A pitch is somebody’s own place and it looks like them, not like this field and not like the street. The signs below say what is inside so that walking in is a decision rather than a surprise.
  • The stream is the boundary. Past it is woodland and we have not drawn a map of it. That is on purpose too.

WHY THERE IS A FIELD AT ALL

Danny the Street rearranges himself for whoever needs sheltering, and for some of us that shape is not a street. A row of lit storefronts is a wonderful place to be and an exhausting place to live, and a site that only had one of those would be telling its own quiet people that the loud room is the only room.

So the street kept its doors and grew an edge. Out here the type is bigger, the ground is cold, and there is exactly one thing to read at a time. The street is still there when you want it.

HOW LOUD DO YOU WANT IT?

Starts wherever your device says. Nothing moves, flashes, or plays until you say so — and in this field, nothing moves even then.
