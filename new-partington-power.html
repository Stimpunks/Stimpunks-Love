A brick house where the town gives out onto the farms

New Partington Power’s House

New Partington Power’s: a brick house with a covered porch, a small makeshift maker space at the end of it, and the farm right over the back fence.

The porch is glazed in clear glass, and it is in its own roof’s shade, so everything near you keeps its earth colours: the brick, the painted boards, the chairs and the bench. Everything past the glass is the farm, in full sun and a long way off in the afternoon haze, so the sky, the fields, the barn and the sheep are soft and pale. Every pastel here is the farm, far off beyond the glass. There is no lamp on the porch, and the old television on the bench is off.

Whose this is

This is New Partington Power’s. A home of their own on Nature’s Contribution, built to be somewhat like their own place: an ordinary suburban house, brick, with a covered porch and somewhere to make things. It stands at the very edge of town, where the last street stops at a gate and the farm’s lane carries on, so it has a suburb on one side and the fields on the other.

The house and the porch

  • Brick, all the way round, red and dark red in courses, with the front door painted the mint of the far field.
  • A covered porch across the front, with a brick knee wall, pale posts and a rail, and the whole of it glazed in clear glass, so you can sit out in any weather the farm has and still see all of it.
  • Two chairs on the porch, and a little table between them with a mug on it.
  • Painted boards underfoot, grey-green, and the porch roof’s boards overhead.

The maker space

At the end of the porch, small and makeshift, the way a maker space at home usually starts: whatever was to hand, put to use.

  • The bench is an old door laid across two sawhorses, its knob still sticking out of one end.
  • A pegboard leaned against the end pane, with a wrench, a screwdriver, pliers, a tape measure and a reel of solder hung on it.
  • An old television, the deep kind with a curved glass screen, switched off, and a VCR beside it, with a few tapes stacked at the other end of the bench.
  • A soldering iron in its coiled stand, and a coffee can of screws.
  • A stool, and an extension lead run across the boards from the outlet by the front door, which is the most makeshift thing on the porch and the most useful.

New Partington Power’s own “SJ” Station ID, a glossy station logo warped like an old tape, with a broken heart tucked between the letters, is pinned on the Pebble Board.

Over the back fence

The back fence is where the yard stops and All Together Now Farms starts: its fields come right up to it, with sheep in the nearest one and a row of corn, and its barn and silo stand up the lane. The farm’s lane goes on from the gate at the end of the street, past the house, to the café in the old dairy.

A home owes the land nothing. Every other place on Nature’s Contribution gives something to the others, and this one is somebody’s house, so it is in none of the rows of what goes where.
