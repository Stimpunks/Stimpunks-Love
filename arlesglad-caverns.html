Arlesglad Caverns

Under the street there is rock nobody dug, and a way down into it that anybody can take. The weather stops at the top of the shaft. The daylight stops too, and the clock is up there with it. What is down here is here because it needs things to stay the same, and down here they do. We are glad of it, which is half the name.

What is down here

  • The deeper you go, the steadier it gets. A cave keeps its weather out. At the real Carlsbad Caverns, the National Park Service measures the Big Room at an average of 56°F (13°C), moving a little with the seasons, and the lowest point at a constant 68°F (20°C) with the air close to saturated all year. So everything down here needs something to hold still: a cellar, a crop that grows in the dark, a light that only shows without one, warm water that holds you up, and a room at the bottom that is simply the steady part.
  • The only light is the handrail. It runs at hand height along the one route, from the lift to every door, and the rock is lit where the path goes and nowhere else. There is no sky down here and no lamp overhead. Down here the only colour is the light; each room brings its own.
  • The lift is the ordinary way down. The stair comes down beside the shaft, and every landing has the same names painted on it as the lift’s panel, so neither one is the other’s alternative. If you came down in the lift, you came the usual way.
  • Nobody was sent down here. Underground, here, is a direction and not a place to keep things out of sight. A cellar is under the street because a cellar is always under the street; a mushroom farm is in a cave because that is where mushrooms have always been grown. Down is not the same as hidden.
  • Nothing down here moves on its own. The dial still works and each room sets its own noise, but the passage has no ambient layer at any setting. The rail does not flicker, because it is the one thing you are holding.

Down by the lift

One button for every room, top to bottom. A lit button is a door you can go through. An unlit button is a room that is not open yet, and it says so rather than doing nothing when you press it.

Where the name comes from

Arlesglad is a play on Carlsbad Caverns, with a glad spin, and with ARLES in the middle of it: the Stimpunks Design Method, whose ladder starts at the bottom, where a person actually is, and works outward to the room and the systems around it. This is the one place on the street you reach by going down, and that is why the name fits. The caverns are not a diagram of the method, though, and no room down here is a rung on it.

Carlsbad Caverns is a real place, a national park in New Mexico looked after by the National Park Service. This is not it. We borrowed a pun and two of the Park Service’s measurements, and say where they came from. The park is not affiliated with Stimpunks and does not endorse us.
