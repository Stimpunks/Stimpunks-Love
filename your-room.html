Your Room

There is a storefront on this street with nothing in it, and it is empty on purpose. It is not scenery and it is not “coming soon” — it is a room we are not going to decorate, because it is not ours to decorate.

THE OFFER, PLAINLY

One Disabled, Autistic, neurodivergent or otherwise stimpunk person gets a page on this street. You get:

  • A room of your own at stimpunks.world/<your-room>, linked from the front door beside all the others.
  • Your own stylesheet. Not a theme, not a template with your colours swapped in — a blank CSS file and a room that is allowed to contradict every other room on the street. That is the whole architecture here.
  • Your name on it, spelled and pronounced how you say, linked where you want it linked.
  • Help building it if you want help, and none if you don’t. Describe the room and we will build it; or take the files and do it yourself; or anything between.

What we keep

Four things, and they are the same four every room here already honours. They are not editorial control — nobody is going to tell you the room is too much.

What every room on this street keeps, and why none of it is negotiable
The ruleWhy it is not negotiable
The dial worksGentle mode has to turn your room down without taking any of it away. It is the promise the whole street makes at the front door.
Text clears 4.5:1Clashing is not the same as illegible. Be as ugly as you like; be readable while you do it.
Nothing autoplaysSound, video, motion: on press, never on arrival. No exceptions, including for a very good song.
Credit what you didn’t writeAttribution is a licence, not a house style. Quote anything; name whose it is.

What we are not going to do

Edit your room for tone. Ask you to be more positive, or more palatable, or more professional. Take it down because somebody complained that it was weird. If we would not say it to Enid, we are not saying it to you.

How to ask

Tell us what the room is. One paragraph is plenty — what it is for, who walks into it, and what it looks like when they do. There is no form and no application, and you do not need a portfolio or a reason to deserve it.

Reach us however is easiest: through the Stimpunks contact page, or in our community.

Build it with your AI, if you like

You can also design your room in conversation with Claude, or whichever AI you use, and send us what it makes. Copy the prompt below into a new chat. It asks you about the room first, then builds it as one web page you can look at and keep changing until it is right. Claude calls that page an artifact, and other AIs have their own names for it. When you are happy with it, send us the file or a share link, the same way as above.

We take it from there. We bring the room onto the street, give it a share card and a place on the map, measure it against the four rules above, and fix anything that falls short with you rather than around you. Then we credit you, and say the room was built with an AI, the same way How this site is made says how we use one ourselves. That page is also the best short account of how rooms here work, if you or your AI want to read it first.

You do not need an AI, and the room is yours either way. The prompt tells the AI to use your own words for anything about you and not to write your story for you. One thing to know first: whatever you tell an AI goes to the company that runs it, so tell it only what you are happy for that company to have. Nothing on this page sends anything anywhere. The prompt is text you copy.

The prompt

I'd like your help designing my own room on Stimpunks.World (https://stimpunks.world), a website run by Stimpunks, a Disabled people's organisation. The site is a street of rooms, and no two rooms look alike: every room is its own visual world with its own colours, typefaces and light. How the site works is explained at https://stimpunks.world/design.html, and the offer I'm answering is at https://stimpunks.world/your-room.html. If you can read web pages, read both. Make my room look like nothing else on that street.

FIRST, ASK ME ABOUT THE ROOM. Ask a few questions at a time and let me skip any of them:
- what the room is for, and who walks into it
- what it looks like when they do, and where its light comes from (a lamp, a window, a screen, the floor, nowhere at all). Rooms on this street are told apart mostly by their light, so let that decide the palette.
- what should be in it: words, drawings, songs or videos, anything to press
- what it must never do
- my name as I want it credited, how to say it, my pronouns if I want them shown, and where my name should link

Use my own words for anything about me. Don't write my story for me. If you draft words, show them to me as drafts I can change. If I don't know an answer yet, pick something and let me react to it.

THEN BUILD IT as a single self-contained HTML page I can preview and we can keep changing together.

HOW THE PAGE HAS TO BE BUILT, so the street can bring it in:
1. One HTML file with lang="en", one <h1>, and headings in order. Put class="room-SLUG" on <body>, where SLUG is a short name for the room in lowercase with hyphens. Everything the room shows goes inside <main>.
2. All the CSS goes in one <style> element. Every selector starts with .room-SLUG, apart from :root. Every class name starts with a short prefix of two or three letters followed by a hyphen (for example "xy-"). Declare every colour once, as a hex custom property on :root that uses the same prefix (for example --xy-ground).
3. Typefaces from Google Fonts only, under the SIL Open Font License or the Apache License 2.0. You may load them from Google Fonts for the preview; the street hosts its own copies.
4. No other external files at all: no scripts, stylesheets, images, trackers or embeds from anywhere else. Draw pictures as inline SVG or CSS. Don't use an image generator: nothing on this street comes from one. If I want a photograph in the room, leave a clearly marked space and say so in the notes. I'll send the photo separately, and only one I took myself, with the say-so of anybody in it.
5. THE DIAL. The street sets data-intensity="gentle", "regular" or "max" on <html>, and the room has to honour all three:
   - gentle: nothing moves. No animation, no transition, no rotation or skew. Every word and every thing is still there. Gentle turns the room down and never takes anything away.
   - regular: motion only on hover or keyboard focus.
   - max: ambient motion is allowed.
   At every setting, nothing flashes or strobes (never more than three flashes a second). Until the street sets the attribute, follow prefers-reduced-motion. Add a small preview dial with three buttons, marked data-preview-only, so I can try all three settings; the street removes it and uses its own.
6. CONTRAST. Every piece of text must reach at least 4.5:1 against the colour actually behind it. Put text on flat colour, never directly on a gradient, pattern or picture. Choose a link colour for every ground a link sits on, and make the keyboard focus ring clearly visible on each of those grounds. Next to each colour in the CSS, note in a comment which colours it is used with and the ratio.
7. NOTHING PLAYS BY ITSELF. No <audio>, no <video>, no iframes. For each song or video I want, put a button saying what it is, who made it and how long it runs, with the link in a data-media attribute. The street turns those into its own press-to-play players.
8. NOTHING IS SENT OR KEPT. No forms, no fetch, no cookies, no localStorage, no analytics. Any script is small, plain JavaScript in one <script> at the end of the page, and the room still reads completely with scripts switched off. Anything meant to be found is reachable by keyboard and by a screen reader. Nothing is hidden with display:none or shrunk to a tiny target to make it a secret.
9. It works at 360 pixels wide with no sideways scrolling. Every meaningful picture has alt text, and decoration is aria-hidden.
10. CREDITS. Add a credits section at the foot of the room. Name every quotation (who, which work, which year, and a link), every typeface and who designed it, every song, and anything else borrowed. Don't quote anything you can't source. If you're unsure of a source, say so rather than guessing. Say in the credits that the room was built with your help, and name which AI you are.

LAST, add an HTML comment at the very top of the file headed "FOR THE STREET". In it list: my name as credited and where it links; the slug; where the room's light comes from; the typefaces; every song or video with its link and runtime; any photographs to come; which words are mine and which you drafted; and anything I asked for that you couldn't do within these rules.

Keep improving the page with me until I say it's done.

A note on what happens if it goes quiet

A room here does not expire and does not need maintaining. If you stop updating it, it stays up exactly as you left it, with your name on it. If you want it taken down, say so once and it comes down, no conversation required. A gift with conditions attached is not a gift.
