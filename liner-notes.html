Liner Notes

Who made this noise. This street dropped almost every careful habit our other sites have — careful palettes, careful hedging, the balanced tone. It kept exactly one, and this is the page where it lives.

Attribution is not a house style here. It is a licence, it is the cheapest thing on the page, and it is the only one of our habits whose absence is a legal problem rather than an aesthetic one. So the credits are loud instead of hidden: scrawled in the margins of the rooms, and collected in full here.

All are licensed under the SIL Open Font License and all are self-hosted — reading a page here makes no third-party request, the same rule queering.earth and starstuff.earth follow.

Chairy’s lines
Twenty-eight sayings, each recorded in data/chairy.json with the page it came from. Five are paraphrased from our own Behold the Spectrum, which in turn quotes Carol Black’s A Thousand Rivers. The rest come from our philosophy, framing and creed pages. Two are not ours and Chairy says so aloud: “Nothing About Us Without Us” is a motto of the self-advocacy movement, credited on our own page to Michael Masutha and William Rowland of Disabled People South Africa, 1993; and “Design for Real Life” is a book by Eric A. Meyer and Sara Wachter-Boettcher. A talking chair that passed off a movement slogan as a house line would be the exact failure this site argues against.

The borrowed names

Six rooms named after things other people made. None of these people or estates are affiliated with Stimpunks, and none of them endorse us. The rooms are homage, not licence — we took the shape of the idea and wrote our own words in it.

Danny the Street
A character from DC Comics’ Doom Patrol, created by Grant Morrison and Richard Case. A sentient street that rearranges itself to shelter whoever needs sheltering. The whole architecture of this site is stolen from Danny, and we would like that on the record.
Pee-Wee’s Playhouse
Created by Paul Reubens. Where the idea of a room in which every object is alive and clickable comes from.
Pink Pony Club, and Chappell Roan
A song by Chappell Roan, and now also a subroom of thirteen videos from the same catalogue. Both take the shape of the work and none of the words — not because a lyric is untouchable, but because this room never needed one to make its point. Where we do quote a song we follow how we quote: a line or two, never the chorus on its own, and the songwriter named separately from the performer.
Enid Sinclair
A character in Netflix’s Wednesday. Colorful goth, and the patron saint of not matching the house.
Riot grrrl
A movement rather than an author. The zine table takes its form — xerox, ransom note, staples, free — and none of its text.
Quantum queering
Barad’s argument, carried into physics education by Alexis Buzzell and Ramón Barthelemy, and read from Autistic experience by Helen Edgar.
Esmx the Porkypine — Kaya Oldaker
Not borrowed, and in this list anyway. Esmx is the Stimpunks Foundation’s mascot, commissioned from Kaya Oldaker and ours to use; the artist is credited here, in the Arcade and on the drawing itself, because holding the rights to a commission is not a reason to stop naming the hand that drew it. “Esmx” is CC BY-SA 4.0 with an invitation attached — feel free to customise! Make variants of Esmx to represent your community — and the Esmx in the cabinet is us taking our own invitation rather than pasting in the original. Kaya also asks that anybody sharing Esmx include alt text. Esmx’s own page has the originals, every official variant and the blank template.
Sonic, and Pokémon
Named on Esmx’s own page as the inspiration behind the design, due to their popularity within the Autistic community. No character, sprite, sound or mechanic of either is used here; what the Arcade takes from them is the fact that the mascot already came from that shelf, which is the reason there is an arcade at all.
Quill Drift’s one rule
That a porcupine cannot shoot, throw or fire a quill is the correction on card No. 3 of the Quillery, which sources it to Uldis Roze, Porcupines: The Animal Answer Guide (Johns Hopkins University Press, 2012). It is the cabinet’s whole mechanic and not a note at the bottom of it.

The rooms somebody else designed

Your Room has always said that a storefront on this street belongs to whoever decorates it. This is where that is kept.

The Faery Yurt — Helen Edgar
Pitch 01 in the campgrounds is Helen’s, and hers is the design as well as the words: the palette, the three typefaces, the drawings, the bookshelf, the glimmer window and every line of copy came from a mockup she built and sent on 2026-09-20. What this site added was its own plumbing — the dial, self-hosted faces, the consent record behind the photograph — and one colour, because her smallest grey measured 4.30 against the tent ground and 3.43 under the ember glow, and contrast is one of the two things that stay careful here. Helen is at Autistic Realms. She is a colleague, not a corpus; the room is credited to her and is not folded into our “we”.
