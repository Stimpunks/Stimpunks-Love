Arlesglad Caverns, level B2

Mycelium Munchies

A mushroom farm with a restaurant at the end of the growing rooms. All kinds of mushrooms, served all kinds of ways, and every one of them grown on the shelves you walk past to get to the pass.

On the pass: the house specialities

  • Crispy fried oyster mushrooms

    fried · grey oyster mushroom, pink oyster mushroom and golden oyster mushroom

    Oyster mushrooms torn into pieces along their own gills, coated and fried until the edges shatter.

    In it: Grey, pink and golden oyster mushrooms; flour, cornflour, salt, pepper and smoked paprika; fried in sunflower oil; a lemon and garlic dip on the side, made with oil and no egg.

  • Lion's mane nuggets

    fried · lion's mane

    Lion's mane pulled into pieces the size of a bite, pressed, breaded and fried, soft all the way through.

    In it: Lion's mane; flour, oat milk and breadcrumbs; salt, garlic powder and onion powder; fried in sunflower oil; a mustard and maple dip.

  • Mushroom steaks

    seared · king oyster mushroom and portobello

    A king oyster cut into thick planks and scored, or a whole portobello cap, seared hard in a heavy pan and finished with the pan's own juices. Your pick of the two.

    In it: King oyster mushrooms or portobello caps; olive oil, garlic, thyme, soy sauce and black pepper; mashed potatoes and greens on the side.

The rest of the menu

Every dish says how it is cooked, which mushrooms are in it, and everything else that is in it too, before you order.

  • Shiitake broth with noodles

    simmered · shiitake and enoki

    A dark, clear broth made from dried and fresh shiitake, with noodles, and a nest of enoki dropped in at the end.

    In it: Shiitake, fresh and dried; enoki; wheat noodles; soy sauce, ginger, spring onion and sesame oil.

  • Roast maitake

    roasted · maitake

    A whole maitake cluster roasted until the frilled edges crisp and the middle stays tender, pulled apart at the table.

    In it: Maitake; olive oil, salt and lemon; a spoonful of miso butter made with plant butter.

  • Blue oyster tacos

    griddled · blue oyster mushroom

    Blue oyster mushrooms griddled flat under a weight until they brown, folded into warm tortillas.

    In it: Blue oyster mushrooms; corn tortillas; cumin, chilli, lime and salt; shredded cabbage and coriander.

  • Grilled enoki bundles

    grilled · enoki

    Bundles of enoki tied at the root end and grilled until the tips go crisp.

    In it: Enoki; soy sauce, maple syrup and garlic; a sprinkle of sesame seeds.

  • Mushrooms on toast

    fried slowly · button mushroom and chestnut mushroom

    Button and chestnut mushrooms cooked slowly in a wide pan until they give up their water and take colour, piled on toast.

    In it: Button and chestnut mushrooms; plant butter, garlic and parsley; sourdough toast.

  • Chestnut mushroom soup

    simmered and blended · chestnut mushroom

    A thick soup of chestnut mushrooms cooked down with onions and blended smooth.

    In it: Chestnut mushrooms; onion, garlic, potato, vegetable stock, thyme and oat cream.

  • Pink oyster crisps

    baked · pink oyster mushroom

    Pink oyster mushrooms pressed thin and baked slowly until they snap, which is when they stop being pink.

    In it: Pink oyster mushrooms; olive oil, smoked salt and a little maple syrup.

  • Golden oyster stir-fry

    stir-fried · golden oyster mushroom

    Golden oyster mushrooms tossed fast in a very hot pan with greens, over rice.

    In it: Golden oyster mushrooms; pak choi, garlic, ginger and soy sauce; white rice.

  • Mushroom skewers

    grilled · king oyster mushroom, shiitake and button mushroom

    Chunks of king oyster, whole shiitake caps and button mushrooms on skewers, grilled and brushed as they go.

    In it: King oyster, shiitake and button mushrooms; olive oil, garlic, smoked paprika, and a lemon to squeeze.

The growing rooms

What is on the shelves, what it looks like on this farm’s shelves, what it is like cooked, and what it grows on. Each one says where you will find it on the menu.

  • Grey oyster mushroom

    On the shelf: Soft grey fans growing in overlapping clusters out of the side of their bags, like little shelves.

    Cooked: Tender and a little chewy, and it tears into strips along its gills.

    Grown here on straw. On the menu as Crispy fried oyster mushrooms.

  • Pink oyster mushroom

    On the shelf: Fans of a bright, almost unlikely pink, with frilled edges.

    Cooked: Chewier than the grey, and it goes a pale tan when it cooks, which surprises everybody once.

    Grown here on straw. On the menu as Crispy fried oyster mushrooms and Pink oyster crisps.

  • Golden oyster mushroom

    On the shelf: Small yellow caps in tight bunches, like a bouquet somebody handed you.

    Cooked: Delicate and nutty, and it cooks in almost no time at all.

    Grown here on hardwood sawdust. On the menu as Crispy fried oyster mushrooms and Golden oyster stir-fry.

  • Blue oyster mushroom

    On the shelf: Blue-grey caps when they are young, fading towards grey as they open out.

    Cooked: Firm enough to press flat under a weight and brown hard.

    Grown here on straw. On the menu as Blue oyster tacos.

  • King oyster mushroom

    On the shelf: A thick white stem as long as your hand with a small brown cap on top, so nearly all of it is stem.

    Cooked: Dense and meaty, and it holds its shape when it is cut into planks.

    Grown here on sawdust, in bottles. On the menu as Mushroom steaks and Mushroom skewers.

  • Lion's mane

    On the shelf: A round white cluster of soft hanging spines, like a pom-pom or a waterfall that stopped.

    Cooked: Soft and stringy the way crab is, and it pulls apart into pieces.

    Grown here on blocks of hardwood sawdust. On the menu as Lion's mane nuggets.

  • Shiitake

    On the shelf: Brown caps with pale flecks, on short tough stems that go into the broth rather than the pan.

    Cooked: Rich and savoury, and deeper again once it has been dried.

    Grown here on blocks of hardwood sawdust, and logs. On the menu as Shiitake broth with noodles and Mushroom skewers.

  • Maitake

    On the shelf: A frilled grey-brown cluster of overlapping fronds, big enough to need both hands.

    Cooked: Crisp at the edges and tender in the middle when it is roasted whole.

    Grown here on hardwood sawdust. On the menu as Roast maitake.

  • Enoki

    On the shelf: Long, thin white stems with tiny caps, packed tight in bundles.

    Cooked: Soft and slippery in a broth, and crisp at the tips on a grill.

    Grown here on bottles, in the dark, which is why it comes out long, thin and white. On the menu as Shiitake broth with noodles and Grilled enoki bundles.

  • Button mushroom

    On the shelf: Small, smooth white caps, closed tight underneath.

    Cooked: Mild, and it takes on whatever it is cooked with.

    Grown here on compost. On the menu as Mushrooms on toast and Mushroom skewers.

  • Chestnut mushroom

    On the shelf: The same kind of mushroom as the button, in a brown skin, with a firmer bite.

    Cooked: A little deeper in flavour than the white one.

    Grown here on compost. On the menu as Mushrooms on toast and Chestnut mushroom soup.

  • Portobello

    On the shelf: The chestnut mushroom again, left to grow until its cap opens as flat and wide as a saucer.

    Cooked: Deep, dark and juicy, and big enough to be a meal on its own.

    Grown here on compost. On the menu as Mushroom steaks.

Why a mushroom farm

Because of a post of ours. We once spent a Campfire Learn Together on the way fungi grow, break things down and make something new of what is left, and one line of it is the whole reason this room is under the street:

“Composting is not destruction. It is how a commons renews itself.”

From our own The Mycelial Worldview Is Punk

A mushroom is the fruit. The part that does the growing is the mycelium, the pale threads that run all the way through a block of straw or sawdust and that nobody ever sees on a plate. When a block has given all the mushrooms it is going to, it goes out of the farm as compost. Mushrooms are fungi, not plants, which is also why this is not the plant kitchen upstairs.

House rules

  • Everything on a plate was grown here, on the shelves you walked past.
  • Every dish says what is in it before you order, so nobody has to ask.
  • Nothing is served raw. Every mushroom here is cooked.
  • The mushrooms are named the way the kitchen names them, and never in Latin.
  • Nothing on this page can tell you whether a mushroom you found is safe to eat, and nothing on it tries. Every mushroom here is described as it looks on this farm’s own shelves.
  • A mushroom here is dinner and not a supplement. No dish is for your brain, your nerves or anything else. It is for eating.
  • There are no prices on this menu, and nothing on it is ranked above anything else.

The dishes, the drawings and every word about the mushrooms are ours. The line in the middle of the page is from our own post, and the rest of the post is there to read. Credits are in the liner notes.
