A recording studio on the street

The Live Room

Sessions played in a studio rather than at a gig, and the band on the other side of the glass.

A session is a band playing a room with nobody in it but the people making the recording — or a room with a small crowd in it, sitting down, that the band can see the faces of. A television studio, a radio station’s live room, somewhere with a desk and a sheet of glass. It is the nearest a record gets to being played in the room you are sitting in.

That is why a room of them is worth having here. No queue, no crowd to stand in, no volume that somebody else chose, and you can stop in the middle and nobody sees you leave. On the desk so far: Austin City Limits, a television studio with a crowd in it, and two radio stations’ live rooms, KEXP’s and KCRW’s. Anybody else with a live room is welcome next.

While the red light is on

  • Nothing plays until you press it. Nothing reaches YouTube until you press a strip, and pressing one plays that session and no other.
  • Every strip says how long before you press it, and where and when the session was taped. Where a video is not the whole session, the strip says that too, because a runtime is only honest if you know what it is the runtime of.
  • The desk is not a chart. Nothing here is ranked, rated or counted. The sessions are in the order they were patched in, and the order is not an argument about which is better.
  • The red lamp is steady. It does not flash at any dial setting, and nothing else in the room does either. A light that can put somebody on the floor has no business in a room anybody is meant to enjoy.
  • Nobody is drawn in the live room. The only people in there are the players, in their own sessions.

Austin City Limits Austin, Texas

A television studio with a crowd in it: the longest-running music series in American television, made by KLRU in Austin for PBS since 1974. Its first thirty-six seasons were taped in Studio 6A on the University of Texas campus, in front of a painted skyline of the city; since 2011 it has been taped at ACL Live at The Moody Theater, downtown. Every strip says which of the two it was.

The desk takes suggestions: a whole session played in a studio, filmed — more from KEXP or KCRW, or anybody else’s live room — best of all posted by the studio or the band themselves. Send it through the Stimpunks contact page or in our community. It is checked first to see that it plays, that it can be shown here, how long it runs and that it was played indoors, because a set played outside belongs at Looming Rocks.

Whose this all is

Austin City Limits is KLRU’s, the public television station in Austin, and it is broadcast on PBS. It is the longest-running music series in American television: its pilot was shot on 17 October 1974 with Willie Nelson, it was taped in Studio 6A on the University of Texas campus for its first thirty-six seasons, and it has been taped at ACL Live at The Moody Theater since February 2011. Read off Wikipedia’s article on it on 2026-09-25. The Live Room is neither of those rooms, and Stimpunks has no connection to the show, KLRU or PBS and is not endorsed by any of them.

KEXP is a listener-supported radio station in Seattle, run by the non-profit Friends of KEXP, with its studios at the Seattle Center; read off Wikipedia’s article on it on 2026-09-25. Every KEXP session here is on the station’s own channel, and each video’s own description names the players, the host and everybody who engineered, mixed and filmed it — follow a strip through to YouTube for the full credits. Stimpunks has no connection to KEXP and is not endorsed by it.

KCRW is a non-profit public radio station in Santa Monica, by its own account in every one of its videos. Its sessions here are all on its own channel; most were played in the performance studio at its headquarters, two at Apogee Studio and one at The Village Studios, each strip saying which, and each video’s description credits the players and the crew. Stimpunks has no connection to KCRW and is not endorsed by it.

Every strip names the channel its video is on. Some are the show’s own; the others are on channels that collect recordings, and two of those say in their own descriptions that the recording is not theirs. None of these artists, labels or channels is affiliated with Stimpunks, and none of them endorses us. The full list, with links, is in the liner notes.

The room’s name is written on masking tape in Just Another Hand, by Astigmatic, under the Apache License 2.0; everything else is B612, by Nicolas Chauveau, Thomas Paillot, Jonathan Favre-Lamarine and Jean-Luc Vinot, under the SIL Open Font License — a face drawn for the displays in an aircraft’s cockpit, which is the nearest thing to the legend on a desk.
