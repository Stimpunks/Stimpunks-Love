Moderators

A room for Stimpunks Moderators to meet.

What the base looks after

  • The CB’s house norms, at the Community Center: BASE is the one mark on the channel that is checked, and only the base can take somebody else’s message off the air before its 48 hours are up.
  • Our covenant, which applies in every Stimpunks space, and is where our norms and how we repair harm live.
  • The calls: the base moderates every room’s call, lets guests in from the lobby at Cavendish Coworking’s open meetings, and is the only one who can record a call or turn on transcription, and then only after saying so in the call.
  • Reports: anybody can reach us privately, and our concern reporting policy says how a concern is handled.

The desk: invites

Everybody joins the CB with an invite. The starter password was retired on 21 October 2026, and every username on the CB is claimed now, with a password of its own. Make an invite here and share its link: it opens the joining page with the code filled in, where somebody chooses a username and a password of their own, and an email address for recovery if they want one. The code works typed into that page too.

An invite can be for one person or for many, and runs out after a day, a week, a month, or never. One for a group, or for the join form on the Stimpunks Community page, is one with no limit; make a new one now and then, and delete the old. A note says what an invite is for, so the next moderator knows. Nothing records who joined with which invite: an invite counts how many times it has been used, and that is all. Deleting one stops it at once, and the usernames made with it stay. A moderator’s username needs no invite: it is claimed with the moderators’ password.

The invites open for a moderator signed on as the base, and the server refuses everybody else whatever this page shows.

The desk: roles

Who is a moderator, and who also holds a role, is changed here by our administrators (since 21 October 2026). Being on the list makes somebody a moderator: to make somebody a moderator and nothing more, put them on the list with nothing else ticked. Moderator opens the moderation tools on the CB, this room and its desk, and the Moderators, Team Docs and Back Office Operations forum categories. Board opens the Board room and Executive Session, with their channels, calls and forum categories, and Tasks and the office; director the Directors’ room, the Board room, Tasks and the office; administrator every private room except Executive Session, and Tasks, the office and this list. A username on the list that has not been claimed yet is claimed on the joining page with the moderators’ password.

A few names are on the break-glass list, kept in the site’s settings rather than here, so nobody can be locked out of this desk: it shows them, and cannot change them. Board is given or taken only by somebody on the board or on the break-glass list, because Executive Session is the board without staff, and nobody can be left without an administrator. Each name keeps who changed it last and when, and nothing more. A change reaches every page within half a minute, and the person’s radio picks up their new pass by itself the next time it listens.

The desk: everybody

Every username on the CB, how many there are, and who is seen in a room now, so that we know what we could tell at a glance if we were all in one room together. Moderators asked for it for safeguarding, and it is Ryan’s call of 22 October 2026, which reverses the street’s old rule that there was no list of everybody. It is here and nowhere else: the street’s rule against counting people is about its public surfaces, and this is not one.

Seen means they pressed Be seen here, by the same rules as Who’s online on the radio: a room that keeps to itself is never named, and somebody seen only there is not shown as seen. So everybody else may be on the CB too, unseen; the list never says anybody is offline. A name opens their profile, the one anybody signed on can open from the channel; At the desk looks them up below. The list says nothing else about anybody: not when they joined, not their email address, not their roles beyond moderator, and nothing records who looked.

The desk: usernames

Everybody on the CB has claimed their username, with a password of their own. When somebody has lost their password and their recovery code, and has no email address on their username or the reset code never came, a moderator can give them a way back in from here. Look up the one you were asked about by name, or from the list above. The desk never shows anybody’s email address.

Before a reset, make sure who you are talking to, on Stoat or in a call, the way you would before giving anybody a key. A reset code is good for a day, signs the username off everywhere, and is shown to you once: pass it on privately. Deleting a username deletes its password and its pets, and anybody can claim the name afterwards.

Kick, time out and ban (since 22 October 2026) each sign the username off every device at once. Kick is only that: they can sign straight back on with their own password. Time out keeps them off the CB for ten minutes, an hour, a day or a week, and ban until a moderator lifts it; while either holds, nothing that needs signing on works for them, reading included. Signing on, they are told a moderator timed them out and for how much longer, or banned them, and where to reach us; nobody else who tries the name learns anything. Nothing records who did it or why, only until when, so tell them why yourself if you can, and tell the other moderators. A ban holds the name, not the person: with another invite they could join under another name. Their messages stay until their 48 hours are up, so take off any that need to go.

A profile card that breaks our covenant can be cleared on its own. Look the username up and clear its card: the card and its nameplate on Who’s online go, and the username, its password, pets, avatars, jobs and patches stay, so they can fill in a new one. Nothing tells them it was cleared, and nothing is kept about it, so tell them why yourself if you can.

The desk: reported messages

Direct messages on the CB are between two people with claimed usernames, and no moderator can read them. When one of them reports a message the other sent, a copy comes here: its words, its picture if it had one, who sent it and when, who reported it, and their note if they wrote one. A picture stays behind Show the picture until you press it. Nothing else about the conversation comes with it, and the person reported is not told.

Close a report once you have dealt with it, and it is deleted; one nobody closes goes after thirty days. A report stays even if either of them deletes their username. What happens next is our covenant’s and the desk’s above, which can look the username up: nothing here reaches into anybody’s messages, and the message itself goes from their conversation 48 hours after it was sent, whatever happens to the report.

Talking

The channel and the call in here are for any moderator: moderators sign on at the Community Center with a username on the moderators’ list and their own password. Anybody can come in and look around, and the radio waits outside the door for everybody else.

This browser is not signed on as a moderator this room is for, so the call in here is not yours to join. Moderators sign on at the front desk.

The call is run by 8x8’s Jitsi as a Service, and the server hands a pass into it to the moderators this room is for and nobody else. Camera and microphone start off, and while somebody is in the call we keep their name in it, until they leave. What goes where.

Why the street’s own light

Because this is the room that looks after the street, and the street is what lights it. There is no lamp in here: the shopfronts’ colours come up through the window and lie on the ceiling side by side, none of them mixed into the next, which is how the street is meant to stay. The radio on the desk is off while nobody is using it. Nothing in the room moves, at any setting.
