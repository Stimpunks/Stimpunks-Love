The Outskirts

The street has lights on it and somebody sweeping it. This does not. Keep going past the last lamp, past the point where the kerb gives up and becomes a verge, and there are premises out here too — the mystical, the eldritch, and whatever else keeps its own hours. It is always dark in The Outskirts. That is not a mood we are setting. It is the hours.

What is out here

  • You can only see as far as your headlights. Everything on this road is lit from one low, level direction and goes black an inch past the beam, which is why the signs below have a bright face and no back. It is also why this page tells you what is down each turning rather than showing you: out here, a sign is the honest thing and a picture would be a claim.
  • A sign with no road behind it is still a sign. Some of what is named here is not built. Those ones are lit and say so, plainly, on their own board — because an area that quietly listed rooms you cannot get into would be the street lying about its own architecture, and a website pretending it has no unfinished edges is doing something every actual outskirts knows better than.
  • Each premises is its own world and shares nothing with this road. The verge is cold and dead and painted by hand. What is down the turning is not, and the sign says what you are walking into so that walking in is a decision rather than a surprise.
  • Nobody was sent out here. Our town is stimpunks.love, which accepts everybody, so it has no outside to push anybody to — this road is a direction and not a verdict. What is down these turnings is here because this is where it is: a fort stands in a field, a screen stands in water, and neither of them is in exile. Out is not the same as out of favour.
  • Nothing out here moves on its own. The dial still works and each premises sets its own noise, but the road itself has no ambient layer at any setting. Headlights that swept would be a car going past, and there is no other car.

On the unlit signs

A board out here can stand lit with no road behind it, and say so. That is a state this road has and keeps: the alternative is a road that only ever appears fully formed and never admits to having been planned. The campgrounds past the other end of the street settled this first — an area has a state where a plot is spoken for and empty, and a website is usually the only kind of place that pretends otherwise. An unlit board is never a link, never a disabled control, and never hidden. A board that is lit and says not built is information. A board that quietly did nothing when you pressed it would be a broken page.

Job marker — a reflector set into the verge

The job: The far end of the street

Before the code: The Outskirts says you can only see a certain distance, and it is very specific about what is doing the lighting. What light is it?

Hint 1

It is in the first item of the list called What is out here.

Hint 2

It is not the moon and it is not a lamp. There are no lamps out there; that is the point of the place.

Last hint

You are in a car, and you have stopped.

Give me the answer

The answer is Headlights. Headlights — low, level, close, and coming from off the page, which is why every sign out there has a bright face and no back, and why the road goes black an inch past the beam. It is the one light no other world on this street uses, and it is the whole reason the area can say it is always dark without simply being unreadable: what the beam reaches, it reaches properly.

Taking the answer is a real way through this, not a lesser one. Nothing is watching and nothing is written down.
