## Settled

**Cavendish Coworking's Events door opens the room our recurring events meet in.** Ryan confirmed it, 2026-09-25: Solidarity Sesh, Infodumplings, Campfire Learn Together and Watering Hole Hangs meet in the Proton room named Events, and the door lists them with their times off our events page. The Hang Suites upstairs (Cave, Campfire, Watering Hole) are Ryan's words as he gave them, with two slips fixed and one norm corrected by him (camera and mic off), all written down in `data/coworking.json`.

**The CB is the first server-side code on this site, and it was allowed in on narrow terms, 2026-09-24.** Ryan's brief: a simple chat patterned after CB radio, a shared password rotated periodically, no alerts, the latest ten messages cleared daily, reachable only by signing on at a new Community Center, floating bottom right once you are on. What made it acceptable on a street whose privacy page said nothing is sent anywhere: the privacy page was rewritten *first*; `connect-src` went from `'none'` to `'self'` and no further, so a page can talk to this site and nobody else; it runs on Netlify, the host we already use, so no new party learns anything; nothing is stored but the channel itself, and no IP address, handle list or log line is kept by us; the pass is an HMAC keyed by the password, so rotating the password is the whole of revoking access; and `cb.js` is not downloaded by anybody who has not signed on. **The radio keeps its own clothes in every room**, like the dial, inside a shadow root so no room's rules can reach it, rather than taking each room's colours. **It stays out of the Healing Checkpoint**, Ryan's call: that room promises it writes nothing down about you, and `make-checkpoint.py` refuses the page if `data-cb="off"` goes. **The other rooms' promises were narrowed to their own objects** instead ("the cabinets store nothing and send nothing", "nothing is fetched from YouTube until"), because they had been saying something about the whole page that the radio makes untrue for a signed-on visitor. **No count anywhere**: not of people on the channel, not of messages, and no unread badge. **BASE is the one thing on the channel that is checked**, because a shared password means handles cannot be, and the moderators need a mark nobody else can wear.

**The Garden's roster is read out of `data/arrivals.json`, 2026-09-22.** Which sites are in the garden, in what order, under what names and at what addresses is not in `data/garden.json` and not in the tool — it comes from the list The Feed runs on, whose order is stimpunks.org's own published feeds page. Recorded because the alternative looks so reasonable: a garden is exactly the sort of thing you would arrange by eye, and a second copy of a name and a URL is a copy that goes stale in one of the two places. This way a bed cannot be invented, a site cannot be quietly left out, and re-sorting the garden would mean overruling a decision made somewhere else — the Jungle Room's rule about its cams. `data/garden.json` holds nothing but our own writing about each site.

**Every bed links out, so the garden is an opening and not a door, 2026-09-22.** It gets a gate in the wall between two shopfronts rather than a storefront in the grid, because a shopfront on this street promises a room you walk into and none of those sites is a room of ours. Recorded with the gate's own copy because the tempting version — mirroring a little of each site behind a door here — would be a worse copy of somewhere that already exists, and would quietly make this street the destination rather than the platform.

**The Garden is a planting plan and The Feed is a timetable, 2026-09-22.** Both rooms list every site we run, which makes The Feed the room this one could collapse into, so the division is structural rather than a matter of tone: a board carries a title, a date, a destination and the minute it was set, and a garden carries what a site is for, whose hands are in it, and nothing that changes when somebody publishes. That is why `make-garden.py` touches no network, has no pull step beside it, and **refuses a date in a bed**. Recorded because the friendly edit is real — the beds would look livelier with "updated last Tuesday" on them — and the moment one carries a date the room has to be re-pulled or start lying, which is the whole problem The Feed solved by saying the minute out loud.

**The garden's palette splits at the soil line, 2026-09-22, and it is the first contrast failure here with no palette fix.** `check-contrast.py` measured the two greens at 1.38 and 2.14 against the bed's earth, and because the greens are only 1.55 apart from each other no brown clears 3:1 against both of them at once — so nothing could be re-coloured. The drawings changed instead: green above the line, pale below it, so every root, rhizome, mycelial thread, spilled seed and blanched shoot is drawn in the path's own colour, which is also what a shoot looks like before it reaches the light. `make-garden.py` walks every coordinate of every green element and refuses one that crosses the line, and it was broken on purpose first — a stem two pixels under and a leaf circle whose edge dipped below — because a checker nobody has broken is a checker nobody should believe.

**The garden's drawings are told apart by form rather than by colour, 2026-09-22.** In the campgrounds each pitch gets one lit colour of its own and the next one needs a new kind of light, which is a grammar with a short future in it. A garden has as many habits of growth as you like, so every bed here is in the same inks and differs in what it is doing — running sideways, coming into flower, living on what it is joined to, being carried away. Recorded because it looks like an inconsistency between two areas and is the opposite: both rules exist so that adding the next one cannot be done by picking a tint.

**The garden sets no display face at all, 2026-09-22.** Every other world on this street has something that shouts, and a garden has no signage in it — only labels. So one text serif sets the garden's own name and the label on every bed in the same breath, a plain sans carries the notes, and the loud things in the room are the plants. Recorded because "this room needs a proper display face" is a reasonable-sounding note that would delete the only claim the room makes about type.

**The Garden Spider's caveats are ours and they stay, 2026-09-22.** That a spider is a guide to the garden and never a replacement for it, that its best answer is an entrance, that it can misstate and confidently produce things we did not say, and that every trail ends at a page a person wrote — all of that is quoted from our own `/ask/` page rather than paraphrased into something softer, with the ethics pages linked beside it and a way to reach a human instead. Recorded because this is the one page here recommending a generative tool, the friendly edit is the one that trims the hedging to make the button look better, and a Disabled people's organisation overselling an AI answer is precisely the failure we exist to name.

**The Feed's board is re-set by a timer, 2026-09-22, Ryan's call.** The open question was whether the board should age quietly or be re-pulled on a schedule, and the answer is a schedule: the **arrivals-board-daily** scheduled task runs `tools/daily-arrivals.sh` every morning, which pulls, redraws, gates on the fast offline checkers, and commits two paths. Recorded with its limits, because the limits are the interesting part. The timer is what keeps the room's sentence *fresh*; it is not what makes it *true* — the board says the minute it was set either way, and a day when every feed is unreachable ends with yesterday's board standing and a refusal in the report, which is the correct outcome rather than a failure. **What was not chosen: making the page fetch the feeds itself.** Not one of those hosts sends the header that would allow it, and the ways round it are a proxy we do not run and a third-party reader we would be handing visitors to without asking. Still outstanding and not ours to fix: the Knowledge System's `update-logs-daily` reads this repository's commits into Ryan's weekly changelog, and its `AUTOMATED` skip pattern is applied only to its own repo, so the daily board reset will show up there until a one-line change is made over there.

**The Feed's board is a snapshot and says so, 2026-09-21.** It cannot be anything else: not one of those hosts sends an `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` header, so the page cannot read a feed. Rather than hide that, the room is built out of it — a board in a station is set by a machine somebody switched on, at a time, and is a picture of the railway rather than the railway. Every plate carries the minute its own wire was read, the hall carries the minute the board was set, both generated from the same file the rows come from, and every feed is linked so the live thing is one press away. Recorded because this is `make-jungle.py`'s refused runtime standing on its head and the two look like opposite rules: a live camera has no length to give, so publishing one would be a number that goes quietly wrong; a board *has* a time it was set, so leaving it off would be the identical fault upside down. Both tools are keeping the same promise.

**A row on that board is a title, a date and a destination, 2026-09-21.** No summaries, no excerpts, no opening lines — and `make-arrivals.py` *refuses* a row carrying a summary field rather than merely not rendering one. A departures board does not read you the contents of the train, which is the metaphor and also the practical point: a room that pasted in everybody's first paragraph would be republishing other people's writing onto a surface it does not own, which is the opposite of sending somebody to them. Recorded with the refusal in the tool because the edit is a friendly one and it will arrive — the rows look bare, somebody helpfully pastes the words back, and the board has become a reader.

**Nothing on that board is ranked and no site's activity is added up, 2026-09-21.** No busiest, no quietest, no rows-per-week, no indication of which of our sites has been getting on with it. Recorded rather than left as taste, because a hall of boards is exactly the shape of thing that grows a league table and the reason not to is specific: most of the people publishing on those sites are Disabled, several of those sites are one person, and a board that scored them would turn publishing into a race between our own people. It is the pebbling cabinet's refusal of a tally arriving *between* sites. The running order is stimpunks.org's own, off its feeds page, for the Jungle Room's reason — a decision made somewhere else is not this room's to overrule.

**The flap's fold sits at the foot of the row and not through the letters, 2026-09-21.** A real Solari flap splits straight through the glyphs, and that is what it was twice: once as a hairline at 50%, which made the share card's row lettered NEVER read as a word struck through, and once as two faces meeting at 50%, which did the same thing to the *second line* of every wrapped title on a phone. Recorded because the reason is structural and will outlive the fix: a real board's rows are one line of fixed-width characters and cannot wrap, and ours carry the titles people actually gave their pages, so there is no proportion of a row that is not inside somebody's sentence. Authentic and misread is worse than slightly stylised and legible.

**Every toy in the Playhouse makes a noise, 2026-09-21.** Six of the ten shipped silent, and in a room where the others answer out loud the quiet ones read as broken rather than as deliberate — Ryan's report, and the reason the rule is enforced in `make-toys.py` rather than left as intent. Each noise is its own: no two alike, and none alike to the sound board's keys. The off switch is the awkward one and it gets a noise too, played after the stopping rather than before, because a button whose whole job is silence still has to confirm that it worked.

**The echo plays the last sound back as well as the last words, 2026-09-21, Ryan's idea.** It made the toy the thing its name says it is. The mechanism is that a toy's noise is registered as a factory rather than as a sound: called once to choose, returning a function that plays that choice. Recorded because the obvious implementation — call the toy's noise function again — silently advances anything that varies, so the echo of the stim box would be the *next* note up the scale and nothing would look wrong.

**Every control in the Playhouse answers where you pressed it, 2026-09-21.** The room narrated into one live region at the top of the page and nothing else moved, so pressing a toy from halfway down the page produced no visible result — Ryan reported the buttons as broken, having asked for them an hour earlier. The tiles hold their own answer now and the sound board has a strip under its keys; the box at the top keeps narrating and stays the only live region, with every local readout aria-hidden so nothing is announced twice. Recorded because the obvious alternative was making the box sticky, which fixes the visibility and not the connection: a bar at the top of the screen is still "up there somewhere", and what was missing was the control reacting.

**The Playhouse's toys are glossary entries wearing costumes, and each one says which, 2026-09-21.** Six more were added to fill the column beside the house rules, and the rule they were built to is that a toy is never just a gag: echolalia, the double empathy problem, safe food, spoon theory, infodumping, each named and linked under its own tile. The line sits outside the button because an anchor cannot live inside one, which turned out to be the right shape anyway. Recorded because the cheap version of this room is ten jokes, and the room is only worth having if pressing a thing and learning where it came from are the same action.

**The spoon drawer hands out sentences and not spoons, 2026-09-21.** The obvious build is a drawer that gives you a spoon every time and never runs out, which is generous and quietly deletes the limit that Christine Miserandino's essay exists to describe. What the drawer holds instead is the sentences — the things you can say to somebody across a table, which is what she invented spoons for and the half that gets forgotten. Nothing is counted and nothing is deducted. Recorded because the infinite-spoons version will look like kindness to whoever proposes it next.

**The double empathy telephone never says which account was right, 2026-09-21.** Two handsets, one encounter, two honest reports of it, and no adjudication anywhere in the toy. Damian Milton is named on the face of the tile. Recorded because a toy that resolved the misunderstanding would be a toy that had picked a side, and the idea it is wearing is precisely that neither side is the broken one.

**The off switch has no confirmation, 2026-09-21.** It stops every sound on the page at once and says so; there is no are-you-sure, nothing is saved and nothing is waiting. A stop button that asks whether you meant it is not a stop button, and this room can now stack nine sound-board keys and two recorded yells with no single way out. Recorded because "are you sure?" is what gets added to controls like this by default.

**The Hermitage's magenta and teal chairs were lightened rather than re-coloured, 2026-09-21, Ryan's call.** Their copy measured 2.79 and 3.38 against the 4.5 it needs, which had passed every run because `check-contrast.py` held three lighter hexes the page has never used and three dead `--bag-N` properties in `:root` kept the story plausible. The fix moves the lightness and nothing else: same hue, same saturation, all three chairs now sitting together at 4.80 to 4.82. Recorded because the obvious repair was to take the lighter palette the checker had been assuming, which would have put the teal at 7.34 and made one chair a different weight from its neighbours — in a room whose own comment says no two of them are the same colour because nobody furnishes a room like this by agreeing.

**The Pebble Board's slug ink was darkened by four thousandths of lightness, 2026-09-21.** `#8A6D2F` measured 4.41 on its own paper, nine hundredths short at 18px, which is the closest thing on this street to a pass and exactly the kind of number a pair list rounds the friendly way. Hue and saturation untouched.

**The sound board's mood keys have no faces on them, 2026-09-21.** Ryan asked for a bottom row of mood keys that toggle between happy, sad and angry noises. The obvious drawing for a mood key is a face and it is the first thing anybody sketches; a row of happy/sad/angry faces on a Disabled people's site is the emotion flashcard, which is the thing autistic self-advocates spend their lives being drilled with. A mood here is the shape of a noise — a hum that lifts, a hum that sags, a hum with a buzz in it — carried by a word, a drawn waveform and the sound itself. Recorded because the absence looks like something nobody got round to, and the friendly edit that fills it in is one small SVG.

**A mood key shows the mood it is about to play, 2026-09-21.** Press it and you get that noise, and the key then offers the next one. The alternative — the key naming what you just heard — was rejected because the face of a button should describe what pressing it does. The cost is that hearing the same mood twice takes three presses, which in a room whose other toy says "press it forty times" is a feature rather than a fault. The flourish keeps the colour of the mood that just played while the label has already moved on, which is why there are two attributes on that button and not one.

**Nothing on the sound board is counted, 2026-09-21.** No total, no streak, no memory between visits. A board of nine keys is exactly the shape of thing that grows a press counter, and the stim box one object over already counts its own presses as a joke about counting — the joke does not survive being repeated by a second object with a straight face. This is the pebbling cabinet's refusal of a tally and the guild's refusal of a score, recorded a third time because it arrives a third time.

**The board is three across where there is room and one column on a phone, 2026-09-21.** Held at three columns at every width it would have been a 3×3 at all sizes, which is what was asked for, and a third of 375px is about seventy pixels of text column — every blurb one word to a line. **Songs are quoted under queering.earth's policy, 2026-09-19, Ryan's call.** The launch position was that this site reproduces no lyric at all. **A borrowed voice is not ours to licence, 2026-09-19.** `LICENSE` excluded typefaces, songs, borrowed names and photographs, and said nothing about recordings. The grey placeholder keeps its square, because there is nothing in it to cut.

**Community photographs are excluded from the site licence, 2026-09-19.** This was the thing that had to be fixed before a single photo could be accepted, and it was not obvious: `LICENSE` excluded the typefaces, the songs and the borrowed names, and said nothing about photographs — so CC BY-SA 4.0 would have swept up pictures of Disabled community members and licensed strangers to reuse their faces commercially, for ever. **CC licences cannot be revoked.** A photo published under one could be removed from our server and nothing more, which would have made "it comes down when you say so" a promise the licence contradicted. The photographs now carry no onward licence at all, and that exclusion is what makes withdrawal mean something.

**The wall's promises are enforced by a tool, not by intentions, 2026-09-19.** `make-polaroids.py` refuses a photograph with no alt text, no named subject or no consent date, and refuses any file still carrying EXIF — which is where a phone records the location a picture was taken. Sending us a photo should not tell the internet where somebody lives. Withdrawal is deletion rather than a hidden flag: remove the entry, remove the file, run the tool, and the wall returns to grey with no "hidden" state left for a later session to un-hide by accident.

**We do not publish photographs of children, 2026-09-19.** Not because the consent cannot be given — a parent can give it — but because the child cannot withdraw it in ten years when it matters to them, and a permanent public photograph is not a decision somebody else gets to make for them. **Superseded 2026-09-21 — see “A young person gives their own consent” above.** The reasoning was sound and aimed at the wrong case: it was written against a parent deciding for a child, and it read as a ban on a teenager deciding for herself.

**The audio room reads new prose, not the glossary, 2026-09-19, Ryan's call.** The room promised "the glossary read aloud, in our own voices" and that promise could not be kept honestly. The monotropism entry is ~11,600 words and is mostly *curation*: the explanation in it belongs to Dinah Murray, Wenn Lawson and Mike Lesser, to the Monotropism Questionnaire's authors, and to Helen Edgar, whose opening section Ryan confirmed is hers. Reading it aloud would have been reading other people's words, and only one line in the whole entry — the one about infodumping as a love language — turned out to be ours. So the passages were written for this room instead, which keeps "our own prose only" without the constraint starving the room, and means nothing recorded here is anyone else's to clear. The promise was rewritten to match: selected passages, read by Ryan Boren.

**The superposition panel got a subject, 2026-09-19.** It had a mechanism and no content — A and B were not readings *of* anything, so pressing a button returned a sentence about having pressed it. Worse, the open state said "neither one wrong", asserting a symmetry this organisation does not hold: for the diagnostic encounter the room is about, the pathology reading is not an equally valid alternative. Monotropism supplied the content, because it is a real case of two readings of the same behaviour — the deficit description and the monotropic one — and they are *not* symmetric. The captions now say so: only one of them knows it is a measurement. Pressing a panel button highlights its passage and never starts audio.

**No autoplay, enforced rather than promised.** Every embed is a press-to-play facade that makes no request until pressed. The Playhouse's noises are synthesised through Web Audio, which cannot start without a user gesture — the platform enforces the consent model rather than us intending it. Verified in a browser on 2026-09-19: zero iframes and zero YouTube requests on load.

**The panel stitches playback; the collapse buttons still do not, 2026-09-19, Ryan's call.** Highlighting a passage was all the panel did, and the expectation it set was that choosing a measurement would let you *hear* that measurement. It does now: A plays what monotropism is and then how the clinic writes it down, B plays it and then what it looks like from inside, open plays the whole argument ending on the line that resolves it. One press starting several files is still press-to-play — the visitor asked for the sequence — and what keeps that honest is the label, which states the count and the duration before the press rather than after it. A bare play triangle would have been the same behaviour and a worse promise. The collapse buttons deliberately start nothing: choosing a measurement and listening to one are separate acts, which also means somebody can collapse to A and read it where they cannot play sound. "A love language" is in no sequence, because it carries no panel state and folding it into an argument about diagnosis would flatten it. Missing recordings are skipped and counted in the label rather than stalling the run, so the feature works at two recordings out of five exactly as it will at five.

**Twelve typefaces, self-hosted.** 322 KB across 16 woff2 files, all OFL, all under `fonts/` with their origins recorded in `fonts/_sources.json`. Reading a page here makes no third-party request, which is the same rule queering.earth and starstuff.earth follow. `@font-face` is lazy, so a room downloads only the faces it sets.

**Hand-authored HTML, four generators.** The pages are the artifact and are committed as written; only the jukebox list, its credits table, the sitemap/llms.txt pair and the CSP hash are generated. This follows queering.earth's split — hand-write the pages, generate the derived things — and avoids the failure where somebody hand-edits an HTML file that a build step then overwrites.

**Both jukebox surfaces read one file.** `data/jukebox.json` feeds the dancefloor and the credits page through two tools, so they cannot disagree about who made what. A credits page that contradicts the room it credits is worse than no credits page.

**Recordings are stripped of their metadata, and the tools refuse otherwise, 2026-09-19.** Enid's wall refuses a photograph carrying EXIF, because sending us a picture should not tell the internet where somebody lives. Audio was never held to that, and when the first yell arrived it carried the recording device, its OS build and the exact second — as did all five readings **already published**, which nobody had checked. A voice identifies a person as surely as a face does and the rule should have been the same from the start. All six files were re-muxed rather than re-encoded, so the audio is byte-for-byte the same sound and the durations prove it. `make-readings.py` and `make-yells.py` now refuse a file that still carries any.

**The yell button takes community voices, and the house rule writes itself, 2026-09-19, Ryan's call.** The button synthesises a shout and always will: with nothing recorded the toy still works, which is why the synth stays rather than being replaced. Recordings layer on top, one chosen at random, and the synth is also the fallback if a file will not play. The Playhouse says "the noises are synthesised here, not fetched from anywhere" — true until the first recording, so that sentence is **generated from the same data as the button**. A claim the site makes about itself should not depend on somebody remembering to edit it. A yell follows the polaroid wall's process rather than a second one invented for it: a named yeller, a consent date, withdrawal by deletion, and no onward licence. The credit is spoken when the button is pressed, the way Chairy names the sayings that are not ours.

**Chairy's sayings moved into a file so they could carry their sources, 2026-09-19.** Five lines fitted in a `data-lines` attribute; twenty-eight do not, and an attribute cannot record where a line came from. That mattered more than the tidiness: **two of the sayings are not ours.** "Nothing About Us Without Us" is a motto of the self-advocacy movement, credited on our own page to Michael Masutha and William Rowland of Disabled People South Africa in 1993, and "Design for Real Life" is a book by Eric A. Meyer and Sara Wachter-Boettcher, which our own philosophy page already credits. Chairy speaks the credit aloud with those two rather than leaving it to the liner notes, because a talking chair that passes a movement slogan off as a house line is precisely the failure this site spends a page arguing against. Every URL was read out of the Knowledge System mirror's frontmatter rather than constructed, for the same reason the jukebox ids were.

**A dead embed is checkable, and the check stays out of the deploy sequence, 2026-09-19.** Bad Cop / Bad Cop's "Warriors" died some time after launch and nothing noticed, because a dead facade renders exactly like a live one until a reader presses it. `tools/check-jukebox.py` now asks YouTube. It is deliberately not part of the run-before-deploy sequence: it is the only tool here that needs the network, and one that fails on a train would either block a deploy or train everyone to skip the whole list. Two tests that look like they should work do not, and both cost time to rule out — oEmbed answers 200 with the correct title for a video that will not play, and loading an embed URL directly fails identically for every video because there is no origin on the request. Only the watch page's `playabilityStatus` separates them. Channel renames are reported and never fail the run, because a rename is not a broken page.

**Print resets colour universally, not by class list, 2026-09-19.** The zine's sentence has two halves and both were false. The print block reset backgrounds on `.door, .panel, .card, .qcard, .toy, .rules, .keeps`, so anything not on that list kept its colour: the ransom note printed in yellow, cyan and red, and `.pullquote` and the dark credit boxes kept a `#15121f` ground while the rules below forced their text to `#000` — 1.14:1, invisible on paper. What was lost was the most prominent quotation on each sheet and the whole attribution box, on the page that argues attribution is the cheapest thing you can do. Colour and text are now reset *together* and universally, because doing either alone breaks the other — white grounds under the pullquote's `#b9b2a3` citation would read 1.9:1 just as badly in the other direction. `check-print.py` holds an allowlist of the inks allowed to reach paper, in the same spirit as check-contrast's named pairs: a new ink has to be looked at by a person. Border colours are deliberately outside the reset, which is why two near-neutral tints are on the list, and why the Stoop still prints violet borders — no claim is made about that room.

**The credits print a name we chose; the file also records YouTube's, 2026-09-19, Ryan's call.** Two channels read badly verbatim. Merge Records is now called "Merge Records on YouTube", which would have printed "on YouTube, via Merge Records on YouTube", and the replacement Warriors id sits on an auto-generated channel spelled "Bad Cop Bad Cop - Topic", without the slashes the band uses. Both were normalised for display. The reason this is not simply overwriting the provenance is that `channel_verbatim` keeps YouTube's exact name alongside, and `check-jukebox.py` compares against *that* rather than the printed one. Without the second field every deliberate wording choice would be reported as drift on every run, and a report that is always noisy is a report nobody reads — which would have cost us the one signal that part of the tool exists to give. The credits are for a reader; the verbatim field is for the checker.

**The site moved to stimpunks.world on 2026-09-23, and took the name with it.** Ryan's call: the site had been called Stimpunks World in conversation as it grew past a street, so the domain was registered to match and made primary, and the name moved too — every title, `og:site_name`, the feed, the favicon, the manifest, the share cards and the wordmark, whose pink second line now reads `.world`. stimpunks.love stays registered as an alias and `_redirects` sends it path-for-path to the new domain, with the force flag, because Netlify serves an alias with a 200 rather than redirecting it (Cavendish's lesson from 2026-09-15). Three things deliberately did **not** move. **The feed's guids** stay minted on stimpunks.love and are marked `isPermaLink="false"`, because a reader decides what is new by guid and rewriting them would have republished the whole changelog as news. **The changelog's old entries** still say stimpunks.love where that was true at the time — history is not rewritten to match a rename. **The repository and the Netlify site** are still `Stimpunks-Love` / `stimpunks-love`, which are identifiers nobody reads; renaming them would change the deploy wiring for no reader's benefit. The agent skill moved to `stimpunks-world`, with a redirect from the old path.

**The domain went live 2026-09-19, and the netlify.app rule came on the same day.** stimpunks.love resolves on Netlify DNS with www and http both 301ing to the apex. The two lines in `_redirects` that send `stimpunks-love.netlify.app` to the custom domain were uncommented only after checking that the apex answered 200 — before that they would have pointed the one working hostname at one that did not exist. The site is deployed from GitHub on a read-only Netlify deploy key plus a push webhook, the same wiring as every sibling; neither is created by `createSiteInTeam`, so both had to be added by hand and the first push after launch silently did not deploy until the webhook existed.

**The changelog is a page, not a file.** Ryan's rule, 2026-09-15: all of our sites publish a changelog. This one is `changelog.html` and it lists what is *not* built alongside what is.

**The feed is generated from the changelog, 2026-09-19.** The open question was whether to have one at all: `index.html` briefly linked `/feed.xml` and the link was removed rather than the file invented, because a hand-maintained RSS file drifts the first week nobody remembers it. `tools/make-feed.py` resolves that the same way the sitemap did — the feed is derived from `changelog.html`, so the two cannot disagree. RSS 2.0 with an `atom:link` self-reference, which is the shape queering.earth's feed already uses. Every `<h2>` now carries a stable id and the tool refuses without one, because that id is the item's permalink and a feed whose guids move republishes every old entry into somebody's reader as if it were new. One limit is accepted rather than papered over: entries sharing a day share a `pubDate`, since the alternative is inventing times that are not true, and a repo that will not guess a video's songwriter should not guess a timestamp either.
