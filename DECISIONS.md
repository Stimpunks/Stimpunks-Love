Settled choices and open ones, each with the reasoning, so the same question is not re-litigated in three weeks. Hand-authored. One line per paragraph.

## Open

**The meeting hall links to Stoat and frames nothing, 2026-09-29.** Ryan asked whether Stoat, where the community is moving from Discord, can be built into the street. Open: the channel list in the room is our field guide's Discord list in outline and should be brought up to date once the move is done; stimpunks.org's community and join pages still say Discord. Settled the same day, Ryan's call: **the CB's password is asked for in the meeting hall**, and the front desk says so.

**Opposite generator checks, one promise, 2026-09-20.** `make-jungle.py` refuses a cam that carries a runtime; `make-den.py` refuses a track that lacks one. **A live cam's facade must NOT carry a runtime, 2026-09-20.** Every other press-to-play control on this street says how long it runs before you press it, and `make-latibulum.py` refuses a track without one — that number is the whole reason the promise works, because it is what lets somebody decide. **The zine table holds issues, not a feed, 2026-09-19, Ryan's call.** The room invited submissions and had nowhere to put them. **One song is printed in full, on permission, 2026-09-23, Ryan's call.** "Up All Night", by Josephmooon with lyrics by Ronan Boren, a Stimpunk, is published whole in The Small Hours, because Stimpunks helped produce it and holds permission to distribute it and its lyrics. **Songs are quoted under queering.earth's policy, 2026-09-19, Ryan's call.** The launch position was that this site reproduces no lyric at all. That read as caution and was in fact a third rule smuggled in beside attribution and contrast, never argued for on its own. queering.earth had already settled the question properly in [how we quote](https://queering.earth/how-we-quote#songs), and the rule here is now that policy *by reference* rather than restated, so the two sites cannot drift apart: a line or two and never the chorus on its own, the songwriter named separately from the performer, the recording named with its release and year, and a link to a copy the rightsholder is paid for. The argument that decides it is not the fair-use one, it is the access one — "go and listen to the recording" is an audio-only requirement, and a page whose point can only be reached by hearing something excludes Deaf and hard-of-hearing readers outright. On a site published by a Disability foundation that is not a trade worth making in order to look careful. Pink Pony Club still quotes nothing, which is now a choice about that room rather than a prohibition.

**The dial defaults from the device and can be turned UP.** The alternative — capping intensity at whatever `prefers-reduced-motion` asks for — treats a media query as more authoritative than the person using the browser. It is not. The OS setting is the *starting* position; an explicit choice always wins, in both directions. This is the design's central argument and the reason accessibility here is a feature rather than a tax.

**Gentle hides nothing.** No content sits behind an intensity level. A reduced mode that drops material is a worse site wearing a politer name, and on a site by Disabled people for Disabled people it would be the ordinary insult rendered in CSS.

**The panel measures a term as well as a pattern, 2026-09-19, Ryan's call.** With one term, "collapse to A" was unambiguous; with two it is not. **Developmental Pace is read from the glossary; Monotropism could not be, 2026-09-19.** The two terms were cut differently and the difference is not stylistic. **A borrowed voice is not ours to licence, 2026-09-19.** `LICENSE` excluded typefaces, songs, borrowed names and photographs, and said nothing about recordings. Our own readings are our work and are covered. A reading by anybody else is not: the words may be ours, the voice is theirs, and CC BY-SA cannot be revoked — so somebody who lends us their voice has to be able to take it back. This is the polaroid problem arriving one step *before* the thing it protects rather than one step after, which is the first time on this site that has happened.

**A photograph keeps its own shape, 2026-09-19.** The polaroid plate was square with `object-fit: cover`, which crops — and `polaroids.html` promises in as many words that we will not crop anybody. **Community photographs are excluded from the site licence, 2026-09-19.** This was the thing that had to be fixed before a single photo could be accepted, and it was not obvious: `LICENSE` excluded the typefaces, the songs and the borrowed names, and said nothing about photographs — so CC BY-SA 4.0 would have swept up pictures of Disabled community members and licensed strangers to reuse their faces commercially, for ever. **CC licences cannot be revoked.** A photo published under one could be removed from our server and nothing more, which would have made "it comes down when you say so" a promise the licence contradicted. The photographs now carry no onward licence at all, and that exclusion is what makes withdrawal mean something.

**The wall's promises are enforced by a tool, not by intentions, 2026-09-19.** `make-polaroids.py` refuses a photograph with no alt text, no named subject or no consent date, and refuses any file still carrying EXIF — which is where a phone records the location a picture was taken. Sending us a photo should not tell the internet where somebody lives. Withdrawal is deletion rather than a hidden flag: remove the entry, remove the file, run the tool, and the wall returns to grey with no "hidden" state left for a later session to un-hide by accident.

**We do not publish photographs of children, 2026-09-19.** Not because the consent cannot be given — a parent can give it — but because the child cannot withdraw it in ten years when it matters to them, and a permanent public photograph is not a decision somebody else gets to make for them. **Superseded 2026-09-21 — see “A young person gives their own consent” above.** The reasoning was sound and aimed at the wrong case: it was written against a parent deciding for a child, and it read as a ban on a teenager deciding for herself.

**The panel stitches playback; the collapse buttons still do not, 2026-09-19, Ryan's call.** Highlighting a passage was all the panel did, and the expectation it set was that choosing a measurement would let you *hear* that measurement. **Twelve typefaces, self-hosted.** 322 KB across 16 woff2 files, all OFL, all under `fonts/` with their origins recorded in `fonts/_sources.json`. Reading a page here makes no third-party request, which is the same rule queering.earth and starstuff.earth follow. `@font-face` is lazy, so a room downloads only the faces it sets.

**Hand-authored HTML, four generators.** The pages are the artifact and are committed as written; only the jukebox list, its credits table, the sitemap/llms.txt pair and the CSP hash are generated.
