# Decisions — Stimpunks.Love

Settled choices and open ones, each with the reasoning, so the same question is not re-litigated in three weeks. Hand-authored. One line per paragraph.

----

## Open

**The polaroid wall has one photograph on it.** Ryan sent the first, of himself, with the description and caption in his own words — which is the process working rather than being bypassed by the person who wrote it. The terms are on `polaroids.html`, the licence excludes photographs, and `tools/make-polaroids.py` refuses one missing its alt text, its named subject, its consent date, or still carrying EXIF. Open: whether anybody else sends one. One photo is a wall that has started, not a wall that is finished.

**Developmental Pace is written and unrecorded, and Helen Edgar is the intended reader.** Five passages are cut, on the page, and wired to the panel; none is recorded. Helen agreed to read them (confirmed by Ryan, 2026-09-19). Her name is on the page in the future tense — "to be read by" — until the files exist, because a credit in the past tense for passages nobody has read yet would be untrue about a named person; the tool switches tense on its own when the first recording lands. Her reading will carry no onward licence, unlike ours. Open: only the recordings.

**The guild has no bed in it.** A guild is an inn as well as a board and this one is a board and a counter. When the room over it exists it will be about rest as something owed rather than earned, which means it will owe a great deal to The Nap Ministry and Tricia Hersey and must credit them in its own copy rather than in a footnote. Open: the room. Not open: whether it looks like the job board. It must not — a bedroom drawn as a filing system would make rest look like an administrative category, which is the precise thing that argument exists to refuse. The board says the bed is missing rather than leaving a gap, because a guild with no inn reads as an oversight.

**Helen Edgar has not seen the candle stub yet.** Ryan's call put a job marker on her windowsill (see Settled below) and it is drawn in her own declared colours, but it is the second thing this repository has added to her room on its own initiative and she has not been asked. Open: her answer. If she would rather it went, it goes, and the Faery Yurt becomes the one room on the street with no job in it — which is a smaller cost than redecorating somebody else's room over their head.

**Nobody has claimed Your Room.** The empty storefront has terms, a route in, and no tenant. Open until somebody takes the keys — and it should stay visibly empty rather than be filled by us, because an empty room we decorate ourselves is scenery, which is the exact thing `your-room.html` says it is not.

**The last line of the course is an empty slot.** Issue #2 of the zine has a box shaped for one or two lines of *Rainbow Shiner* by Ex Hex and nothing in it. What is missing is not permission, it is the credit the policy requires. Every fact the policy asks for is now on the page (Ryan, 2026-09-19): written by Laura Harris, Betsy Wright and Mary Timony, who are Ex Hex themselves so no separate performer credit is owed, from *It's Real*, released 22 March 2019, linked to the band's own Bandcamp. Outstanding: only the line. It stays visibly empty the way Enid's polaroids stay grey, because a slot that shows what it is waiting for is more use than one quietly closed up.

## Settled

**The Adventurer's Guild is the one tidy room, 2026-09-21.** Ryan asked for the trope — the guild with the inn, the job board, jobs ranked by difficulty class. The room it got is a bureaucracy: manila, printer's ink, hairline rules, ruled rows, a class in the margin, a stamp, square corners, no gradient and no shadow anywhere. That is the joke rather than a crack in the site's argument, and it only works while it is the only one: a street whose architecture is that nothing matches, with one room where somebody has filed everything. Recorded because tidiness is the instinct this repository refuses everywhere else, and the next person will read §26 as permission rather than as a costume. It also needed keeping off two neighbours that were each one edit away — the Hermitage has a sun in it and this has no light source at all, and the Doomscroll is one unbroken unruled roll where this is made of rules.

**The job marker is never the play button, 2026-09-21.** The brief's own example starts from one: play a song in The Den, get a code. It is a spindle adapter on the den floor instead, and the question is what makes you read the tile. Three reasons, any one of them enough. `love-embed.js` replaces a pressed facade with a bare `<div class="facade">`, so anything the button was wearing evaporates — the fault that shipped Swaying Sweetgrass's players at 0×0 the day before. A play button that also does a second thing hands a popup to somebody who only wanted the song. And gating a quest behind a press would put it behind consenting to a third-party frame, which is the consent this street spends a mechanism protecting. Recorded because "why doesn't playing the song just give you the code" is a reasonable question with three answers.

**Nothing on the board is scored, 2026-09-21.** No total, no percentage, no streak, no rank you climb, nothing that expires; the board stamps a job and that is the whole of the bookkeeping. Enforced in `make-guild.py` rather than left as intent, because a job board is exactly the shape of thing that grows a score and the change that adds one is friendly — somebody thinks a board looks bare without a progress bar. A score beside a walking tour of a Disabled people's site turns a wander into a workload. The difficulty class is defined as how far you walk, enumerated in the tool and measured in doors, so it says nothing about the person doing it. The refusal's first run was a false positive on "a book it points at", and the pattern was narrowed rather than given an exception — check-counts.py's own first-run lesson, happening a second time.

**The markers are on the floor of every room, 2026-09-21.** They could have been scattered at a different depth in each room, which is more like a hunt. They are all at the foot of their page and the board says so out loud. On a Disabled people's site the hunt should be the walk between rooms rather than a search within one, and a marker at an unpredictable depth makes finding it a test of eyesight and of a mouse. What is never the same twice is the marker itself: one drawing per room, in that room's own colours, refused by the tool if a room has none. Recorded because uniform placement looks like laziness until you know it was the accessible choice.

**The quest codes are not secret and the tool says so, 2026-09-21.** Every code is printed in plain text in the markup of a public page; that is the entire mechanism. The board holds a checksum of each rather than a copy, purely so that opening the source of the *board* is not a spoiler sheet for every room at once. It is a courtesy and not a lock and `make-guild.py` says so where it computes it. Recorded because the obvious next move is to "harden" it, and there is nothing here that wants hardening — it is a scavenger hunt on a website, and every marker carries a give-me-the-answer way out anyway.

**The candle stub on the Faery Yurt's windowsill is ours, 2026-09-21, Ryan's call.** Asked directly, because that room is Helen Edgar's design and this repository does not redecorate it. Three options were put: a question-gated marker out on the campground's own board, touching nothing of hers; a marker inside the yurt, drawn in her palette and written up; or leaving pitch 01 out and saying why on the board. Ryan chose the marker in the room. It is in the liner notes beside the one colour of hers this repository has changed, for the same reason that colour is in four places — a change to a contributor's room should be visible somewhere other than a diff — and she has the final say on whether it stays. Recorded because the option that touched nothing of hers was the safer one and was not chosen, which is exactly the kind of call that gets re-litigated.

**The escape from a question is a `<details>` and not a script, 2026-09-21.** The code, the hints and the give-me-the-answer way out all work with JavaScript off; `quest.js` only adds the answer box, which ships hidden so a page without scripts shows no dead control. `check-quests.py` refuses an escape that is not a native disclosure. Recorded because collapsing the whole marker into one scripted panel is tidier code and would put the only thing guaranteeing nobody is stuck behind a script — and because the copy tells people that taking the answer is a real way through rather than a lesser one, which is a promise the markup has to keep.

**The chapter readings of *Braiding Sweetgrass* are doors and not screens, 2026-09-21, Ryan's call.** Asked directly, because the provenance changes what the room is. Ryan's brief pointed at a twenty-one-chapter playlist of the book read aloud; it is uploaded by The Anarchist Audio Library and is an unauthorised reading of a book that is in copyright — Milkweed, 2013, Kimmerer alive, publishing, and narrating the authorised audiobook herself. Every chapter plays and every chapter embeds, so nothing in the machinery would have refused them. The option offered alongside was her own voice on authorised channels. The answer was both: her voice as the fire, the readings named and linked as a way out, provenance stated. Enforced in `make-sweetgrass.py` rather than left as intent, because the change that breaks it is friendly — the doors look broken next to the presses and somebody flips one word. Recorded because "why isn't this one embedded like the others" is a reasonable question with an answer.

**The Elder in the 21-strand teaching is not named, 2026-09-21.** The teaching is transcribed from *Sweet Grass Teaching*, published by Shawenim Abinoojii Inc., matched to that video by reading its transcript rather than guessed at. The video names nobody: not in the description, not on the channel, not at the end. So the page credits the organisation and says out loud that the speaker is unnamed there. Recorded because the obvious repair is to find a name somewhere and put it on, and a name attached to somebody else's teaching on the strength of a search is worse than no name — the herbarium's refusal to guess at a binomial, applied to a person.

**One of the four Kimmerer passages is published unverified and says so, 2026-09-21.** Three are verbatim on our own pages and were checked against the Knowledge System mirror. The Cajete and goldenrod-and-asters passage is on no page of ours; archive.org's search-inside was unreachable and Google Books rate-limited every attempt, so it could not be confirmed against the book from here. It is published on Ryan's word, and `data/sweetgrass.json` and the liner notes both say which passages were checked and which was not. Recorded because the tidy thing is to let four quotations look alike, and this site's whole habit is that they must not.

**Swaying Sweetgrass is lit from behind, 2026-09-21.** It is the sixth warm room on a street where "they are all cosy, they should feel the same" has already been refused five times, and the first one with an actual fire in it. What keeps it separate is that the light is the low sun *through* the grass rather than a flame inside the room, so nothing has a lit face; the fire is painted in the room's own two golds and given no colour of its own. Recorded because the obvious improvement — warm the room up around the fire — would collapse it into the Faery Yurt two pitches away.

**VideoPress is the third framed origin, 2026-09-21.** Ryan's ten seconds of backlit grass is already published by us on stimpunks.org's Nature entry, so it is framed from where it lives rather than copied onto this site — the Faery Yurt's wireless rule. The allowlist was in three hand-kept places and shipping this proved that does not hold: `_headers`' CSP line is generated by `make-csp.py` from a literal of its own, so the hand edit that added videopress was reverted on the next run, worked on a dev server that serves no headers, and was refused live. `love-embed.js`'s `ORIGINS` array is the single source now and both tools read it. Recorded because self-hosting a ten-second clip of our own looks like the obvious simplification and would start a category this site does not have.

**The Mopery is three pages and not one or four, 2026-09-21, Ryan's call.** Asked directly, because a library, a screen, a reading nook, a deck of cards and a feed of poems can be split several ways and each costs a different amount. The line drawn: the library, the parlour screen and the Raven nook are zones of one place, the way the Hermitage's cave, campfire and workshop are; the deck and the scroll are their own rooms, because a deck of cards and a roll of newsprint are not a library and pretending otherwise would give three unrelated things one visual world. Recorded because "why isn't the Raven its own page" is a reasonable question with an answer.

**No content notes on the Mopery's shelf, 2026-09-21, Ryan's call.** Asked directly, with the dark romance on that shelf named in the question, and the option of a tool that refused a book without one offered alongside. The answer was no. Written into `data/mopery.json` beside the notes rather than only here, because a shelf with a Keri Lake and a RuNyx on it and no content notes reads like an oversight to the next person, and the next person is the one who would quietly add them.

**One scan is published and one is only linked, 2026-09-21.** The Library of Congress's facsimile of *The Raven* in Poe's hand carries "reproduction prohibited without permission" along the foot of its own first leaf — a 1949 Yale exhibition printing — and loc.gov answers a script with a bot check rather than its rights statement, so that notice is the only rights information anybody here has read. The poem is free; that object is somebody else's. The 1865 Tyndale printing Ryan found in the SKS inbox is published by the Internet Archive under the Public Domain Mark 1.0, which is an explicit statement, so three of its leaves are in the room. Recorded because the two look like the same kind of thing and are not, and because the obvious tidy-up is to publish both now that one is fine.

**Doré's plates are placed by us, 2026-09-21, Ryan asked for the art.** The 1884 edition printed the poem straight through and put all twenty-six engravings after it, each captioned with the lines it illustrates. Reproducing that layout would have been faithful and a worse way to read, so each plate stands at the lines its own caption quotes and the page says the placing is ours. The captions are verbatim, including where they disagree with the text underneath them — matching them to stanzas failed on exactly two, which is how the difference was found. Recorded because "the captions don't match the poem, fix them" is the obvious repair and it is the wrong one.

**The oracle deck asks and never answers, 2026-09-21.** No reversals, no spread, no positions, no reading of several cards together, nothing that could be assembled into a statement about somebody's life. Enforced in `make-oracle.py` rather than left as intent, because the change that breaks it is a friendly one: somebody decides a question is a bit thin and writes what the card *means*. On a Disabled people's site a deck that told somebody how their life was going to go would be doing the thing the rest of these pages exist to refuse.

**The Doomscroll's bar is public domain everywhere, 2026-09-21.** Not US public domain: author dead more than seventy years, checked against the current year. It cost the scroll its best opening item — Eliot's *The Hollow Men*, free in America and in copyright across much of Europe until the 2030s. Recorded because the entry is sitting there unused and re-adding it is a one-line change that looks like an improvement.

**A young person gives their own consent, 2026-09-21, Ryan's call.** The flat no-children rule would have pulled a named teenage volunteer's photograph off the Pebble Board — an active Stimpunks volunteer who appears on our other sites, does the work, and had said yes. That is the rule failing at the case it was supposed to protect: it was written against *a parent consenting on a child's behalf*, and a self-advocate consenting for herself is the opposite situation wearing the same age. Keeping her off the page would have handed the credit for her own activities to the adults standing beside her, which is what *nothing about us without us* exists to refuse, and that motto has no lower age bound. What carries over unchanged: the consent must be the young person's own and a parent's does not substitute for it; withdrawal is deletion and works for them exactly as for everybody; and somebody too young to give that consent themselves is still not published, with no parental override. Rewritten in `polaroids.html`, `data/polaroids.json` and `CLAUDE.md`; the 2026-09-19 decision below is marked superseded rather than deleted, because the old reasoning is why the new one has to say which case it is about.

**Nothing in the herbarium is determined to species, 2026-09-20.** A pressed sheet with a Latin name on it is the most authoritative-looking object this site could publish, which makes it the worst place for a guess. Half the specimens are groups rather than species anyway (moss, lichen, fern, mycelium). The enforcement is a shape check with an allowlist of English words rather than a list of Latin endings, because the endings version leaked three times and each fix moved it closer to firing on prose — recorded because "just add the suffix" is the obvious repair and it is the wrong one.

**The watering hole's three benches are one room and not three pages, 2026-09-20.** Three of the herbarium's sheets cite pieces sitting on the Star Stuff table two feet away. Splitting them would have made each bench tidier and broken the only thing that makes a workshop a workshop. Recorded because the room is long and splitting it is the obvious next tidy-up.

**The Hermitage is daylit, 2026-09-20, Ryan's call.** Asked directly, because it inverts the street: every other room is a dark ground with light applied and this is a lit ground with things standing in it. The reason it is not merely a preference is that the room runs on solar panels and a cabin that reads as midnight would be arguing against itself. Consequence: the first set of contrast pairs on this site where the dangerous inks are the pale accents rather than the dim greys, and the first room whose drawing had to be lightened to pass rather than darkened.

**The laptop is a live frame, 2026-09-20, Ryan's call.** The alternative was a convincing picture of a screen. A real same-origin iframe means the street inside the cave is the street, and it cost two things worth recording: `frame-ancestors` went from `'none'` to `'self'` and `X-Frame-Options` from `DENY` to `SAMEORIGIN` — the only loosening in that policy, with every other origin still refused — and `hermitage.js` has to refuse to nest, because a laptop you can walk back into the room through produces a laptop inside a laptop and the third one is a page that will not stop loading. It still waits to be pressed even though no third party is involved, since an unasked copy of the site means a second dial, doubled fonts and a screen reader walking everything twice.

**The cave's books are curated, not sampled, 2026-09-20, Ryan's call.** The brief said a random selection from the Knowledge System. Random at page load is a page whose content changes with nobody editing it and whose links nobody has checked; random at build time is just a shuffled fixed list with the provenance harder to see. So the shelf is a fixed set in `data/hermitage.json`, every title one stimpunks.org already argues from, every citing page read out of the mirror's frontmatter, and every borrow and buy link requested and answered before publication. A borrow link is required; a buy link is not.

**The watering hole is visibly unfinished, 2026-09-20.** Its three tables — a herbarium, a solar bench, a table of Star Stuff — are named on the page with the benches in and nothing laid out on them, rather than omitted until ready. Same idiom as the empty storefront and the zine's empty line: a slot that shows what it is waiting for is more use than one quietly closed up. Open: all three tables.

**A pitch's drawing belongs to the field, not to the pitch, 2026-09-20.** The campground's board now carries a picture on each taken plot, and the obvious way to draw them — each one in its own room's palette, a little preview of what is inside — would have turned the board into a contact sheet and the field into a design system, which is the thing this area exists to refuse. So both are drawn in the field's inks with weight rather than tint doing the hierarchy, and what separates them is one lit colour each doing opposite work: the yurt emits and the hermitage catches. An open plot gets no drawing at all. Recorded because the next pitch will arrive with a palette of its own and the temptation will be to use it.

**A hermitage's one lit window is a screen, 2026-09-20.** It would have been warmer and easier to put a candle in it, next to a yurt that has one. The pitch's argument is connected solitude — [lone wolfing](https://stimpunks.org/2023/06/18/lone-wolfing-the-joys-of-autistic-solitude/) is choosing the reach rather than being unreachable — and the window is the only place a drawing can say that. It is also the structural break from the tent beside it. Recorded because it reads as a colour choice and is not one.

**Pitch 03 is taken and has no door, 2026-09-20.** The Solarpunk Hermitage is Ryan's and is a big build; the drawings are stage one. Rather than publish a half-built room or point a listing at nothing, the plot is `.pitch--raising`: solid post, sign, drawing, no link, and a who-line that says the cabin is going up. A campground has this state. Open: the room itself — a cave of red velvet and books, a campfire ring of bean bags around a shared screen, and a workshop with three tables.

**The Den is an easter egg, and "subtle" is a visual setting only, 2026-09-20.** Ryan's brief: keep the signposting subtle yet discoverable, patterned after The Chappell's. So it is the same shape as that door — a short line and one sentence at the foot of the parent room — turned right down, with no storefront on the street and no announcement anywhere. What it is *not* is hidden: `.den-door` is an ordinary focusable link with real text, in the tab order, at full size, and `make-sitemap.py` lists the page like every other. On a Disabled people's site a secret that only a sighted mouse user can find is not a secret, it is an exclusion, and that floor is written into the stylesheet so the next person to make it subtler knows where it is.

**A subroom that shares its parent's NAME and its parent's COLOUR still shares nothing else, 2026-09-20.** The Den is Graceland's Jungle Room hanging off ours, which is the hardest version of the subroom problem this site has hit — The Chappell at least did not sound like Pink Pony Club. The separation is structural rather than a change of swatch: upstairs the green is the dark ground with the light falling from above and cream type on it; down here the room is brown, panelled and lamplit, the carpet is the brightest thing in it, and the track list is set *dark on the green*. Same hue, opposite job. Plus: open and curved versus low, closed and notched; a swelling serif versus a flat slab. Written down because "it is the room behind the other one, it should match" is the excuse this street has now refused more times than any other.

**Opposite generator checks, one promise, 2026-09-20.** `make-jungle.py` refuses a cam that carries a runtime; `make-den.py` refuses a track that lacks one. That looks like an inconsistency and is the same rule: tell somebody what they are pressing before they press it. A live camera has no runtime to give, so a number there would be invented reassurance; a song has one, so leaving it off withholds the fact that lets somebody decide. Recorded because the two files sit next to each other and the obvious tidy-up is to make them agree.

**A live cam's facade must NOT carry a runtime, 2026-09-20.** Every other press-to-play control on this street says how long it runs before you press it, and `make-latibulum.py` refuses a track without one — that number is the whole reason the promise works, because it is what lets somebody decide. The Jungle Room inverts it: a live camera does not stop, so the only runtime available to store is the length of whichever stream happens to be up today, which is wrong tomorrow and looks authoritative in the meantime. `make-jungle.py` therefore refuses a cam that *has* one. The label says the true thing instead — it runs until you close it — and the room states in its own copy that the picture may be darkness, rain, an empty waterhole or a cam between streams. Recorded because the reflex arriving from three other data files is to add the field back, and because the two rules look like a contradiction until you say out loud what the number is for.

**Playing and embedding are two different permissions, 2026-09-20.** `love-embed.js` validates an id and builds an iframe; it has no way to know the owner has switched embedding off, so the frame loads and shows a refusal where the picture should be — a button somebody presses and presses that never becomes a video, which is the failure `make-chappell.py` was written to prevent, arriving through a door nothing was watching. Two Jungle Room cams are like that. They are published as a way out to YouTube, dressed as a door rather than as a window, saying why. `check-jukebox.py` reads `playableInEmbed` off the same fetch as the status now, for every facade on the street; a cam already marked `link` is expected to fail it and is not reported.

**A dead cam is replaced by whoever curates the events page, not from here, 2026-09-20.** Monterey Bay Aquarium's Live Jelly Cam and CritterVision's Critter Cam both answered UNPLAYABLE. An id chosen in this repo would be a cam nobody chose, so both were left dark, named in the room's own copy rather than making the gallery quietly shorter than the page it came from, and kept in `data/jungle.json` with the date. **The process then ran the same day, twice**: Ryan sent replacements for both, and both were checked before they went up, because a replacement nobody checked is the original bug with a fresh id on it. The jelly cam (`eQ_foBERmzA`) came back as a screen — same title, same channel, status OK, `playableInEmbed` true. The critter cam (`0cn-Y3Mfx3g`) came back as a **link**: it plays and it was live when checked, but `playableInEmbed` is false, on the same channel id as the one it replaces, with oEmbed refusing it the same way — CritterVision has embedding off across the channel, so no id from them would come out differently, and a screen there would show a refusal where the picture should be. Each entry carries a `replaced` note recording what was measured. Nothing is dark now, and the room's counts and the grammar around them are generated rather than typed.

**No single design system, 2026-09-19, Ryan's call.** The brief asked for "a bold and unique design unlike our other sites", and the first answer offered was a set of themes to choose between. That was the wrong shape: picking a winner would have produced another consistently-styled site, just louder. What makes this site structurally unlike the others is that it has *no* shared look — the street is the system and the rooms contradict each other. All six ship. Recorded because the instinct to unify will return, and because it is correct everywhere else.

**Attribution stays; the rest of "careful" goes.** Ryan listed attribution among the disciplines to drop. It was kept over that objection and he agreed: the other three are aesthetic habits, and attribution is a licence. The compromise is that credits are *loud* rather than hidden — margin scrawls, a liner-notes page — so keeping them costs the site nothing in attitude. A direct consequence: every borrowed line on the street carries its source, including in the loudest room.

**Contrast is not negotiable; palette discipline is.** These were conflated in the brief and they are separable. Clashing colours are the point; illegible ones are an accident. `tools/check-contrast.py` enforces WCAG 1.4.3 across 66 pairs and caught two real failures on its first run, both of which would have shipped.

**The zine table holds issues, not a feed, 2026-09-19, Ryan's call.** The room invited submissions and had nowhere to put them. The two shapes on offer were a growing wall of contributions generated from a data file, the way the jukebox works, or discrete numbered issues hand-set in the room's own faces. The wall scales and would have turned the room into a feed, which is the one thing a photocopied object is not. Issues won: `zine-table.html` stays issue #1 and each new one is its own page, which `make-sitemap.py` then refuses to let us publish unlisted. A submission is a page in the next issue; a whole storefront is Your Room, and the two asks are now answered separately instead of by the same link.

**The one-sheet promise was false for a day and is now enforced.** The room says every page in it prints to one sheet and that the print stylesheet is not a joke. Nobody had measured it: issue #1 printed to three sheets, because the two-column row wrapped at paper width and `a[href^="http"]::after` expanded every URL inline. (An early estimate of 2.11 was made by measuring content height in a browser; the real figure came from rendering a PDF, because `break-inside: avoid` pushes a block whole onto the next page rather than filling the previous one. Estimating a paginated number understates it.) §13 now carries the only room-specific print rules on the street and declares `@page` itself, so the promise does not depend on a print dialog we do not control. A claim the site makes in its own copy is a claim somebody has to check, so `tools/check-print.py` now renders every `room-zine` page to PDF and counts the sheets. It finds its own pages by reading the room class rather than from a list, so a future issue is covered without anybody remembering to add it.

**Songs are quoted under queering.earth's policy, 2026-09-19, Ryan's call.** The launch position was that this site reproduces no lyric at all. That read as caution and was in fact a third rule smuggled in beside attribution and contrast, never argued for on its own. queering.earth had already settled the question properly in [how we quote](https://queering.earth/how-we-quote#songs), and the rule here is now that policy *by reference* rather than restated, so the two sites cannot drift apart: a line or two and never the chorus on its own, the songwriter named separately from the performer, the recording named with its release and year, and a link to a copy the rightsholder is paid for. The argument that decides it is not the fair-use one, it is the access one — "go and listen to the recording" is an audio-only requirement, and a page whose point can only be reached by hearing something excludes Deaf and hard-of-hearing readers outright. On a site published by a Disability foundation that is not a trade worth making in order to look careful. Pink Pony Club still quotes nothing, which is now a choice about that room rather than a prohibition.

**The dial defaults from the device and can be turned UP.** The alternative — capping intensity at whatever `prefers-reduced-motion` asks for — treats a media query as more authoritative than the person using the browser. It is not. The OS setting is the *starting* position; an explicit choice always wins, in both directions. This is the design's central argument and the reason accessibility here is a feature rather than a tax.

**Gentle hides nothing.** No content sits behind an intensity level. A reduced mode that drops material is a worse site wearing a politer name, and on a site by Disabled people for Disabled people it would be the ordinary insult rendered in CSS.

**The panel measures a term as well as a pattern, 2026-09-19, Ryan's call.** With one term, "collapse to A" was unambiguous; with two it is not. The panel gained a picker above the collapse row — *measuring: monotropism / developmental pace* — rather than a panel per term or a panel that only ever served the first one. That keeps Barad's point rather than working around it: the instrument and the object both matter, and the visitor chooses both. It also scales to a third term with no redesign, which the alternatives did not. Highlighting is done in JavaScript rather than by a descendant selector, because the match now depends on two choices and static CSS cannot name a term it has not met.

**Developmental Pace is read from the glossary; Monotropism could not be, 2026-09-19.** The two terms were cut differently and the difference is not stylistic. Monotropism's entry is ~11,600 words and mostly curation — exactly one line in it was ours — so its passages were written for this room. Developmental Pace is ~1,800 words and about 39% our own prose, and that prose carries the argument, so its passages are *cut from the entry* — which is what the room promised in the first place. The rule is not "always write new" or "always lift": it is that we read our own words and nobody else's, and which of those is possible depends on the entry.

**A borrowed voice is not ours to licence, 2026-09-19.** `LICENSE` excluded typefaces, songs, borrowed names and photographs, and said nothing about recordings. Our own readings are our work and are covered. A reading by anybody else is not: the words may be ours, the voice is theirs, and CC BY-SA cannot be revoked — so somebody who lends us their voice has to be able to take it back. This is the polaroid problem arriving one step *before* the thing it protects rather than one step after, which is the first time on this site that has happened.

**A photograph keeps its own shape, 2026-09-19.** The polaroid plate was square with `object-fit: cover`, which crops — and `polaroids.html` promises in as many words that we will not crop anybody. Nobody noticed until a real photograph arrived and the implementation had to meet the promise. The frame adapts to the picture now. The grey placeholder keeps its square, because there is nothing in it to cut.

**Community photographs are excluded from the site licence, 2026-09-19.** This was the thing that had to be fixed before a single photo could be accepted, and it was not obvious: `LICENSE` excluded the typefaces, the songs and the borrowed names, and said nothing about photographs — so CC BY-SA 4.0 would have swept up pictures of Disabled community members and licensed strangers to reuse their faces commercially, for ever. **CC licences cannot be revoked.** A photo published under one could be removed from our server and nothing more, which would have made "it comes down when you say so" a promise the licence contradicted. The photographs now carry no onward licence at all, and that exclusion is what makes withdrawal mean something.

**The wall's promises are enforced by a tool, not by intentions, 2026-09-19.** `make-polaroids.py` refuses a photograph with no alt text, no named subject or no consent date, and refuses any file still carrying EXIF — which is where a phone records the location a picture was taken. Sending us a photo should not tell the internet where somebody lives. Withdrawal is deletion rather than a hidden flag: remove the entry, remove the file, run the tool, and the wall returns to grey with no "hidden" state left for a later session to un-hide by accident.

**We do not publish photographs of children, 2026-09-19.** Not because the consent cannot be given — a parent can give it — but because the child cannot withdraw it in ten years when it matters to them, and a permanent public photograph is not a decision somebody else gets to make for them. **Superseded 2026-09-21 — see “A young person gives their own consent” above.** The reasoning was sound and aimed at the wrong case: it was written against a parent deciding for a child, and it read as a ban on a teenager deciding for herself.

**The audio room reads new prose, not the glossary, 2026-09-19, Ryan's call.** The room promised "the glossary read aloud, in our own voices" and that promise could not be kept honestly. The monotropism entry is ~11,600 words and is mostly *curation*: the explanation in it belongs to Dinah Murray, Wenn Lawson and Mike Lesser, to the Monotropism Questionnaire's authors, and to Helen Edgar, whose opening section Ryan confirmed is hers. Reading it aloud would have been reading other people's words, and only one line in the whole entry — the one about infodumping as a love language — turned out to be ours. So the passages were written for this room instead, which keeps "our own prose only" without the constraint starving the room, and means nothing recorded here is anyone else's to clear. The promise was rewritten to match: selected passages, read by Ryan Boren.

**The superposition panel got a subject, 2026-09-19.** It had a mechanism and no content — A and B were not readings *of* anything, so pressing a button returned a sentence about having pressed it. Worse, the open state said "neither one wrong", asserting a symmetry this organisation does not hold: for the diagnostic encounter the room is about, the pathology reading is not an equally valid alternative. Monotropism supplied the content, because it is a real case of two readings of the same behaviour — the deficit description and the monotropic one — and they are *not* symmetric. The captions now say so: only one of them knows it is a measurement. Pressing a panel button highlights its passage and never starts audio.

**No autoplay, enforced rather than promised.** Every embed is a press-to-play facade that makes no request until pressed. The Playhouse's noises are synthesised through Web Audio, which cannot start without a user gesture — the platform enforces the consent model rather than us intending it. Verified in a browser on 2026-09-19: zero iframes and zero YouTube requests on load.

**The panel stitches playback; the collapse buttons still do not, 2026-09-19, Ryan's call.** Highlighting a passage was all the panel did, and the expectation it set was that choosing a measurement would let you *hear* that measurement. It does now: A plays what monotropism is and then how the clinic writes it down, B plays it and then what it looks like from inside, open plays the whole argument ending on the line that resolves it. One press starting several files is still press-to-play — the visitor asked for the sequence — and what keeps that honest is the label, which states the count and the duration before the press rather than after it. A bare play triangle would have been the same behaviour and a worse promise. The collapse buttons deliberately start nothing: choosing a measurement and listening to one are separate acts, which also means somebody can collapse to A and read it where they cannot play sound. "A love language" is in no sequence, because it carries no panel state and folding it into an argument about diagnosis would flatten it. Missing recordings are skipped and counted in the label rather than stalling the run, so the feature works at two recordings out of five exactly as it will at five.

**Twelve typefaces, self-hosted.** 322 KB across 16 woff2 files, all OFL, all under `fonts/` with their origins recorded in `fonts/_sources.json`. Reading a page here makes no third-party request, which is the same rule queering.earth and starstuff.earth follow. `@font-face` is lazy, so a room downloads only the faces it sets.

**Hand-authored HTML, four generators.** The pages are the artifact and are committed as written; only the jukebox list, its credits table, the sitemap/llms.txt pair and the CSP hash are generated. This follows queering.earth's split — hand-write the pages, generate the derived things — and avoids the failure where somebody hand-edits an HTML file that a build step then overwrites.

**Both jukebox surfaces read one file.** `data/jukebox.json` feeds the dancefloor and the credits page through two tools, so they cannot disagree about who made what. A credits page that contradicts the room it credits is worse than no credits page.

**Recordings are stripped of their metadata, and the tools refuse otherwise, 2026-09-19.** Enid's wall refuses a photograph carrying EXIF, because sending us a picture should not tell the internet where somebody lives. Audio was never held to that, and when the first yell arrived it carried the recording device, its OS build and the exact second — as did all five readings **already published**, which nobody had checked. A voice identifies a person as surely as a face does and the rule should have been the same from the start. All six files were re-muxed rather than re-encoded, so the audio is byte-for-byte the same sound and the durations prove it. `make-readings.py` and `make-yells.py` now refuse a file that still carries any.

**The yell button takes community voices, and the house rule writes itself, 2026-09-19, Ryan's call.** The button synthesises a shout and always will: with nothing recorded the toy still works, which is why the synth stays rather than being replaced. Recordings layer on top, one chosen at random, and the synth is also the fallback if a file will not play. The Playhouse says "the noises are synthesised here, not fetched from anywhere" — true until the first recording, so that sentence is **generated from the same data as the button**. A claim the site makes about itself should not depend on somebody remembering to edit it. A yell follows the polaroid wall's process rather than a second one invented for it: a named yeller, a consent date, withdrawal by deletion, and no onward licence. The credit is spoken when the button is pressed, the way Chairy names the sayings that are not ours.

**Chairy's sayings moved into a file so they could carry their sources, 2026-09-19.** Five lines fitted in a `data-lines` attribute; twenty-eight do not, and an attribute cannot record where a line came from. That mattered more than the tidiness: **two of the sayings are not ours.** "Nothing About Us Without Us" is a motto of the self-advocacy movement, credited on our own page to Michael Masutha and William Rowland of Disabled People South Africa in 1993, and "Design for Real Life" is a book by Eric A. Meyer and Sara Wachter-Boettcher, which our own philosophy page already credits. Chairy speaks the credit aloud with those two rather than leaving it to the liner notes, because a talking chair that passes a movement slogan off as a house line is precisely the failure this site spends a page arguing against. Every URL was read out of the Knowledge System mirror's frontmatter rather than constructed, for the same reason the jukebox ids were.

**A dead embed is checkable, and the check stays out of the deploy sequence, 2026-09-19.** Bad Cop / Bad Cop's "Warriors" died some time after launch and nothing noticed, because a dead facade renders exactly like a live one until a reader presses it. `tools/check-jukebox.py` now asks YouTube. It is deliberately not part of the run-before-deploy sequence: it is the only tool here that needs the network, and one that fails on a train would either block a deploy or train everyone to skip the whole list. Two tests that look like they should work do not, and both cost time to rule out — oEmbed answers 200 with the correct title for a video that will not play, and loading an embed URL directly fails identically for every video because there is no origin on the request. Only the watch page's `playabilityStatus` separates them. Channel renames are reported and never fail the run, because a rename is not a broken page.

**Print resets colour universally, not by class list, 2026-09-19.** The zine's sentence has two halves and both were false. The print block reset backgrounds on `.door, .panel, .card, .qcard, .toy, .rules, .keeps`, so anything not on that list kept its colour: the ransom note printed in yellow, cyan and red, and `.pullquote` and the dark credit boxes kept a `#15121f` ground while the rules below forced their text to `#000` — 1.14:1, invisible on paper. What was lost was the most prominent quotation on each sheet and the whole attribution box, on the page that argues attribution is the cheapest thing you can do. Colour and text are now reset *together* and universally, because doing either alone breaks the other — white grounds under the pullquote's `#b9b2a3` citation would read 1.9:1 just as badly in the other direction. `check-print.py` holds an allowlist of the inks allowed to reach paper, in the same spirit as check-contrast's named pairs: a new ink has to be looked at by a person. Border colours are deliberately outside the reset, which is why two near-neutral tints are on the list, and why the Stoop still prints violet borders — no claim is made about that room.

**The credits print a name we chose; the file also records YouTube's, 2026-09-19, Ryan's call.** Two channels read badly verbatim. Merge Records is now called "Merge Records on YouTube", which would have printed "on YouTube, via Merge Records on YouTube", and the replacement Warriors id sits on an auto-generated channel spelled "Bad Cop Bad Cop - Topic", without the slashes the band uses. Both were normalised for display. The reason this is not simply overwriting the provenance is that `channel_verbatim` keeps YouTube's exact name alongside, and `check-jukebox.py` compares against *that* rather than the printed one. Without the second field every deliberate wording choice would be reported as drift on every run, and a report that is always noisy is a report nobody reads — which would have cost us the one signal that part of the tool exists to give. The credits are for a reader; the verbatim field is for the checker.

**The domain went live 2026-09-19, and the netlify.app rule came on the same day.** stimpunks.love resolves on Netlify DNS with www and http both 301ing to the apex. The two lines in `_redirects` that send `stimpunks-love.netlify.app` to the custom domain were uncommented only after checking that the apex answered 200 — before that they would have pointed the one working hostname at one that did not exist. The site is deployed from GitHub on a read-only Netlify deploy key plus a push webhook, the same wiring as every sibling; neither is created by `createSiteInTeam`, so both had to be added by hand and the first push after launch silently did not deploy until the webhook existed.

**The changelog is a page, not a file.** Ryan's rule, 2026-09-15: all of our sites publish a changelog. This one is `changelog.html` and it lists what is *not* built alongside what is.

**The feed is generated from the changelog, 2026-09-19.** The open question was whether to have one at all: `index.html` briefly linked `/feed.xml` and the link was removed rather than the file invented, because a hand-maintained RSS file drifts the first week nobody remembers it. `tools/make-feed.py` resolves that the same way the sitemap did — the feed is derived from `changelog.html`, so the two cannot disagree. RSS 2.0 with an `atom:link` self-reference, which is the shape queering.earth's feed already uses. Every `<h2>` now carries a stable id and the tool refuses without one, because that id is the item's permalink and a feed whose guids move republishes every old entry into somebody's reader as if it were new. One limit is accepted rather than papered over: entries sharing a day share a `pubDate`, since the alternative is inventing times that are not true, and a repo that will not guess a video's songwriter should not guess a timestamp either.
