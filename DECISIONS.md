# Decisions — Stimpunks.World

Settled choices and open ones, each with the reasoning, so the same question is not re-litigated in three weeks. Hand-authored. One line per paragraph.

## Open

**Direct messages, 2026-10-18.** Ryan asked how big a job direct messages would be, as the community moves off Discord, and then to build a first version with Claude's suggested defaults: claimed only, off until switched on, requests, blocking, 48 hours, not end-to-end encrypted, report to the desk, no counts or receipts, one-to-one. **Built**: the Direct messages part of `netlify/cb/lib.mjs`, `/cb/dm` and `/cb/dm/reports`, a **Messages** part on the radio between Channel and Watching together, **Message** under the card in somebody's profile, *When somebody messages me* among the notification switches (`notifyDm`), reported messages on the desk in the Moderators' room, `tools/check-messages.py`, `privacy.html#messages` and `cb-guide.html#messages`. **Claude's choices, each Ryan's to change**: the switch governs who can **start** a conversation, and one already going goes on when it is switched off; **answering a request accepts it**, as well as Accept; whoever asked can write nothing more until it is answered, and can edit it or take it back; **a request with no answer goes at its 48 hours**, and they can ask again; **a conversation with nothing left in it is forgotten**, so two people who talk every few days are asked again after two quiet days (the alternative is an accepted list kept per person, a record of who talks to whom, which is why it was not built); **blocking empties the conversation for both** and is kept by the name blocked, so it survives the name being deleted and claimed again, and a blocked person is told only "not taking messages from you just now"; **a report keeps the reporter's username**, so a moderator can follow up, and **stays thirty days even if either username is deleted**, so deleting a username cannot erase a report about it; moderators have **no way into a conversation**, on the desk or anywhere; at most 40 conversations a person, 200 blocks, 200 reports on the desk, 2,000 characters a message with the channel's Markdown; **words only**: no pictures, stickers or reactions. **Open**: **safeguarding first**: the street has young volunteers on it, and private messages between adults and young people are where grooming happens on other platforms; requests, blocking and reports help, but who may message whom is a question for Ryan and the board, and the CB knows nobody's age; **pictures** (the channel's redraw would carry over, and the picture would have to be bound to its conversation the way a channel picture is bound to its room); whether **a moderator's message should say BASE** in a conversation, as it does now; **group messages**, which rooms and quiet rooms partly cover; and whether the desk should be able to **see whether a username has messages switched on**, which it cannot now.

**Props, 2026-10-18.** Ryan's brief: a means of giving a user props, after the forums' Props & Gratitude category, as a Props tab in profiles where folks read and write props for the user; before new props are posted the user can Confirm or Reject them; and a switch for showing the tab, like the other tabs. A prop is a sentence or short paragraph thanking, acknowledging or celebrating someone, usually for helping them or the community. **Built**: the Props part of `netlify/cb/lib.mjs`, `/cb/props` and `/cb/props/of`, a Props tab last among a profile's tabs, a Your props section in Profile under the profile card, `tools/check-props.py`, and `privacy.html#props`. **Claude's choices, each Ryan's to change**: **writing a prop needs a claimed username** (the forums' `forumReader`), because a prop carries the writer's name on somebody else's profile and an unclaimed handle is anybody's; reading needs only a pass, like the other tabs. **The switch starts off, and while it is off nobody can give that person props** and their tab says it is not open, the other tabs' rule; confirmed props are kept and come back when it is switched on. **Rejecting tells nobody**, but a writer sees their own prop while it waits, with Take it back, so a rejected one visibly stops waiting; the alternative was hiding waiting props from their writer, which the one-at-a-time rule would have leaked anyway. **One prop from each writer waits for a person at a time**, 20 waiting and 100 up on a tab, at most 500 characters with basic Markdown; the writer can give more once one is confirmed. The owner takes any prop off, the writer takes theirs back, waiting or up, and the base takes down any that is up but never sees what is waiting; nothing is kept about any of it. **No time and no count**, held by `check-props.py`: no date on a prop, no number on the tab or beside Waiting for you. Props a person wrote for others **stay up when they delete their username**, under their name, the way a forum post does; the mark of the claimed username that wrote each one means a name claimed again cannot take back its old owner's props. **Built the same evening**: **a notification when props arrive**, the follow-up first left open here, because until then a person only learned of waiting props by opening Profile and nothing on the radio may light up for them. It is a fifth push switch, *When somebody gives me props*, off until switched on (`notifyProps` in `lib.mjs`, called by `/cb/props/of` once a prop is waiting). **Claude's choices, each Ryan's to change**: it tells only a device switched on as the person the props are for, whose pass is still that claimed username's own, so a device switched on before the name was claimed is dropped rather than told; it says who wrote them and, under the existing words switch (relabelled *Show the words of a message, a post or props* rather than given a sixth), their first 180 characters, and never how many are waiting; **the writer is never told anything**, not whether a notification went, nor whether it was read, confirmed or rejected; each props notification stands alone rather than replacing the last, so a second writer's name is not lost; and pressing it opens the street at `/#cb=props`, where the radio opens Profile at Your props, switched on and at Normal for that page only, leaving what it remembers alone. It opens a new tab on the front page rather than the page the person is on, because moving a page they are on (a film playing, a forum topic whose address is its place) is worse than one more tab. **Open**: whether **deleting a username should take down the props it wrote** on other people's tabs, which would need an index of who wrote where; whether **a moderator should be able to clear a whole Props tab** from the desk, as they clear a card; and whether the Props tab should come **first** among the tabs rather than last.

A direct consequence: every borrowed line on the street carries its source, including in the loudest room.

**Contrast is not negotiable; palette discipline is.** These were conflated in the brief and they are separable. Clashing colours are the point; illegible ones are an accident. `tools/check-contrast.py` enforces WCAG 1.4.3 across 66 pairs and caught two real failures on its first run, both of which would have shipped.

**The zine table holds issues, not a feed, 2026-09-19, Ryan's call.** The room invited submissions and had nowhere to put them. The two shapes on offer were a growing wall of contributions generated from a data file, the way the jukebox works, or discrete numbered issues hand-set in the room's own faces. The wall scales and would have turned the room into a feed, which is the one thing a photocopied object is not. Issues won: `zine-table.html` stays issue #1 and each new one is its own page, which `make-sitemap.py` then refuses to let us publish unlisted. (Narrowed 2026-10-07: a page can now say on its own `<body>` that it is unlisted, for rooms you have to know the address of. An issue of the zine is not one of those, and stays in the sitemap.) A submission is a page in the next issue; a whole storefront is Your Room, and the two asks are now answered separately instead of by the same link.

**The one-sheet promise was false for a day and is now enforced.** The room says every page in it prints to one sheet and that the print stylesheet is not a joke. (An early estimate of 2.11 was made by measuring content height in a browser; the real figure came from rendering a PDF, because `break-inside: avoid` pushes a block whole onto the next page rather than filling the previous one. A claim the site makes in its own copy is a claim somebody has to check, so `tools/check-print.py` now renders every `room-zine` page to PDF and counts the sheets. **One song is printed in full, on permission, 2026-09-23, Ryan's call.** "Up All Night", by Josephmooon with lyrics by Ronan Boren, a Stimpunk, is published whole in The Small Hours, because Stimpunks helped produce it and holds permission to distribute it and its lyrics. That is not a loosening of the quoting policy below, which still governs every other song on the street: it is permission, written down in `data/small-hours.json` and required by `make-small-hours.py`, and the words are excluded from this site's CC BY-SA licence in `LICENSE`, because permission to distribute is not permission to relicense. The access argument below is the reason it matters: a page playing a song about Autistic insomnia should carry its words for anybody who cannot hear it. The recordings stream from the band's own site and are not hosted here.

**Songs are quoted under queering.earth's policy, 2026-09-19, Ryan's call.** The launch position was that this site reproduces no lyric at all. That read as caution and was in fact a third rule smuggled in beside attribution and contrast, never argued for on its own. queering.earth had already settled the question properly in [how we quote](https://queering.earth/how-we-quote#songs), and the rule here is now that policy *by reference* rather than restated, so the two sites cannot drift apart: a line or two and never the chorus on its own, the songwriter named separately from the performer, the recording named with its release and year, and a link to a copy the rightsholder is paid for. The argument that decides it is not the fair-use one, it is the access one — "go and listen to the recording" is an audio-only requirement, and a page whose point can only be reached by hearing something excludes Deaf and hard-of-hearing readers outright. On a site published by a Disability foundation that is not a trade worth making in order to look careful. Pink Pony Club still quotes nothing, which is now a choice about that room rather than a prohibition.

**The dial defaults from the device and can be turned UP.** The alternative — capping intensity at whatever `prefers-reduced-motion` asks for — treats a media query as more authoritative than the person using the browser. It is not. The OS setting is the *starting* position; an explicit choice always wins, in both directions. This is the design's central argument and the reason accessibility here is a feature rather than a tax.

**Gentle hides nothing.** No content sits behind an intensity level. A reduced mode that drops material is a worse site wearing a politer name, and on a site by Disabled people for Disabled people it would be the ordinary insult rendered in CSS.

**The panel measures a term as well as a pattern, 2026-09-19, Ryan's call.** With one term, "collapse to A" was unambiguous; with two it is not. The panel gained a picker above the collapse row — *measuring: monotropism / developmental pace* — rather than a panel per term or a panel that only ever served the first one. That keeps Barad's point rather than working around it: the instrument and the object both matter, and the visitor chooses both. It also scales to a third term with no redesign, which the alternatives did not. Highlighting is done in JavaScript rather than by a descendant selector, because the match now depends on two choices and static CSS cannot name a term it has not met.

**Developmental Pace is read from the glossary; Monotropism could not be, 2026-09-19.** The two terms were cut differently and the difference is not stylistic. Monotropism's entry is ~11,600 words and mostly curation — exactly one line in it was ours — so its passages were written for this room. Developmental Pace is ~1,800 words and about 39% our own prose, and that prose carries the argument, so its passages are *cut from the entry* — which is what the room promised in the first place. The rule is not "always write new" or "always lift": it is that we read our own words and nobody else's, and which of those is possible depends on the entry.

**A borrowed voice is not ours to licence, 2026-09-19.** `LICENSE` excluded typefaces, songs, borrowed names and photographs, and said nothing about recordings. Our own readings are our work and are covered. A reading by anybody else is not: the words may be ours, the voice is theirs, and CC BY-SA cannot be revoked — so somebody who lends us their voice has to be able to take it back. This is the polaroid problem arriving one step *before* the thing it protects rather than one step after, which is the first time on this site that has happened.

**A photograph keeps its own shape, 2026-09-19.** The polaroid plate was square with `object-fit: cover`, which crops — and `polaroids.html` promises in as many words that we will not crop anybody. Nobody noticed until a real photograph arrived and the implementation had to meet the promise. The frame adapts to the picture now. The grey placeholder keeps its square, because there is nothing in it to cut.

**Community photographs are excluded from the site licence, 2026-09-19.** This was the thing that had to be fixed before a single photo could be accepted, and it was not obvious: `LICENSE` excluded the typefaces, the songs and the borrowed names, and said nothing about photographs — so CC BY-SA 4.0 would have swept up pictures of Disabled community members and licensed strangers to reuse their faces commercially, for ever. **CC licences cannot be revoked.** A photo published under one could be removed from our server and nothing more, which would have made "it comes down when you say so" a promise the licence contradicted. The photographs now carry no onward licence at all, and that exclusion is what makes withdrawal mean something.

**The wall's promises are enforced by a tool, not by intentions, 2026-09-19.** `make-polaroids.py` refuses a photograph with no alt text, no named subject or no consent date, and refuses any file still carrying EXIF — which is where a phone records the location a picture was taken. Sending us a photo should not tell the internet where somebody lives. Withdrawal is deletion rather than a hidden flag: remove the entry, remove the file, run the tool, and the wall returns to grey with no "hidden" state left for a later session to un-hide by accident.

**We do not publish photographs of children, 2026-09-19.** Not because the consent cannot be given — a parent can give it — but because the child cannot withdraw it in ten years when it matters to them, and a permanent public photograph is not a decision somebody else gets to make for them. **Superseded 2026-09-21 — see “A young person gives their own consent” above.** The reasoning was sound and aimed at the wrong case: it was written against a parent deciding for a child, and it read as a ban on a teenager deciding for herself.

**The superposition panel got a subject, 2026-09-19.** It had a mechanism and no content — A and B were not readings *of* anything, so pressing a button returned a sentence about having pressed it. Worse, the open state said "neither one wrong", asserting a symmetry this organisation does not hold: for the diagnostic encounter the room is about, the pathology reading is not an equally valid alternative. Monotropism supplied the content, because it is a real case of two readings of the same behaviour — the deficit description and the monotropic one — and they are *not* symmetric. **No autoplay, enforced rather than promised.** Every embed is a press-to-play facade that makes no request until pressed. The Playhouse's noises are synthesised through Web Audio, which cannot start without a user gesture — the platform enforces the consent model rather than us intending it. Verified in a browser on 2026-09-19: zero iframes and zero YouTube requests on load.

**The panel stitches playback; the collapse buttons still do not, 2026-09-19, Ryan's call.** Highlighting a passage was all the panel did, and the expectation it set was that choosing a measurement would let you *hear* that measurement. **Twelve typefaces, self-hosted.** 322 KB across 16 woff2 files, all OFL, all under `fonts/` with their origins recorded in `fonts/_sources.json`. Reading a page here makes no third-party request, which is the same rule queering.earth and starstuff.earth follow. `@font-face` is lazy, so a room downloads only the faces it sets.

**Hand-authored HTML, four generators.** The pages are the artifact and are committed as written; only the jukebox list, its credits table, the sitemap/llms.txt pair and the CSP hash are generated.
