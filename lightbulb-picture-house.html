A picture house on the street

The Lightbulb Picture House

Every screening is a relaxed screening. The house lights stay up.

A lot of us found out who we were from a video. Somebody on a screen described a life that turned out to be ours — the textures, the plans that fell apart, the conversations that went sideways for no reason anybody would name — and something switched on. This picture house is named for that moment, and for the short film on its first screen that calls it a lightbulb moment.

Most of what is showing here was made by the people it is about. It is on two screens, both of them our own playlists, and every film on the first one has a card on the rack saying who made it, how long it runs and what is in it, before you press anything.

Every screening is a relaxed screening

  • The house lights stay up. Nobody sits in the dark who did not ask to.
  • The captions are on. Every screen here asks for them before the film starts, and you can switch them off in the player.
  • Nothing plays until you press it, nothing plays before the film you chose, and the volume is yours.
  • Move, stim, rock, pace, flap, hum, sit on the floor, lie across three seats. Nobody shushes anybody.
  • Bring your ear defenders, or borrow the pair hung on the seat in the second row.
  • Come in late and leave early. You can walk out in the middle of a film and walk back in at the end of it, and nobody will ask why.
  • Every film on the rack tells you what is in it before you press play — how long it runs, and anything you might want to know first, from a panic attack to a sponsor segment. Screen Two says how long each film runs and has no rack yet.

Nothing in this building is blue. Every April, autism charities run by parents and professionals light landmarks up blue, and autistic people have been saying for years what they think of it — one of them, below, better than we could. The bulbs here are warm on purpose, there is no puzzle piece anywhere in the place, and the ornament over the screen is the gold infinity — Autistic UK’s, from Au, the chemical symbol for gold.

Awareness says: Donate to the awesome organizations that have all the pretty puzzle pieces and all the pretty blue lights!

Amy Sequenzia, I Don’t Want Your Awareness, as quoted in our Acceptance entry

Screen One Animated Introductions to Autism, ADHD, and Neurodiversity

Short animations, most of them made by the people they are about. The rack underneath has a card for every one.

No runtime on a whole screen: it is a playlist we keep adding to, and a total would be wrong the next time it changed. Animated Introductions to Autism, ADHD, and Neurodiversity, on YouTube, from our own channel.

The rack

Every film on Screen One, in the order it plays, one card each. Press any card to watch just that film.

  1. 01

    An introduction to neurodiversity

    Written and narrated by Kieran Rose, the Autistic Advocate; animated by Josh Knowles Animation

    Neurodiversity as biodiversity for human brains: every flower is unique and every one is still a flower. It says there is nothing right or wrong about either kind of brain, and that the problem is a world built around the majority, which society needs to fix.

    6:17 · on YouTube, via Autism Central Learn

    Neurodiversity, in our glossary →

  2. 02

    An introduction to the Double Empathy problem

    Written and narrated by Kieran Rose; animated by Josh Knowles Animation, for the National Autism Trainer Programme

    Damian Milton’s double empathy problem: when autistic and non-autistic people misunderstand each other, the breakdown runs both ways, and it is not an autistic deficit. Our Autism Pathway pairs it with the monotropism film further along this rack.

    5:37 · on YouTube, via The Autistic Advocate

    The double empathy problem, in our glossary →

  3. 03

    Discovering You’re Autistic

    Made by an international team of neurodivergent creatives, from the stories of people in Thriving Autistic’s Discovery Programme

    Growing up feeling different without knowing why, and what a word for it can do: help you find ways of doing things that work with your brain and body rather than against them, and help you find the people on your wavelength.

    2:30 · on YouTube, via Thriving Autistic

    Our Autism Pathway →

  4. 04

    Lightbulb Moments: Being Autistic

    Made from the stories of people in Thriving Autistic’s Discovery Programme

    The film this picture house is named after. A string of small recognitions — interests that flow like a river and worries that swirl like a whirlpool, a plan that has to be rebuilt when it changes, thinking in constellations rather than lines — from somebody who thought it was just them.

    2:44 · on YouTube, via Thriving Autistic

    Autism, in our glossary →

  5. 05

    An Introduction to Monotropism

    Written and narrated by Kieran Rose; animated by Josh Knowles Animation, for the National Autism Trainer Programme

    Monotropism, the theory Dinah Murray, Wenn Lawson and Mike Lesser built, drawn as a gravity well: autistic attention takes time to build up, locks on, and flows, and being pulled out of it too fast is what gets misread as challenging behaviour.

    4:01 · on YouTube, via The Autistic Advocate

    Monotropism, in our glossary →

  6. 06

    Walk In My Shoes

    Made by the Donaldson Trust with Erin Davidson, from Erin’s experiences at fourteen

    The walk to school, the corridors, a lesson with no filter on it, and being surrounded by people and still alone. It ends on what autism means to the person telling it, and on knowing somebody in order to support them.

    Before you press: Anxiety and panic attacks throughout.

    10:23 · on YouTube, via Donaldson Trust

  7. 07

    I found out I have ADHD.

    JaidenAnimations and team

    A good student who came apart after leaving home, a brother’s diagnosis that raised the question, eight months before booking an assessment, a report thirty-seven pages long, and the first time on medication.

    Before you press: Stimulant medication is a large part of it, and there are two passing jokes about suicide.

    17:53 · on YouTube, via JaidenAnimations

  8. 08

    Times I should’ve realized I was autistic

    illymation and team

    Childhood sensory memories — textures, automatic toilets, itchy jeans — and masking that went wrong, alongside why criteria built around young white boys miss so many girls and people of colour. It stands up for self-identification; the maker has since been diagnosed, and says so under the video.

    Before you press: Brief bullying at school.

    11:26 · on YouTube, via illymation

    Autism, in our glossary →

  9. 09

    I’m Autistic, and Here’s How People React To My Autism

    Cypopps

    Coming out as autistic, and the replies: “I don’t think so”, “I’m so sorry”, “you don’t look autistic” — and the best one, which is “cool” and moving on.

    4:29 · on YouTube, via Cypopps

  10. 10

    Do You Have Autism or ADHD (OR Both)?

    Written by Kelly Soong; revised by Brian Cham and Amanda Silvera; voiced by Amanda Silvera; animated by Tris

    Where autism and ADHD overlap — sensory sensitivity, hyperfocus, freezing under overload — and how both can look quieter in people who mask. For all its title, it says plainly that it is not there to diagnose anybody, and that nobody needs a diagnosis to deserve understanding.

    9:26 · on YouTube, via Psych2Go

  11. 11

    I was the weird kid.

    Cypopps

    A tenth-grade year of being singled out, and a refusal of the idea that the weird kid is bullied for a reason.

    Before you press: Sustained bullying throughout, including unwanted physical contact and photographs taken and shared without consent. The maker puts a content warning on it too.

    6:21 · on YouTube, via Cypopps

  12. 12

    Maria — Autistic Masking

    Directed, written, designed, animated and edited by Arran Bull; Maria voiced by Gemma-Louise Keanne

    A school day that gets louder until Maria cannot hold it together any more, and one teacher who does not ask for an explanation. Four minutes, almost without words.

    Before you press: A meltdown in class, with screaming and sobbing.

    4:02 · on YouTube, via Third Eye Animation

    Before you press: A meltdown at school. Has a sponsor segment.

    9:12 · on YouTube, via owiebrainhurts

    The social model, in our glossary →

  14. 14

    not fitting in

    owiebrainhurts

    Not fitting in, through autism and through gender, and finding that a lot of the friends who stuck turned out to be neurodivergent too.

    8:34 · on YouTube, via owiebrainhurts

  15. 15

    why nobody realised I was autistic

    owiebrainhurts

    Diagnosed at twenty-two after a string of other diagnoses: masking, research built on boys, sensory needs written off as picky eating, and why self-identification is necessary for so many people. It credits the illymation film earlier on this rack.

The job: Where the attention goes

Before the code: The monotropism film on the rack says focused autistic attention is like something in space, with a pull you cannot easily get out of. What?

Hint 1

It is on the card for the monotropism film, and in the film itself.

Hint 2

The card calls it a gravity well.

Last hint

Two words; nothing escapes one.

Give me the answer

The answer is A black hole. In the monotropism film, the focus of autistic attention has more mass than anything else around it, and its gravity drags you in. Coming back out of a gravity well takes time, and being pulled out too fast is what gets mistaken for challenging behaviour.

Taking the answer is a real way through this, not a lesser one. Nothing is watching and nothing is written down.
