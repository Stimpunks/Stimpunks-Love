A picture house on the street

The Lightbulb Picture House

Every screening is a relaxed screening. The house lights stay up.

A lot of us found out who we were from a video. Somebody on a screen described a life that turned out to be ours — the textures, the plans that fell apart, the conversations that went sideways for no reason anybody would name — and something switched on. This picture house is named for that moment, and for the short film on its first screen that calls it a lightbulb moment.

Most of what is showing here was made by the people it is about. Every screen is one of our own playlists, and every film on the first and the third has a card on a rack saying who made it, how long it runs and what is in it, before you press anything.

Every screening is a relaxed screening

  • The house lights stay up. Nobody sits in the dark who did not ask to.
  • The captions are on. Every screen here asks for them before the film starts, and you can switch them off in the player.
  • Nothing plays until you press it, nothing plays before the film you chose, and the volume is yours.
  • Move, stim, rock, pace, flap, hum, sit on the floor, lie across three seats. Nobody shushes anybody.
  • Bring your ear defenders, or borrow the pair hung on the seat in the second row.
  • Come in late and leave early. You can walk out in the middle of a film and walk back in at the end of it, and nobody will ask why.
  • Every film on the rack tells you what is in it before you press play — how long it runs, and anything you might want to know first, from a panic attack to a sponsor segment. Screen Two says how long each film runs and has no rack yet; Screen Three has the campfire rack.

Nothing in this building is blue. Every April, autism charities run by parents and professionals light landmarks up blue, and autistic people have been saying for years what they think of it — one of them, below, better than we could. The bulbs here are warm on purpose, there is no puzzle piece anywhere in the place, and the ornament over the screen is the gold infinity — Autistic UK’s, from Au, the chemical symbol for gold.

Awareness says: Donate to the awesome organizations that have all the pretty puzzle pieces and all the pretty blue lights!

Amy Sequenzia, I Don’t Want Your Awareness, as quoted in our Acceptance entry

The campfire rack

Campfire Learn Together is our Sunday watch-along: we screen something together, take a bodymind break, and talk about it as a community, with nobody at the front. The name is the campfire of David Thornburg’s caves, campfires and watering holes, the place where people gather round a story. Screen Three is everything we have watched there, and this rack has a card for every film on it, each one linking to the post we wrote for that campfire. Press any card to watch just that film, there on the card or up on Screen Three in place of the whole programme.
