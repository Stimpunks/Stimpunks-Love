Faery Yurt

Helen’s candlelit yurt on Stimpunks.World, pitched at the edge of the woods. Lots of books, music, Faeries, candle light and weird thoughts and happenings.....

This is my yurt, pitched at the edge of the woods in the campgrounds of Stimpunks.World, with a stream tracing the trees; follow the fairy lights from the treeline and you will find it. It is always night here, the good kind of night, candles lit along the shelves and fairy lights strung low across the canvas so the whole room glows amber instead of going properly dark.

The sofas are too soft to sit in properly (you sink, and then you stay), the beanbags were claimed by a hamster some time ago, and the ivy worked its way in through the window frame years back and was never asked to leave. The flap of the door is always open. Come in, take your shoes off, and stay as long as you need to.

Like More Realms, the looser, weirder realm beyond Autistic Realms, this is not a place of conclusions or answers. It is a place to wander, to feel, to think sideways, and to deep dive monotropically into other ways of experiencing the world. There is no map, but there are threads, glimmers, and soft, weird spaces to explore…

HOW LOUD DO YOU WANT IT?

Starts wherever your device says. In here MAX lights the fairy lights and turns the record, and makes the stars sparkle behind any portal door you open and the amber and amethyst glow in the Alchemist’s Arch; nothing else in the yurt moves unless you ask it to, below.

Faeries drifting across the page, and ivy and mushrooms growing very slowly round its edges. Each stays still until you press it, whatever the dial says, and pressing again clears it. Nothing flashes, and nothing is remembered: next time both are off.

Pebbles by the door

Penguins bring each other pebbles, and so do we: a small thing somebody found and thought somebody else would like, given on nobody’s clock. There is a log basket here of things Helen has put out for anybody to take, and a bowl for leaving one of your own. It was Helen’s own idea, the day she went to the Solarpunk Hermitage to watch a film; what pebbling is, and whose word it is, is in our glossary and in the Arcade’s pebbling cabinet.

The log basket: pop one on the fire and warm yourself up!

The bowl in the hollow stump: leave one

The bowl needs scripts to read it.

    Anybody who comes to this door can see what is in the bowl, signed on to the CB or not, for seven days.

    Leaving a pebble takes a username on the CB, which you join with an invite: get started or sign on at the Community Center, then come back. Taking one takes nothing.

    • Anybody can see the bowl, and only people signed on to the CB can leave a pebble in it. A handle is whatever somebody typed, so a name in the bowl is not proof of who left it.
    • A pebble goes seven days after it is left, and a full bowl lets its oldest go to make room. The base station can lift one out sooner, and so can we if you ask: what the bowls keep.
    • A link shows its whole address, so what you read is where it goes.
    • Nothing here is counted: not what was taken, not what was left, and not who came to the door. Leaving a pebble is the only footprint there is, and nobody has to leave one.

    The Moon Spoon Pebbling Pot

    My moon stone sits on the windowledge beside my moon spoon, pressed with flowers I gathered in the local woods. Through the window you can watch the video the moon spoon came from.

    Moon Spoon is Vi Hart’s video, on her own Vimeo, 6:30, and the moon spoon is her idea: she says to make your own, or make them for each other, by hand and with love, which is how mine was made. Nothing plays until you press, and nothing of hers is kept here.

    Where To Find Me

    The yurt itself sits on a small campsite off the main street, tucked into the woodlands with a stream tracing the edge of the trees. Follow the fairy lights from the treeline and you will find the door.

    The neighbours on the campgrounds, from the pond, down a pebble path or up the cable car: David Gray-Hammond’s Emergent Future Worlds Meets Mountain Man at the summit, Jess’s Bough House at pitch 05 and Ryan Boren’s Solarpunk Hermitage at pitch 03. Go and visit.

    Out in the wider world, my writing lives in two places. Autistic Realms is where I share grounded, practical support for families, educators, and those navigating Autistic ways of being. More Realms is where things are looser, weirder, more speculative.
