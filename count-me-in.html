This is the stairwell of a public building, and it goes up and never stops. Everybody climbs it together, a number to a step: whoever comes through the door puts the next number, and then somebody else puts the one after. There is a chairlift on the wall, so anybody can take the next step without the number, and a step by the lift is a step. It is after the counting game in our Discord’s Collaborative Nonsense channels.

How the stair works

  • One step at a time, and never two in a row. Once you have taken a step, by number or by lift, the next one is somebody else’s. After theirs you can go again.
  • A number that skips ahead sends the stair back to one. That is the game, and anybody’s number can slip, so the stair never says whose it was: it says where it had got to, and starts again. A number at or below the step we are on is somebody whose page was behind, and it sends nobody anywhere.
  • The lift never sends anybody back. It asks the stair for the next step and takes it. It is there so that nobody has to do sums, type, or trust their eyes on a number to join in, and we are a Disabled people’s organisation, so the stair was never going to have one way up.
  • The stair remembers the highest step it has reached, and that belongs to nobody. It is not anybody’s score and nobody’s fault when a climb goes back to one; it is how high we have been.
  • Nobody is counted. The stair keeps the step it is on, the highest it has reached, where it last went back to one, and a scrambled mark of whoever took the last step so they cannot take the next, which the next step replaces. Nothing says who took any step or how many anybody took. What the stair keeps.
  • Nothing moves, at any setting. The chair is drawn at the landing we are on.

The game

The game is our community’s own, from the Collaborative Nonsense channels in our Discord, where a number goes up one message at a time. The stairwell, the lift and the lamp are drawn for this room. It is set in Allerta Stencil, by Matt McInerney, and Hind, by the Indian Type Foundry, and credited in the liner notes.
