The CB guide

The CB is the street’s radio: a chat channel patterned after citizens band radio, in a panel in the corner of every page. This guide goes through it part by part, top to bottom, with every button named the way the radio names it. To get on, go to the front desk at the Community Center; the house norms are there too, and Privacy says what the CB keeps. Last updated 2026-10-04.

THE SHORT VERSION

  • Open is on, folded is off. Open, with the tab in front of you, the radio listens every four seconds. Folded, or behind another tab, it sends nothing.
  • Ten messages a channel, gone at midnight, Colorado time. An eleventh pushes the oldest off.
  • No alerts. No chime, no badge, no unread count, and the tab’s title never changes.
  • Names, never numbers. A reaction says who pressed it, and Who’s online says who is around and where. Nothing shows a count.
  • Nothing on it moves, at any setting of the dial, and it is the same radio in every room.

Where it is

The radio sits in the bottom right corner of every page. Two places do not have it. The Healing Checkpoint and Stay Frosty each promise something the radio would break, so it waits outside their doors. The private rooms off the Town Hall show it only to the moderators each room is for. The Community Center has its own front desk instead of the bar, and once you sign on there the radio appears in the corner.

Until you sign on, the corner holds a smaller bar. It is not the radio. It knows nothing about the channel and sends nothing to it, and the only thing it remembers is whether you left it open.

Tuning: World or this room

Under the bar, a line says On the channel as and your handle. Then two buttons choose what you hear, and a third shows who is around:

ControlWhat it does
WorldThe channel the whole street shares. A new radio starts here, and tuned to World the radio never says which room you are in.
This roomThe channel of the room you are standing in. Walk into another room and you hear that room’s channel instead. Pages that are not rooms do not have one.
Who’s onlineNot a channel. It opens a list of everybody who has switched on Be seen here, in alphabetical order, with BASE or CLAIMED beside a name the way the channel marks it, and the room each is in; a room’s name takes you there. Your own line says (you), and a line under the list says whether you are on it. While it is open it is redrawn every few seconds, quietly, and it never shows a number.

A line above the messages says Tuned to World or Tuned to the room’s name. Every channel keeps the same rules: ten messages, gone at midnight. A quiet one says Nobody has said anything today.

Be seen here

In a room, Be seen here puts you, by your handle, on Who’s online for anybody signed on, with the room you are in, and shows you and anybody else who has switched it on in the same room to each other. While it is off you are on nobody’s list; you can still open Who’s online, but not see who else is in this room or in its call.

  • Also seen here: lists the others, alphabetically, by handle. Nobody else here is seen. means just that.
  • In this room’s call: lists everybody in the room’s call, so you can see who is there before you join.
  • Names only, and never a count. The lines update quietly, without announcing themselves.
  • Your browser remembers the switch. Folding the radio, leaving the page or putting the tab behind another takes you out of sight.

Reading messages

Each message starts with a line: the sender’s handle, a mark if it has one, and the time it was sent, by your clock. Then come its words, a picture or sticker if it has one, and its reactions.

PartWhat it means or does
The handleA button. Press it to see that person’s pets, if they have chosen to show them. Close or Escape puts it away.
CLAIMEDSent from a claimed username, which only signs on with its owner’s own password. Only the server can put this mark there.
BASESent by one of our moderators. Only the server can put this mark there either. A handle with neither mark is whatever somebody typed.
CopyPuts the message’s words on your own clipboard, as they were typed. It reads Copied for a moment.
ReactOpens the reactions under the message.
A picturePress it to see it larger, with Download and Close. Download under it keeps a copy on your device.
A stickerA drawing of the sender’s pet, with its name. It goes when its message goes.

Links in a message. An address on this street is an ordinary link. An address on another site, written with https://, opens in a new tab and has an arrow and the site’s name after it, like ↗ example.com, so you can see where it goes before you press it. A room written as #the-den shows as #The Den, linked. A time like @34:12 is a button that moves your own film to that place: see watching together.

It never scrolls under you. If you have scrolled up to read, new messages wait at the bottom. The radio follows them only when you were already at the bottom, or when you have just transmitted.

Reactions

React opens a row of quick reactions: a heart, thumbs up, laughing, a party, eyes, sparkles, a penguin for a pebble, a dog, a cat, an otter, a rainbow, a unicorn, the sign of the horns, a hedgehog, a hugging face, people hugging, and a crying face. Under them, Or any emoji takes one emoji of your own from your system’s emoji picker (Control, Command and Space on a Mac, the emoji key on a phone, Windows and . on Windows), and its own React button sends it.

Under the message, each reaction shows the handles of everybody who pressed it, alphabetically, and never a number. Press a reaction there to add yours, or to take yours back.

Markdown

A little Markdown works, and the radio builds it into the page as text, never as code.

There are no headings, images or HTML, and a link’s words are always its address, so [words](an address) stays exactly as you typed it.

Pictures

Press Picture and choose a screenshot or a photo. The radio redraws it in your browser before it goes, which takes off where it was taken and everything else the camera wrote into it. Then:

  • Say what is in it, if you like is a description, up to 280 characters. It is offered, never required, and it is what a screen reader hears.
  • Remove the picture takes it off the message.
  • It goes with your next Transmit, with words or without. It is deleted when its message goes, and one you never send is deleted within the hour.

Pictures on the channel have no public address. Only people signed on can see them, and the same norm as a screenshot applies to passing one on.

The teleporter

Teleport opens a box labelled Teleport to a room, or to @somebody and a list of every room on the street, in walking order. The room you are in says you are here; the others show their #tag.

  • Type to narrow it. It finds rooms whose names start with what you typed, then rooms with it inside a word of their name, so fire finds The Campfire, then rooms whose own description uses the word, with a few of those words shown.
  • ↑ and ↓ move through the list, Enter goes to the room highlighted, and Escape puts the box away.
  • Going is ordinary navigation. Nothing is sent to the channel, and nobody learns where you went.
  • An @ looks for a person. Type @ and the list becomes the people on Who’s online, one line for each room they are in; type some of a handle to narrow it, and Enter goes to the room highlighted. Handles that start with what you typed come first. You are left out of it, and the letters you type are not sent anywhere.

To search the words on every page rather than the rooms’ names, use the search box on the front page or the map.

Watching together

When people watch the same film in a room, each person plays their own copy and pauses when they like. These help everybody find the same place.

ControlWhat it does
An @time in a messageType @ and a time, like @34:12 or @1:02:03. Everybody sees it as a button that moves the film on their own page to that place. It never presses play. If the spot names a film or a room, it only moves that film in that room; anywhere else it says where the spot is. A time without the @ stays words.
Add my spotWrites the @time for you, with the film and the room.
Host this filmEverybody on the channel sees where you are in your film, whether it is playing, and in which room. One person hosts a room at a time. Putting your tab behind another pauses it; folding the radio or leaving the page stops it.
Catch upBeside a host in your room: moves your film to where they are now.
FollowBeside a host in your room: keeps your film in step with theirs, at your own volume. Pausing your own film, or pressing Follow again, stops following, so a break is always fine.
Screen a videoFor moderators. Paste the address of one YouTube video, from youtube.com or youtu.be, and press Put it up: it plays on the page’s first screen, from the time in the address if it has one, in place of what the screen had. It is on your page only. Press Host this film once it plays, and everybody who presses Catch up or Follow beside you gets its play button on their own screen, with how long it runs, waiting for their press. The button under the screen puts back what was there. On a set you tune, like the Solarpunk Hermitage’s television, Looming Rocks’ stage or the Rebellion Rave Room’s big screen, it is tuned on the set instead, which stays off until they press play, and the set’s own controls tune back to its listing. A playlist’s address is refused.

A spot’s room is a link. Following it takes you to the film’s play button, ready to start at that place when you press it. Nobody’s film moves unless they press something themselves.

Calls

Every room has a voice and video call, run by 8x8. Join this room’s call on the radio opens it, and some rooms have a panel of their own that says Join the call as and your handle. A few rooms at Cavendish Coworking also let in people who are not signed on: they type The name you want to be called in the call and press Knock on the door.

The call opens in a window of its own in the bottom left corner.

Profile

Profile opens a tray with two parts.

Your username

  • Claiming. An unclaimed handle can be used by anybody with the community password. Type A password of your own and The same again, then Claim and your handle. From then on only your own password signs on as it, and your messages carry CLAIMED. There is no email.
  • The recovery code is shown once, when you claim. It is the way back in if you forget your password, at the front desk. Keep it, then press I have kept it. A moderator can also give you a reset code.
  • Change my password asks for Your password now, A new password and The same again, then Change it. Every other device signed on as you is signed off.
  • Delete my account asks for your password, then Yes, delete my account or Keep it. It cannot be undone.
  • Five wrong passwords lock a claimed username for fifteen minutes.

Your pets

  • Every pet you have adopted, with its drawing, its name and when you adopted it. Rename or Name them gives one a name; the name it came in under is kept on the list of everybody adopted.
  • Show my pets on the CB lets anybody on the channel press your handle and see them. It needs a claimed username, and it starts off.
  • Forget me deletes everything the street keeps under your handle: your pets and whether they are shown. It asks first, with Yes, forget me or Keep my pets. The animals stay on the list of everybody adopted, with nothing connecting them to you.

What moderators see

Our moderators sign on as the base station, and their messages carry BASE. They also have Take off on every message, which takes it off the air, and Clear the channel, which clears the channel they are tuned to. In a call, the base is the only one who can record or turn on transcription, and the house norms ask them to say so first. If something is wrong, tell the base.

What it sends and keeps

In short: the radio asks this site, and nothing else, for the channel while it is open and in front of you. The channel keeps ten messages, until midnight. No IP address is stored, nothing anybody types is logged, and the only list of who is on is Who’s online, made from Be seen here and gone thirty seconds after somebody stops being seen. Your browser keeps your handle, your pass and where you put the radio. Privacy has the whole of it, and Architecture says how it is built.

HOW LOUD DO YOU WANT IT?

Starts wherever your device says. Nothing moves, flashes, or plays until you say so.
