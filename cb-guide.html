The CB guide

The CB is the street’s radio: a chat channel patterned after citizens band radio, in a panel in the corner of every page. This guide goes through it part by part, top to bottom, with every button named the way the radio names it. To get on, go to the front desk at the Community Center; the house norms are there too, and Privacy says what the CB keeps. Last updated 2026-10-18.

THE SHORT VERSION

  • Open is on, folded is off. Open, with the tab in front of you, the radio listens every four seconds. Folded, or behind another tab, it sends nothing.
  • Fifty messages a channel, each gone 48 hours after it was sent, so people in every timezone can read what was said while they slept. A fifty-first pushes the oldest off. A message from before today says which day, yesterday or the day’s name, beside its time.
  • Notifications only if you ask. The radio has no chime and never a number of anything, and the tab’s title never changes. With a claimed username, what is new to you is marked with a dot, and listed under Unreads. Notifications, under Your radio, can tell a device about mentions, rooms you follow, calls starting and everything on World, even with the site closed.
  • Names, never numbers. A reaction says who pressed it, and Who’s online says who is around and where, and which rooms have somebody in their call. Nothing shows a count.
  • Nothing on it moves when a message arrives, and it is the same radio in every room. The one thing that ever moves is a theme’s drawing, and only at MAX GLITTER or when you let it.

Where it is

The radio sits in the bottom right corner of every page. Two places do not have it. The Healing Checkpoint and Stay Frosty each promise something the radio would break, so it waits outside their doors. The private rooms off the Town Hall show it only to the moderators each room is for. The Community Center has its own front desk instead of the bar, and once you sign on there the radio appears in the corner.

Where you are

At the top of the radio is the name of the room this page is, as the street names it, in the largest words on the radio: The Den, say, or The Street on the front page. It is where you are standing, which is not always the channel you are hearing: tuned to World, you hear the whole street from wherever you are. A page that is not a room says A page that is not a room on the street. Under the name, a line says On the channel as and your handle, then come Be seen here and, in a room when calls are switched on, Join this room’s call.

What is new to you: Unreads

With a claimed username, the radio marks what has been said since you last read it, with a dot and, to a screen reader, the words something new. It is a mark, never a number, and nothing about it sounds, moves or flashes.

  • Where the dots are. On World and This room when the channel you are not tuned to has something new; on Your messages and on a conversation in it when the other person has written since you read it, a first message to you included.
  • Unreads, the part under Who’s around, lists them: World, the rooms you follow, and your conversations. Press one to go there: World tunes to World, a room on this page tunes to it, another room is a link to it, and a conversation opens under Messages.
  • Follow this room: off, beside This room under Channel, puts the room you are in on Unreads whenever something new is said there. Which rooms you follow is kept in this browser only.
  • Follow every room: off, at the foot of Unreads, follows every room you can go into at once, the rooms added later and the forums’ categories you can read included, instead of one at a time (Norah’s ask, 2026-10-21). While it is on, Follow this room is not shown, because this room is followed already. Switched off, Unreads goes back to the rooms you followed one by one, which it leaves as they were. A room that keeps to itself is never on it, and it is kept in this browser only. To be notified about every room as well, Notifications has a Follow every room of its own.
  • What counts as read. A channel when its newest message is on your screen at the foot of the log, with the radio open; a conversation when you have it open. Reading on one device clears the dot on your others, because the street keeps, for your username, when you last read each one, and nothing else. See the privacy page.
  • The radio asks what is new at most every half a minute, and only while it is open, so a dot can take that long to come.

Be seen here

In a room, Be seen here: off, under Where you are, says whether it is on, as the switches under Your radio do; pressed, it says Be seen here: on and puts you, by your handle, on Who’s online for anybody signed on, with the room you are in, and shows you and anybody else who has switched it on in the same room to each other. In a room that keeps to itself it shows you only to people seen there. While it is off you are on nobody’s list; you can still open Who’s online, but not see who else is in this room or in its call.

  • Also seen here: lists the others, alphabetically, by handle. Nobody else here is seen. means just that.
  • In this room’s call: lists everybody in the room’s call, so you can see who is there before you join.
  • Names only, and never a count. The lines update quietly, without announcing themselves.
  • Your browser remembers the switch. Folding the radio, leaving the page or putting the tab behind another takes you out of sight.

With a familiar following you (a chimera you took with you from Chimera Obscura or from Profile) and a claimed username, Bring my familiar: off comes under Be seen here; with no familiar following you, a line there says where to take one from. Pressed, it says Bring my familiar: on, and the people seen in the same room as you see your familiar there, walking about the room, with its name and its sayings, while you are seen; you see theirs, and pressing one says whose it is. It is never on Who’s online. Taking a different chimera with you changes what they see, and sending yours home takes it back in. The privacy page says what is kept.

Who’s around

Who’s online opens two lists. While it is open they are redrawn every few seconds, quietly, and neither shows a number. Beside it, Join World call opens the World call.

  • People: everybody who has switched on Be seen here, in alphabetical order, with BASE or CLAIMED beside a name the way the channel marks it, the avatar a claimed username wears, if it wears one, and the room each is in, with the nameplate they chose, if they chose one, as a strip under the line. A screen reader hears an avatar as what its owner wrote about it, or not at all, and the nameplate not at all. A name opens that person’s profile, the same as pressing a handle on the channel, and a room’s name takes you there. Your own line says (you), and a line under the list says whether you are on it.
  • Calls going now: the World call, while somebody is in it, then every room whose call has somebody in it, in walking order, and a room’s name takes you there. It names the room and nothing else. Who is in a call is shown in its room, to anybody seen there. This list appears once 8x8 is telling us who is in calls.
  • A room that keeps to itself is never on either list, and a room the base keeps is named only to the people it is for.

Reading messages

Each message starts with a line: the sender’s handle, a mark if it has one, and the time it was sent, by your clock. Then come its words, a picture or sticker if it has one, and its reactions. A reply has a line above all that, saying whose message it answers.

At the end of the line are three small buttons, React (a smiley with a plus), Reply (a curved arrow) and More (three dots). They show while your pointer is on the message or the keyboard is anywhere in it, and they are always there on a phone or tablet. Out of sight they are still in their place, so Tab reaches them and a screen reader names them.

Links in a message. An address on this street is an ordinary link. It never scrolls under you. If you have scrolled up to read, new messages wait at the bottom. The radio follows them only when you were already at the bottom, or when you have just transmitted.

Replies

Reply, the curved arrow on a message’s line, opens a line above the message box, Replying to their name and the start of their words, and puts the keyboard in the box. Write and transmit as usual, and your message goes out as an answer to theirs, with that line above it on everybody’s radio. Cancel reply, or Escape in the box, lets the reply go and keeps what you wrote.

A reply keeps nothing of the message it answers but which one it was. The line above it is drawn from what is on the channel now, so if its sender edits it the line changes, and if it is taken off or its 48 hours are up, the line says it answered a message no longer on the channel. If the message you are answering goes while you are writing, the radio says so and what you write goes out on its own. Somebody is told you replied only if they switched on When somebody replies to me.

Reactions

React, the smiley, opens a row of quick reactions: a heart, thumbs up, laughing, a party, eyes, sparkles, a penguin for a pebble, a dog, a cat, an otter, a rainbow, a unicorn, the sign of the horns, a hedgehog, a hugging face, people hugging, a crying face, and a hundred points. Under them, Or any emoji takes one emoji of your own from your system’s emoji picker (Control, Command and Space on a Mac, the emoji key on a phone, Windows and . on Windows), and its own React button sends it.

Under the message, each reaction shows the handles of everybody who pressed it, alphabetically, and never a number. Press a reaction there to add yours, or to take yours back.

Mentioning somebody

Type @ and the start of somebody’s handle, and the radio offers people to mention: everybody who has said something on the channel you are tuned to, everybody those messages mention, and everybody on Who’s online, alphabetically, never you. The first @ asks for Who’s online once. A mention is drawn as the person’s name, which opens their profile, and where you are mentioned your name is filled in, so you can see it. Nothing moves or lights up when one arrives. If they switched on When somebody @mentions me, they are told, as they were before.

Pictures

Press Picture and choose a screenshot or a photo, or paste one into the message box. The radio redraws it in your browser before it goes, which takes off where it was taken and everything else the camera wrote into it. It shows you the picture first, above the box, as it will be sent, with the name of the file you picked, and nothing has gone anywhere yet. Then:

  • Say what is in it, if you like is a description, up to 280 characters. It is offered, never required, and it is what a screen reader hears.
  • Remove the picture takes it off the message.
  • It goes with your next Transmit, with words or without. It is deleted when its message goes, and one you never send is deleted within the hour.

Pictures on the channel have no public address. Only people signed on can see them, and the same norm as a screenshot applies to passing one on.

The teleporter

Teleport opens a box labelled Teleport to a room, or to @somebody and a list of every room on the street, in walking order. The room you are in says you are here; the others show their #tag.

  • Type to narrow it. It finds rooms whose names start with what you typed, then rooms with it inside a word of their name, so fire finds The Campfire, then rooms whose own description uses the word, with a few of those words shown.
  • ↑ and ↓ move through the list, Enter goes to the room highlighted, and Escape puts the box away.
  • Going is ordinary navigation. Nothing is sent to the channel, and nobody learns where you went.
  • An @ looks for a person. Type @ and the list becomes the people on Who’s online, one line for each room they are in; type some of a handle to narrow it, and Enter goes to the room highlighted. Handles that start with what you typed come first. You are left out of it, and the letters you type are not sent anywhere.

To search the words on every page rather than the rooms’ names, use the search box on the front page or the map.

What moderators see

Our moderators sign on as the base station, and their messages carry BASE. They cannot read anybody’s direct messages, except one somebody reports to them. They also have Take off, under More, on every message on a channel, not only their own, which takes it off the air, though like everybody they can Edit only their own, and Clear the channel, which clears the channel they are tuned to. In a call, the base is the only one who can record or turn on transcription, and the house norms ask them to say so first. If something is wrong, tell the base.

What it sends and keeps

In short: the radio asks this site, and nothing else, for the channel while it is open and in front of you. The channel keeps fifty messages, each for 48 hours. No IP address is stored, nothing anybody types is logged, and the only list of who is on is Who’s online, made from Be seen here and gone thirty seconds after somebody stops being seen. Your browser keeps your handle, your pass and where you put the radio. Privacy has the whole of it, and Architecture says how it is built.
