Community Center

CB sign-on at the front desk

The street’s community center, a quiet room in sage lime plaster. One big window faces north onto a hillside, so the light is soft and the same all day and no sun lands anywhere, and a deep seat is built into the bay under it, with a cushion, a folded throw and a plant on the sill; there is a radio on the front desk. This is where you get started in our community: how to join the CB with an invite and a username of your own, just below. The radio is the CB, a chat channel patterned after citizens band radio, and this is the only place it is switched on. Sign on with your username and your password and it rides along in the corner of your screen, on every page of the street, until you sign off. Our community talks on the CB and in the forums, which are the CB’s too, for whatever needs longer than a day. Every room on the street, and who is in each, is on the World room’s web, past the Garden’s spider. On the wall by the desk is the community service board: every way there is to give something to a room on this street, and how.

Getting started in the community

  1. Ask to join. Go to the Stimpunks Community page and request to join, with the join form. It takes a few minutes, and you share only what you are comfortable sharing. As part of that, you are given an invite to Stimpunks.World: a link, or a code. A moderator can give you one too.
  2. Open the invite’s link. It opens the joining page with the code filled in. If you were given a code on its own, type it in there.
  3. Choose your username and a password of your own, eight characters or more, and an email address for password recovery if you want one. Then press Join.
  4. Keep the recovery code the joining page shows you then. It is shown once, and it is the way back in if you forget your password: write it down, or keep it where you keep your passwords.

Your username is yours

From the moment you join, your username signs on with your own password and nothing else, so nobody else can be you. Next time, sign on at the front desk, below, or with Get on the CB on the bar in the bottom right corner of any page. Account, on the radio in the part called You, is where you change your password, add, change or take off your email address, or delete your username.

On the CB with the old starter password? It was retired on 21 October 2026, when every username on the CB came to be claimed. The radio asks you to claim the username you are on as, with a password of your own, before anything else: press Claim and your username, and keep the recovery code it shows you. Your pets, avatars and everything else kept under the username stay with it. Signed off already? Ask a moderator for an invite.

What you can do now

Now that you are on, you can chat on the CB, post to our forums, fill in your profile, and much more. Here are some quests you can go on:

  • Post an introduction in the forums’ Introductions, saying as much or as little as you like.
  • Adopt a pet from Rescue A Cat, Rescue A Dog or Rescue Small Animals. Your pets live in Profile on your radio.
  • Collect badges by doing jobs from the Adventurer’s Guild: jobs that go together make a badge.
  • Collect patches lying about the street and on some shop counters, and sew them onto your battle jacket. Where the patches are, in Profile, lists them.
  • Make your profile your own with a profile card, avatars from the Bodymind Booth, and a theme for your radio.
  • Scroll down this page for the CB’s house norms and the community service board, with everything else there is to do and give here.

How the radio works

Every button and switch on the radio, named the way the radio names it, is in the CB guide. These are the things worth knowing first.

  • Open is on and closed is off. While the radio is open and the tab is in front of you, it listens to the channel every few seconds. Fold it away, or look at another tab, and it stops listening altogether.
  • Notifications only if you ask for them. The radio has no chimes, no badges and no unread count, and nothing is saved up for when you come back. Notifications, under Your radio, can switch a device on to be told when somebody @mentions you, when there is a message in a room you follow, when a call starts, or about everything on World, even with the site closed. Each is off until you switch it on, and Privacy says what that keeps.
  • A picture can go with a message. Press Picture beside Transmit, pick a screenshot or a photo, and say what is in it if you like. The radio redraws it before it goes, which takes off where it was taken and anything else the camera wrote into it, and it goes when its message goes. Press a picture to see it larger, and Download keeps a copy on your own device; the same norm as a screenshot applies to passing it on.
  • A message can be long. Up to 2,000 characters, and it keeps its lines: Enter sends and Shift and Enter starts a new line. The box grows as you type, and you can drag its corner to make it taller. A little Markdown works: **bold**, *italic*, `code`, three backticks on a line of their own round a block of code, > at the start of a line for a quote, and - or 1. for a list. A link’s words are always its address, so [words](an address) stays as you typed it. Copy on a message puts its words, as they were typed, on your own clipboard.
  • Be seen here, if you like. In any room, Be seen here on the radio shows you, by your handle, to anybody else signed on who has switched it on in that room, and shows them to you, with the names of everybody in that room’s call, so you can see who is there before you join. Names only, and no count. It also puts you on Who’s online, beside World and This room, where anybody signed on can see who is around and which room they are in, and go there; typing @ in Teleport finds somebody the same way. While it is off you are on nobody’s list. Your browser remembers it until you switch it off.
  • A profile card, if you claim your username. Press somebody’s handle, on the channel or on Who’s online, and their profile opens: their card, and their avatars, pets, badges and battle jacket as they have chosen to show them. Edit my profile card, in Profile, fills in yours: a theme and a banner, a decoration round the avatar you wear, a frame round your profile and an effect over it, a face for your name, a nameplate on Who’s online, your pronouns, a few words about yourself, links elsewhere and your favourite room. Whatever you fill in is shown to anybody signed on, and nothing on it says when you joined. Privacy has the whole of it.
  • From an icon to the whole window. − and + on the bar take the radio down and up through an icon of a CB set, which is still listening and shows nothing else; Small, which is the bar and the newest message and still listening; Normal; Large, which is wider and taller, for long messages and pictures; and Full, the whole window, for when you want only the CB. Press the icon to open the radio again.
  • A hundred messages, for two weeks, unless a channel says otherwise. The latest hundred are all there is on a channel; once it is full, the newest pushes the oldest off, and every message is deleted two weeks after it was sent, unless our administrators have set that channel otherwise, which the radio says under In this channel, so people in every timezone get to read it. Take off on a message you sent takes it off the air sooner, for everybody, with its picture and its reactions. It cannot be put back. Edit changes the words of a message you sent; it then says edited, and the words it had before are not kept.
  • The forums are the CB’s too, for what needs longer than two weeks. The forums keep topics for weeks or months, in categories, and only a claimed username can read or write in them. Take to the forums, under a message’s three dots, starts a topic with messages from the channel, copied exactly as they are on the channel. What they keep.
  • The same sign-on writes on the chalkboard. Plural Mural has a chalkboard at the foot of its wall, Helen Edgar’s idea, and only people signed on here can chalk a note on it. It is not the channel: anybody walking past can read it, signed on or not, and a note stays up for seven days, where a message on the channel stays two weeks.
  • It sits in the bottom right corner. Drag it by its bar to put it somewhere else, or press Move, the six dots, and use the arrow keys; Home puts it back in the corner. It remembers where you left it.
  • It reads aloud if you want it to. With the radio open, a screen reader hears new messages as they come in, and the switch New messages to a screen reader turns that off. Speak new messages aloud is for everybody else: switch it on and the radio reads each new message out, handle first, in your own device’s voice, with a burst of squelch as the station keys up and the tail as it lets go, like a voice coming over the air. At the Gentle setting you get the words without the static. It is off until you turn it on, it never reads what was already on the channel when you tuned in, and folding the radio away stops it. A screen reader user with both switches on will hear everything twice.
  • There is one room it stays out of. The Healing Checkpoint, room 429, promises that it writes nothing down about you, and a radio that listens to a server and remembers where you put it would make that untrue. So the radio waits outside that door and is there again when you come out.

House norms

  1. Our covenant is the floor. Everything in the Stimpunks Covenant applies on the channel, as it does in every Stimpunks space.
  2. A reaction names who pressed it. React on a message puts a heart, an otter, a penguin or any other emoji under it with your handle beside it, and pressing it again takes yours back. Nobody is shown a count, only names, so a reaction is a person saying something small, not a score.
  3. A username is somebody’s, and only theirs. Every username on the CB is claimed, and signs on only with its owner’s own password, so a message under a name came from whoever holds that name’s password. It says nothing more about who they are. BASE is the one mark, and it means a moderator: only our moderators can send it, each under their own username off the moderators’ list. Nobody can type it or tick it; only the server puts it there.
  4. World, or this room. The radio hears one channel at a time. World is the one the whole street shares. This room is the room you are standing in, heard by whoever else is tuned to it there, and walking into another room tuned that way hears that room’s channel instead. Every channel keeps a hundred messages, each for two weeks, unless our administrators set it otherwise, and the radio says which.
  5. Every room has a call. Join this room’s call opens voice and video for the room you are in, for people signed on to the CB. The rooms off the Town Hall for our directors, board and moderators are the base’s: anybody can walk in, and their channel and call are for the base alone. Camera and microphone start off, and camera off and microphone off is a fine way to be in a call. The base station moderates, and is the only one who can record a call or turn on transcription; before doing either, say so in the call, and a recording or transcript is shared only if everybody in it has said yes, the same as a screenshot of the channel. Anybody can share a YouTube video in the call for everybody to watch together; whoever shares it plays and pauses it for everybody, and while it plays with its sound on, Jitsi mutes your microphone, so unmute to talk over it. For watching at your own pace instead, use the room’s own players and the CB’s spots. Anybody in a call can share a screenshot or a photo there with Share file; it goes to 8x8, not to us, so share nothing with somebody else in it unless they have said yes, the same as a screenshot of the channel.
  6. It is a room, not a private line. Everybody on the CB can read the channel. Say what you would say in a room with people in it.
  7. What is said here stays here. That goes for pictures too. The channel forgets after two weeks; do not carry it off in a screenshot, a repost or a forum topic unless everybody in it has said you can. That is the covenant’s own privacy norm, on the one channel of ours built to forget. If somebody takes what you said to the forums anyway, Take my message out there takes it back.
  8. Nobody has to answer. A notification is the reader’s own choice, never a summons, and an @mention is not a deadline: nobody is waiting on you. A message into a quiet channel is fine, a quiet channel is fine, and you can leave without saying goodbye.
  9. Plain words are fine. Say ten-four and breaker-breaker if you like them. Nobody needs to know the codes.
  10. A room on the street is a link. Type # and the start of its name, like #den, pick it from the list, and everybody on the channel sees it as #The Den, linked. Arrow keys move through the list, Enter or Tab picks, and Escape leaves your # alone. A room’s full address works too, like stimpunks.world/the-den.html.
  11. An address on another site is a link that says it is leaving. Write it out with https:// and it opens in a new tab, with the site it goes to after it, like ↗ example.com. Check that name before you follow it: a handle proves nothing about who sent it. A link can have words of its own, [words](an address), and one to another site still has its site after the words, so check that name too.
  12. A place in a film is a button. Watching something together, type @ and the time, like @34:12 or @1:02:03, or press Add my spot to put in where your film has got to, which film it is and which room you are in. Everybody sees it as a button that moves the film on their own page to that place, and the room as a link to go there. Following that link takes you to the film’s play button, ready to start at that place when you press it. If a spot names a room or a film, it only moves that film in that room, and anywhere else it tells you where that spot is instead. It does not press play, so anybody taking a break can catch up when they come back. A time without the @, like 7:30, stays words.
  13. Hosting is a beacon, not a remote. Press Host this film while you watch something, and everybody on the channel sees where you are in it and in which room, with a Catch up button if they are in the same room, and a Follow switch that keeps their film in step with yours, at their own volume. Nobody’s film moves unless they press one of those themselves, and pausing your own film stops following, so taking a break and coming back is always fine. One person hosts a room at a time.
  14. Keep the password inside the community. It only changes if there is a problem, and everybody is signed off when it does.
  15. If something is wrong, tell the base. Say so on the channel, or reach us privately. The base station can take any message off the air, as you can take off your own. If it keeps happening, the password changes, which signs everybody off at once, and that is the only other lever there is.

The covenant

The Stimpunks Covenant is where our community norms live, and it is longer and better than anything this room could say about them. It starts from the Contributor Covenant, version 2.1, which every Stimpunk agrees to abide by, and adds what we have learned since: no proselytising, prosocial ways of settling conflict, and how to repair harm when it happens. Read it on stimpunks.org, where it is kept up to date, rather than here, where it would not be.

Whose this is

The CB was Ryan’s idea: a simple chat patterned after citizens band radio, one password shared with the community, no alerts until the community asked for notifications, a short history cleared daily until people in other timezones needed two days of it, and a radio that floats on the screen once you are on. It is the one thing on this street that talks to a server, and the only thing that sends anything anywhere, and the privacy page says in plain words what that server keeps.

The room’s look answers a request from our community for something softer and quieter, and leans on 14 Patterns of Biophilic Design, by William Browning, Catherine Ryan and Joseph Clancy for Terrapin Bright Green (2014). The type is Varela Round, by Joe Prince, for the room’s name and headings, and Radio Canada, by Charles Daoud, Coppers and Brasses, Alexandre Saumier Demers and Jacques Le Bailly, for everything that is read, both under the SIL Open Font License and read off their own records rather than typed. The window, the seat and the radio on the desk are ours. Everything is credited in the liner notes.
