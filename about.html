What’s this all about?

Stimpunks.World is a website built as a street. Every shopfront is a door, and behind every door is a room that is its own world, with its own light, its own lettering and its own colours.

It is made by Stimpunks Foundation, which builds accessible infrastructure and public tools for Disabled and neurodivergent people. This is the one of our sites that gets to be loud, and it was built to show that loud and accessible are not opposites.

The short version

A film, thirty seconds long and silent. It plays only when you press play, and everything it shows is written out underneath.

What the film says and shows

  1. 0:00 “As neurodivergent and Disabled people, we’re often told we live in our own worlds.” Then, in yellow capitals: “We said why not.” Small lights come on along the bottom of the screen, one at a time.
  2. 0:05 “So we built a street of them.” The stimpunks.world sign lands, and a row of dark shopfronts rises out of the lights onto a pavement.
  3. 0:09 “No two rooms alike. Each brings its own light, its own type and its own colours.” Walking past the shopfronts, each door lights up in its own colours with its own name in its own lettering: Pink Pony Club, Club Chronic, The Arcade, The Mopery, The Jungle Room, The Adventurer’s Guild, Laughingstock and Samefood Cafe.
  4. 0:16 “How loud it gets is yours to hold.” The street’s dial, asking how loud you want it, turns from Gentle to Regular to MAX GLITTER. At Gentle the doors stand still, at Regular they lift a little, and at MAX GLITTER they sway and sparkles rise slowly past them. “Gentle takes away the wobble, never the words.”
  5. 0:22 “Difference is the layout, not the exception. The street rearranges itself for whoever walks down it.” The doors lift and swap places.
  6. 0:26 The stimpunks.world sign again, over the lit doors, and: “Nobody has to earn the right to walk in.”

What do I do here?

  • Walk in anywhere. There is no right order, nothing to finish and nothing keeping score. The Map shows the whole street, and the foot of every page has a Where am I? that opens it at the page you are on.
  • Turn the dial. Every page carries it, and the one at the foot of this page works too. Gentle takes away the wobble, never the words, and MAX GLITTER turns everything up.
  • Press play only if you want to. Nothing from anybody else plays or loads until you press it, and every play button says first how long it runs, or that it runs until you stop it.
  • Rest. Some rooms are for nothing else, like the Healing Checkpoint and Stay Frosty. Rest is owed here, not earned.
  • Find people. The Community Center is where to start if you want company.

Why a street?

Because a street can hold rooms that have nothing in common. Every other site we make is careful and consistent; this one has no house style on purpose, because here difference is the layout rather than the exception. The rooms share a dial and a set of promises, and the Mission writes each promise down beside the page that keeps it.

The idea of a street that rearranges itself for whoever turns up is not ours. It is Danny the Street’s, a character in DC Comics’ Doom Patrol, created in 1990 by Grant Morrison and Richard Case.

The street is not mutual aid. It pays no rent and gives no grant. The foundation does those things, at stimpunks.org.

Who made the film, and this page

Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, wrote the film as code in Claude Motion and drafted this page, both to Ryan Boren’s brief of 2026-10-15, and the film was exported to video from Claude Motion. Like every drawing on the street, it is shapes and type rather than a generated picture, and How we use AI says more. The doors in it are the front page’s own, each lettered in the typeface its room uses, from this site’s own font files; every typeface and who drew it is in the liner notes.
