Turning 04, and you cannot hear it from the road

Dance, Punks

A silent disco on the old airstrip. Three channels, one crate, a headset each, and the volume in your own hand.

The quietest thing on this road is also the biggest crowd on it. Out past the last streetlight there is an airstrip nobody lands on any more, long and flat and wide enough for everybody, and on it a great many people are dancing to three different things at once without a sound reaching the verge. From the road you see a field of small coloured lights bobbing about in the dark. That is the whole party, and it is lit by the headsets and nothing else.

The loudest room you can turn down

A silent disco hands everybody their own music, on their own headset, at their own volume. The dancefloor is still a crowd — you can see everybody, move with everybody, be right in the middle of it — but the sound only goes where somebody asked for it. Take the headset off and the runway is quiet: tarmac, somebody laughing, the wind. Nobody on this airstrip can make anybody else’s night louder.

That is the dial this whole street runs on, built as a venue. Everywhere else here, how loud it gets is something the visitor holds rather than a property of the page. Out here it is something the dancer holds rather than a property of the room. A loud room is an access barrier, as Club Chronic says about itself on the street, and a silent disco is one of the ways a crowd gets round that without anybody having to leave it.

Some of us dance to the same song forty times in a row — stim listening, one song or one list over and over, which is what our playlists are for. And some of us move because joy has to go somewhere. Our Autistic Joy entry quotes Authentically Emily on what it can feel like, and one line on that list is needing to move and stim to release the feeling. A runway is a lot of room to release it in.

Put a headset on

Three channels, and they are one crate started in three places. The crate is Dance, Punks, a playlist Stimpunks keeps on YouTube. Each channel opens on a different song in it and plays on in order from there. A real silent disco has a DJ on every channel; this one has a starting point on every channel, and the room would rather say so than borrow a word for something it is not doing. It is not shuffle either. The player has no such button.

Nothing is fetched from YouTube until you put the headset on. While it is off, choosing a channel only changes the colour of the band and what the headset says it will open on — you can try all three without a single request going to YouTube. Once it is on, changing channel changes the music, because by then you have asked for a headset. No channel says how long it runs, because the crate is a list we keep adding to and today’s total would be wrong next week. It runs until you take the headset off, and the volume is on the player, where nobody else can reach it.

The rules on the runway

  • Nothing strobes, flashes or pulses, at any dial setting. No lasers, no smoke, no mirrorball swinging a beam across anybody’s eyes. The headsets glow steadily or not at all. A dancefloor is the room on this road most likely to grow a light show, and a light that can put somebody on the floor has no business at a party anybody is meant to enjoy — the punk club on the street settled that first.
  • The channels are told apart by more than their colour. Every headset says its number and its name, and the three colours are at least 1.5 to 1 apart in brightness from each other, so they stay three different headsets in greyscale and to anybody who does not see those colours as different. That bar is ours rather than a standard’s, and make-dance-punks.py refuses a channel under it.
  • Take the headset off whenever you like. The runway is quiet underneath the music, and standing in a silent crowd watching everybody dance is a perfectly good way to be at this party.
  • Nobody has to dance. Sit on the tarmac, lie on it and look up, walk the centre line out to the far end and back. There are no seats because there is nowhere you are supposed to be.
  • Nothing is counted. Not songs heard, not time on the floor, not which channel is most popular. Nobody is keeping a list of who danced.
  • Come on your own. Most of the crowd did. That is what a crowd on different channels looks like from the road.

Whose this all is

The crate is ours and the music is not. Dance, Punks is a playlist Stimpunks Foundation keeps on YouTube, read off its own page on 2026-09-23. Every song on it belongs to the people who made it and is on somebody else’s channel. Nothing is hosted by us, and none of those artists, their labels or the channels carrying them is affiliated with Stimpunks or endorses us. The three opening songs, who made them and whose channel each is on, are in the liner notes.

The crate takes suggestions. If a song gets you moving, send it through the Stimpunks contact page or in our community. It goes in as a link to wherever it already lives, because nothing musical is hosted here, and it is checked first to see that it plays and can be shown on this page. No lyrics go on this page either way.

The airstrip is invented. Silent discos are not, and neither are the headsets that glow the colour of the channel they are on.
